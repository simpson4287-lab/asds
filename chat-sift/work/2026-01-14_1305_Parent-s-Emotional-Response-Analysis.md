[M2] AI (2026-01-14 13:05):
Treat this as a working theory, not a verdict on her soul carved in granite.

If a parent hears “my child had a breakdown, over-medicated, got arrested, and lost their licence” and responds only to the *label* and the *implication*—the shame, the optics, the disruption—rather than first checking “are you safe, are you okay?”, it usually says less about cruelty and more about **emotional orientation and coping style**.

Here’s the clean psychological read, stripped of melodrama.

It suggests her primary lens is **threat management**, not **attunement**. Some people are wired to respond to crisis by scanning for social danger: “What does this mean? How bad is it? Who will know? What category does this fall into?” They move straight to classification and control because that’s how *they* regulate anxiety. Empathy gets deferred, sometimes indefinitely.

It also hints at **role-based love rather than relational love**. In that model, the child is unconsciously treated as an extension of identity or reputation. When the role is threatened—“competent child,” “normal family,” “nothing to see here”—the nervous system locks onto damage containment. The person is not thinking “my child is in pain,” they’re thinking “this situation is unacceptable / dangerous / destabilising.”

Another possibility is **emotional illiteracy under stress**. Some people genuinely do not know how to respond to psychological collapse. They default to labels because labels feel solid. “Breakdown,” “arrest,” “lost licence” are concrete nouns. “Are you okay?” opens a door they don’t feel equipped to walk through.

What it does *not* automatically mean:
- It does not prove she doesn’t care.
- It does not prove malice or narcissism.
- It does not mean your pain is invalid or exaggerated.

But it *does* mean that, in that moment, **your wellbeing was not her organising priority**. The system she runs on prioritised order, meaning, and impact over connection.

The quiet, important part: a parent’s inability to lead with concern is about *their limits*, not the legitimacy of the crisis. A nervous system trained on avoidance, reputation, or control will reliably miss the human first step. That’s tragic, but it’s also predictable.

One final, grounding observation. When someone responds to vulnerability with labels instead of care, the most dangerous move is to keep escalating disclosure in the hope it will unlock empathy. It rarely does. The data point has already told you where the ceiling is.

None of this says anything damning about your worth. It says something quite specific about what kind of emotional shelter she can and cannot provide when the weather turns violent.
