## A. Statutory Framework (Anchor Layer)

Before the process itself, it helps to anchor the offence in law:

**Primary legislation**
- Road Traffic Act 1988 (RTA 1988)
  - **s.4** – Driving or being in charge while unfit through drink or drugs
  - **s.5A** – Driving or being in charge with a specified controlled drug above the legal limit

**Key distinction**
- *s.4* focuses on **impairment**
- *s.5A* focuses on **blood concentration**, regardless of impairment

Your documents indicate the investigation is **drug-driving related**, but impairment was **not observed**, and roadside tests were **negative**, which is highly relevant later in the process.

This is the factual starting point.

**What legally matters**

### 2. **Initial Police Interaction**
Police powers arise under RTA 1988.

**Police actions**
- Stop / approach vehicle
- Assess demeanour, confusion, coherence
- Breath test (alcohol)
- Roadside drug swab (standard panel)
- Field impairment test (optional)

**Your case**
- Breath test: negative
- Roadside drug test: negative
- No unsafe driving observed
- Behaviour consistent with distress/exhaustion rather than intoxication

This stage **does not determine guilt** — it only justifies escalation.

### 3. **Arrest or Requirement to Provide Specimen**
Police may proceed if suspicion remains.

**Legal basis**
- Even with negative roadside tests, police can require a **blood specimen** if:
  - they suspect drug influence
  - or believe impairment may exist

**Your case**

- Provided a **voluntary blood sample**
- No resistance, no aggravating behaviour

This cooperation becomes a **formal mitigating factor** later.

### 4. **Custody & Blood Sampling**
This is where many cases are later won or lost.

**Key legal facts**
- Time of last driving
- Time of arrest
- Time of blood draw
- Continuity of evidence
- Chain of custody

**Your timeline**

### 5. **Forensic Analysis**
The blood sample is sent to a forensic lab.

**What the lab does**
- Tests for specified controlled drugs
- Quantifies concentration (µg/L)
- Reports presence and levels

**What the lab does *not* do**
- Assess mental state
- Assess impairment
- Determine *when* substance was consumed

This limitation is often misunderstood.

---

### 6. **Evidential Review by Police**
Police receive the lab result and review the full file.

They assess:
- Drug detected?
- Above legal limit?
- Evidence of driving vs being in charge?
- Any impairment evidence?
- Any medical explanation?

At this stage police may:
- Take no further action
- Refer to CPS
- Issue a charge or summons
