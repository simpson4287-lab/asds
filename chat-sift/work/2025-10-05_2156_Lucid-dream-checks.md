---

### **2. Main Modes of Transmission**
#### **a. Flea Bites (Bubonic Plague)**
- Fleas feed on infected rodents, then bite humans, transmitting the bacteria.  
- Causes **swollen lymph nodes (buboes), fever, and chills**.  

#### **b. Airborne Droplets (Pneumonic Plague)**
- Inhalation of bacteria from a person or animal with **pneumonic plague**.  
- Highly contagious and can spread **person-to-person**.  
- Causes **lung infection, coughing, and respiratory failure**.  

#### **c. Direct Contact**
- Handling **infected animals or corpses** could spread the plague through **cuts or scratches**.  

---

### **3. Factors That Helped the Spread**
- **Trade and travel:** Ships, caravans, and trade routes carried infected rats and fleas across Europe, Asia, and North Africa.  
- **Urban conditions:** Overcrowded, unsanitary towns allowed rats and fleas to thrive.  
- **Poor hygiene:** Limited understanding of disease control; humans lived closely with rodents.  
- **Climate and environment:** Mild winters and wet summers sometimes increased rodent populations, boosting flea-borne transmission.  

---

### **4. Types of Plague**
- **Bubonic:** Most common; spread by fleas.  
- **Pneumonic:** Spread via cough; highly lethal.  
- **Septicemic:** Infection of the blood; could develop from bubonic or pneumonic forms.  

---

### **5. Historical Impact**
- The Black Death (1347–1351) killed **25–50% of Europe’s population**.  
- Spread rapidly along **trade routes**, ports, and cities, moving from Asia → Middle East → Europe.
