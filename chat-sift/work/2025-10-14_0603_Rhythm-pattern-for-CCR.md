---

### 🎸 **Core Strumming Pattern (Acoustic Rhythm)**

**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Tempo:** ~108 BPM  
**Feel:** Country swing / “train-beat” feel — steady eighth-note pulse with light shuffle swing.

---

### 🔹 **Basic Pattern (Down–Up Notation)**  
```
↓ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &
```

This is a light “boom-chicka-boom-chicka” pattern:
- **Beat 1:** Strong downstroke on the bass note (root of the chord)  
- **Beat 2:** Down–Up on the higher strings  
- **Beat 3:** Bass note again (lighter)  
- **Beat 4:** Down–Up on higher strings  

You can also think of it as:

> **BASS–chuck–BASS–chuck**  
> (like a train rhythm)

---

### 🔹 **Variant with Country Bounce (Optional Shuffle Feel)**  
If you want the authentic CCR groove, slightly delay the “&” strums (a swung 8th feel):

```
↓   ↓↑   ↓   ↑
1   a 2   a 3   a 4   a
```
This gives it that rolling, relaxed “country shuffle.”

---

### 🔹 **Chord Progression (Verse Example)**
In **G major**, the verse progression goes roughly:
```
G   | Em | C   | G   |
G   | Em | C   | D   |
```

---

### 🔹 **Tip**
John Fogerty uses **alternating bass picking** — so try:
- On **G**: alternate low E (6th string) → D (4th string)  
- On **C**: alternate A (5th string) → D (4th string)  
This makes the rhythm more “train-like.”

### 🎵 **Beat Framework (Straight vs. Swing Feel)**  

The song’s in **4/4**, and the **main pulse** is eighth-notes (with a light swing).  
So you count it like:  
> **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**  

- **“1, 2, 3, 4”** = **downbeats** (where your hand goes **↓**)  
- **“&”** = **upbeats** (where your hand goes **↑**)  

Even when you *don’t* hit the strings, your hand should keep that motion going — this is what keeps the groove consistent.

---

### 🎸 **Standard CCR Country Strum (Train Feel)**

Typical bar of **G**:

```
↓     ↓↑    ↓     ↑
1  &  2 &  3  &  4 &
```

You’re striking the **bass note** on beats **1** and **3**,  
and doing a **down-up (↑)** “chuck” on the **&** of 2 and 4.

So if you verbalise it:

> **“Boom-chuck-boom-chuck”**  
> (Bass-strum-Bass-strum)

This gives that rolling **country-folk train rhythm** — consistent up-down motion, accenting 1 & 3 on bass, 2 & 4 on chord strums.
