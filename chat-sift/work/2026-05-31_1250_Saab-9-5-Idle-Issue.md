My Saab 95 aero hot car 2007 year is idleing closer to the 1000. Is this a problem?

[M2] AI (2026-05-31 12:50):
Probably **not a serious problem on its own**, but it depends on the conditions.

For a **2007 Saab 9-5 Aero HOT**, a normal warm idle is usually around **750–850 rpm**. Sitting **near 1000 rpm** can be normal briefly, especially when:

- the engine is cold
- air conditioning or demister is on
- alternator load is high
- battery is low/charging
- the ECU is doing idle control correction
- it has only just been started

Where it becomes worth checking is if it is **fully warm**, all accessories are off, and it still idles steadily around **950–1050 rpm** every time.

Common causes on the Saab 9-5 would be:

1. **Vacuum leak / split hose**  
   Very common on older turbo Saabs. A small air leak can raise idle.

2. **Throttle body dirty or adaptation issue**  
   If the throttle plate is dirty, the ECU may hold the idle higher.

3. **PCV/breather system leak**  
   The 9-5 has a known PCV/breather setup. Perished pipes can cause odd idle behaviour.

4. **MAF sensor or intake leak after the MAF**  
   Can cause idle trim issues.

5. **Coolant temperature sensor reading cold**  
   ECU thinks the engine is colder than it is, so it holds a higher idle.

6. **AC/climate load**  
   Try turning climate control fully off and see if the idle drops.

The main question is: **does it idle at 1000 rpm when fully warmed up after a 10–15 minute drive, with AC/demist off?**

If yes, I’d start with a simple visual check of all vacuum and breather hoses around the throttle body/intake manifold, then scan it with an OBD reader for fault codes and fuel trims. If it drives normally, no warning lights, no hunting/surging, and it is only just under/around 1000 rpm, it is not an immediate panic. But I would not ignore it if it is consistently high when hot.
