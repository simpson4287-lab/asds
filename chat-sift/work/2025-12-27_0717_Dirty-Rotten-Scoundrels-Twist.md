Sudden, intense itching in dogs during mid-winter is common and is usually driven by **environmental changes rather than parasites**, although several causes can overlap. The most likely explanations are below, ordered by probability.

---

## Most common causes in winter

### 1. **Dry indoor air and skin dehydration**
- Central heating significantly reduces humidity.
- Dogs’ skin dries out, weakening the skin barrier and triggering itchiness.
- Often worse on flanks, belly, legs, and behind the ears.
- Multiple dogs itching at the same time strongly supports this cause.

**Clues**
- Dandruff or fine white flakes  
- Increased scratching without obvious redness  
- Worse at night or indoors  

---

### 2. **Seasonal/environmental allergies (winter-specific)**
Winter allergies are often **indoor allergens**, not pollen:
- Dust mites (increase when heating is on)
- Mould spores (especially in damp UK winters)
- Cleaning products, air fresheners, scented candles
- New laundry detergents used on dog bedding

**Clues**
- Licking paws, rubbing face
- Redness around eyes, ears, or groin
- Symptoms worsen indoors

---

### 3. **Diet-related sensitivity (often coincidental timing)**
- Winter diet changes (different treats, richer food, more table scraps)
- Reduced omega-3 intake if food formulation changed
- Food intolerance can emerge suddenly, even after years

**Clues**
- Soft stools, gas, or ear irritation alongside itching
- Year-round itch that suddenly escalates

---

### 4. **Fleas (still possible in winter)**
- Fleas survive indoors year-round.
- One dog can carry them and trigger itching in all.
- Even a single flea bite can cause intense reactions.

**Clues**
- Scratching at base of tail
- Small black “flea dirt” specks
- One dog worse than others initially

---

### 5. **Mites or skin infection (less common, but important)**
- Mange mites are uncommon but possible
- Secondary bacterial or yeast infections can follow dry skin or allergies

**Clues**
- Hair loss
- Strong odour
- Red, thickened, or weeping skin

---

## What you can do immediately

### Short-term relief
- **Add omega-3s** (fish oil or veterinary supplement)
- **Reduce bathing**; when necessary, use a **moisturising, hypoallergenic shampoo**
- **Wash dog bedding weekly** using unscented detergent
- **Vacuum frequently**, especially carpets and sofas
- **Consider a humidifier** if your home is very dry

### Rule out parasites
- Ensure **up-to-date flea treatment** on all dogs, even in winter

---

## When to see the vet
Book a vet visit if:
- Itching lasts more than 7–10 days
- Skin becomes red, sore, or infected
- One dog is significantly worse than the others
- Hair loss or behavioural distress appears

A vet can quickly differentiate between dry skin, allergy, infection, or parasites and may prescribe short-term anti-itch medication if needed.

---

## Why keying helps (in this case)
Most clear plastics (acrylic, polycarbonate, PETG) are:
- Very smooth
- Low surface energy
- Poor for pressure-sensitive adhesives (PSA) in their as-supplied state

Light keying:
- Increases surface area
- Breaks surface gloss
- Gives the adhesive something to mechanically lock into

For a thin steel rule that may see handling, shear loads, and temperature change, keying is the right move.

---

## How to key it correctly (do this, not more)

### Abrasive
- **600–800 grit wet-and-dry**
- Used **dry or very lightly damp**

### Technique
- Light, even passes in **one direction only**
- Goal: *uniform satin finish*, not visible scratches
- Do **not** gouge or cross-hatch aggressively

You should still be able to see through the plastic; it should just lose its shine.

---

## Critical steps people often miss

### 1. Clean after keying
Wipe with:
- Isopropyl alcohol (IPA)
- Or mild detergent + rinse + fully dry

Do **not** touch the keyed area afterwards.

---

### 2. Tape choice matters
If you’re using generic “sticky-back” tape:
- It will work, but bond longevity is unknown

If you want this to stay put long-term:
- **3M VHB (4910 or 5952)** is ideal
- Thin VHB still counts as “tape” but is orders of magnitude better

---

### 3. Pressure + dwell time
- Apply **firm pressure** along the full length (roller or thumb)
- Leave undisturbed for **12–24 hours** if possible
- PSA strength builds over time

---

## What *not* to do
- Do not leave the plastic glossy
- Do not use coarse grit (<400)
- Do not flame-treat unless you know the plastic type
- Do not rely on peel strength — design for **shear**

---

## Steel side?
The steel rule:
- Usually already fine as-is
- A quick IPA wipe is sufficient
- No need to key unless it’s polished mirror-smooth

---

**Yes: lightly key the plastic with 600–800 grit, clean thoroughly, then tape.**  
That gives you the best possible bond using sticky-back tape.
