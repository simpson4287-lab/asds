Short answer: usually **no — you cannot legitimately add someone else’s mobile-only Ticketmaster ticket directly into their Apple Wallet unless the ticket is transferred to that person by the official ticket platform or you have access to their device/account.**

Here’s why and what actually works.

# How mobile ticketing normally works
- Many venues (Ticketmaster, etc.) use **dynamic mobile tickets** — a QR/barcode that refreshes and is tied to the purchaser’s account or the mobile device.  
- These dynamic codes are **not** ordinary files that anyone can copy and reuse. They refresh and often check against the ticket issuer’s system at the turnstile.  
- Consequently, a **screenshot, PDF, or an image of the ticket will usually be rejected** at entry.

# Legitimate ways to get a ticket into another person’s Apple Wallet
1. **Official transfer through the ticketing platform (recommended).**  
   - Seller initiates a transfer inside Ticketmaster (or the primary seller’s system) to the buyer’s Ticketmaster account, phone number or email.  
   - Recipient accepts the transfer via the official Ticketmaster app or email link and then adds the ticket to their Apple Wallet from their own device.  
   - This is the correct, secure method — it preserves dynamic codes and name-checks if required.

2. **PKPASS or pass file sent directly (only if supported).**  
   - Some platforms can export a signed .pkpass (Apple Wallet pass) that a user can open on their device and add.  
   - **Important:** many concert/mobile-ticket systems do **not** issue a usable PKPASS for mobile-entry events, or they issue passes that are device/account-bound. So this will not always work.

3. **If you have physical access to the recipient’s phone (not recommended).**  
   - Someone with access to their phone could add a delivered PKPASS or accept a transfer on their behalf. This is effectively giving control of the ticket and is insecure.

# What does *not* work / is risky
- **Sending a screenshot, PDF, or drive link** (like in the email you showed) and telling the buyer to add it to Apple Wallet — **this is not a reliable or legitimate method for mobile-only entry** and may lead to refusal at the venue.  
- **Printing out a PDF** for a mobile-only event — almost always invalid.

# Practical steps you should insist on

1. **Initiate an official transfer via Ticketmaster** to the recipient’s Ticketmaster account (or to their email/phone so they can accept).  
2. If Ticketmaster says PKPASS is permitted, **ask for documented proof** (screenshot of the issuer’s policy or an official confirmation email) and for the seller to provide a signed PKPASS — then test it on the recipient’s device.  
3. **Refuse screenshots/PDFs/drive links** for mobile-only events — state in writing you will not accept those as fulfilment.
