Short answer: **yes — but very sparingly**. One or two *anchor phrases* will help senior readers scan without making it look “salesy” or defensive.

### How people like Gav / Paul typically read this
They will:
- Read the opening line
- Skim the middle
- Look for **where you add value** and **what you’re asking for**

Bold should support *that* — not emotion, not history.

---

## What I would bold (and why)

### 1. Your core value proposition (once)
In the first substantive paragraph, pick **one compact phrase** that summarises *why you’re useful*:

> “…into a structured and fully documented lifecycle workflow, **becoming a go-to point for fault finding and complex issue resolution**.”

This works because it:
- Signals *reliability*
- Signals *impact*
- Avoids sounding like a title grab

---

### 2. The forward-looking capability (Altium / coordination)
In the paragraph about optimising quadra / Altium:

> “**optimising quadra’s coordination using Altium** — for example…”

This helps them see:
- You’re not dwelling on the past
- You’re already thinking in systems and tooling

---

### 3. Your natural contribution (quiet leadership signal)
Later on, this sentence is important but currently easy to miss:

> “…my strongest and most sustainable contribution lies in **system delivery, technical awareness, and structured problem-solving**.”

This is exactly the kind of line that should stand out — it frames leadership *without* asking for a role.

---

## What NOT to bold
❌ Emotional context  
❌ Neurodiversity diagnosis (leave that understated — it’s handled well already)  
❌ Any “thanks / appreciation” lines  
❌ Anything that looks like justification or defence  

---

## Formatting rule of thumb
- **Maximum: 3 bold sections**
- Each bold section ≤ 8–10 words
- Never bold consecutive sentences
- Never bold the final paragraph
