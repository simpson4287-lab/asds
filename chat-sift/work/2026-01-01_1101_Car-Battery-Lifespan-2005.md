### Expected lifespan
- **4–6 years** under normal UK driving and climate conditions  
- **3–4 years** is common if the car is used mainly for short trips

### Key factors that shorten lifespan
- Frequent **short journeys** (battery never fully recharges)
- Long periods of **inactivity**
- **Cold weather** stress (especially winter starts)
- Electrical drain (alarms, aftermarket stereos, trackers)
- Corrosion at terminals or poor earths (more common on older cars)

### Practical health indicators
- Engine cranks **slower**, especially when cold  
- Headlights dim noticeably at start-up  
- Battery resting voltage below **12.4 V** (fully charged ≈ 12.6–12.8 V)  
- Battery is already **5+ years old** → statistically near end-of-life

### Bottom line
If a battery fitted to a ~2005 car is **older than 4–5 years**, it should be considered **consumable and near replacement**, even if it has not yet failed outright.

Understood—you are asking **how long a healthy ~2005-era car battery will hold its charge when the car is not being used**.

### Typical charge retention (engine OFF)

For a **healthy lead-acid battery** in a mid-2000s car:

- **2–4 weeks**: should still start the car reliably  
- **4–6 weeks**: borderline; may crank slowly  
- **6–8 weeks**: often too discharged to start  
- **3+ months**: usually deeply discharged or damaged  

This assumes:
- Battery is in good condition  
- No abnormal electrical drain  
- Normal ambient temperatures

### Why it loses charge
Even with the ignition off, a 2005 car typically has **parasitic draw** from:
- Alarm / immobiliser
- ECU memory
- Clock / radio presets

Typical parasitic current:
- **20–50 mA** = normal  
- **>80–100 mA** = fault territory  

At ~40 mA draw, a 60 Ah battery loses roughly:
- **1 Ah per day**
- ~50% capacity in **3–4 weeks**

### Voltage reality check
- **12.6–12.8 V** → fully charged  
- **12.4 V** → ~75%  
- **12.2 V** → ~50% (starting risk)  
- **<12.0 V** → likely no-start  

### Important nuance (age matters)
Even if “healthy,” a battery that is:
- **5+ years old** will self-discharge faster  
- More vulnerable to **sulphation** if left standing  
- May not recover fully after deep discharge  

### Bottom line
A **good condition battery** in a ~2005 car should comfortably hold enough charge to start the engine for **2–4 weeks of no use**.  
If it cannot, either:
- The battery is ageing, **or**
- There is excessive parasitic drain.
