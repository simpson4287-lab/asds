What’s so good about infrared heating panels 

[M2] AI (2026-06-14 09:07):
Infrared heating panels are good mainly because they heat **surfaces and people directly**, rather than trying to heat all the air first.

In a normal convection heater/radiator, warm air rises, moves around, gets lost through draughts, and slowly warms the room. An infrared panel emits radiant heat, so walls, floors, furniture, and your body absorb it directly. That can feel warmer at a lower air temperature.

The main advantages:

**1. Fast comfort**
You feel the heat quickly if you are in the panel’s “line of sight”. Good for offices, workshops, cabins, bathrooms, garden rooms, and rooms you do not heat all day.

**2. Less wasted heat in draughty spaces**
Because it is not relying entirely on warm air staying put, it can be useful where convection heating is inefficient: high ceilings, leaky rooms, garden buildings, garages, etc.

**3. Lower running cost in the right use case**
Not because electricity is magically cheaper, but because you may only need to heat the occupied zone, or you can run the room at a lower thermostat setting. For example, feeling comfortable with air at 17–18°C instead of 20–21°C can save energy.

**4. Good for intermittent heating**
If you only use a space for a few hours, infrared can be better than slowly warming the whole thermal mass with radiators.

**5. Silent, slim, low-maintenance**
No fan, no moving parts, no dust blowing around, no pipework. Ceiling and wall panels also save floor space.

**6. Can reduce damp/cold-surface issues**
Because surfaces get warmed, walls and objects may stay above dew point more easily. That can help with condensation in some situations, though ventilation still matters.

The downsides:

**Line of sight matters.** If the panel is behind you, blocked by furniture, or badly positioned, it can feel underwhelming.

**It does not store much heat.** Once off, the panel cools quickly unless the room’s surfaces have warmed up.

**Electricity is expensive per kWh.** If you are using it to heat a whole house continuously, gas central heating is often cheaper to run in the UK.

**Sizing and placement are critical.** Too small and it feels weak; too close and it feels oddly hot on one side. Ceiling panels are often very effective because they radiate down evenly.

For your kind of use — a cabin, workshop, garden building, or a room used intermittently — infrared panels can make a lot of sense. I would not think of them as “free efficiency”; I’d think of them as **better-directed comfort**. They are best when you want heat where you are, quickly, without heating a lot of unused air.
