Usually the common “small” stud size is:

**38 × 63 mm**

Other very common stud sizes are:

- **38 × 89 mm**
- **38 × 140 mm**

In UK timber, that **38 mm** is normally the finished thickness, with the second number being the width/depth.

So if you were thinking of a light internal stud or small framing member, **38 × 63** is the usual answer. If you mean the really common partition stud size used in many timber merchants, it is often **38 × 89**.

If this is for your cabin wall build, **38 × 63** is the smaller standard section.

How long does it take cement to dry hss enough tondrill

[M7] AI (2026-04-03 18:07):
If you mean **fresh concrete / sand-cement**, not fully cured but just hard enough to drill lightly:

- **24–48 hours**: usually hard enough for **careful light drilling**
- **3–7 days**: much better for normal drilling/fixings
- **28 days**: full cure, maximum strength

A few important bits:

- It can feel hard on top long before it has real strength inside.
- If you drill too early, edges can **blow out, crumble, or weaken**.
- Cool, damp weather slows it down. Warm dry weather speeds the surface, but not always the deep cure.

For practical use:
- **Small pilot hole in non-critical area**: often okay after **24 hours**
- **Anchors / concrete bolts / anything near an edge**: better to wait **at least 3 days**, ideally **7**

If you actually mean **cement board**, that’s different — that can usually be drilled pretty much straight away once fixed in place.
