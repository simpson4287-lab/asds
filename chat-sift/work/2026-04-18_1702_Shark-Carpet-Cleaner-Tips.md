[M2] AI (2026-04-18 17:02):
That looks like a Shark carpet washer / spot-clean style floor head rather than a normal dry vacuum. Best results come from treating it like a wet-clean machine, not just pushing it around quickly.

Best method:

1. **Prep first**
Remove loose dirt first with a normal vacuum if you can. That stops mud forming in the carpet.

2. **Fill the clean tank properly**
Use warm water, not hot, plus the correct carpet cleaning solution if the model calls for it. Do not over-soap it.

3. **Test a small patch**
Especially on a spare-room carpet or anything delicate. Some carpets can react badly to too much moisture.

4. **Go slowly**
Press the spray trigger on the **forward pass** to lay down solution.
Then release the trigger and do a **slow backward pass** to suck it back up.

That backward extraction pass is the important bit.

5. **Do not over-wet**
A common mistake is constant spraying. Use just enough to damp-clean the fibres, then spend more time extracting than spraying.

A good rhythm is:
- 1 wet forward pass
- 2 or 3 dry extraction passes backwards/forwards without spraying

6. **Work in strips**
Do neat overlapping lanes, like mowing a lawn. Start furthest from the door and work backwards so you do not walk on damp carpet.

7. **Stained areas**
Pre-treat bad spots first, leave for a few minutes, then run the machine over them slowly. Do not scrub aggressively with the head.

8. **Empty dirty water often**
Once the dirty tank gets full, suction performance drops.

9. **Drying matters**
Open windows if possible, use airflow, and keep people off it until dry. The drier you extract, the better it will smell and the less chance of mustiness.

10. **Clean the machine after**
Rinse the tanks, flush the head, and remove hair/fluff from the nozzle area. Leaving dirty water inside makes them stink quickly.

Best practical technique:
- Vacuum first
- Light spray only
- Very slow suction passes
- Extra dry passes at the end

Avoid:
- soaking one patch repeatedly
- using lots of foam
- using boiling water
- leaving dirty water in the machine
- cleaning wool/delicate carpet without checking suitability

[M4] AI (2026-04-18 17:03):
Perfect — in that case the best method is to use the two modes deliberately rather than switching randomly.

**What the modes are doing**

**Deep Clean mode**
This is the wet wash mode. It lays down more solution/water and agitates the carpet more aggressively. Use it for actual cleaning, marks, traffic lanes, and dull-looking areas.

**Dry mode**
This is mainly for extraction. It pulls more moisture back out with little or no extra spray. Think of it as the “finish properly” mode.

**Best way to use it**

1. **Vacuum first if possible**
Get rid of dry grit and dust before wet cleaning.

2. **Start in Deep Clean**
Do one slow pass over a strip.
Usually:
- forward pass to apply solution
- backward pass slowly to recover

Do not race. With these machines, slow is better.

3. **Do not keep hammering water into it**
For most areas, one wet pass is enough, maybe two on dirty traffic patches.

4. **Then switch to Dry mode**
Go back over the same strip with **2 to 4 slow passes** in Dry mode.
That is what really improves the result.

5. **Overlap each lane slightly**
About 1–2 inches overlap so you do not leave stripes.

6. **For heavily soiled bits**
Use:
- one Deep Clean pass
- maybe a second Deep Clean pass if needed
- then several Dry mode passes

7. **For lightly dirty carpet**
You may only need:
- one Deep Clean pass
- then lots of Dry mode

That is often better than soaking it.

**Good practical pattern**
For each section:
- Deep Clean once
- Dry mode two or three times
- move to next strip

For bad patches:
- Deep Clean twice
- Dry mode three or four times

**How to judge if you’re doing it right**
You should see:
- dirty water collecting in the tank
- carpet left only slightly damp, not wet
- no squelchy feel underfoot
- no strong lingering detergent smell

**Common mistake with these**
People love the Deep Clean mode and underuse Dry mode. That leaves the carpet too wet and can make it smell off later. The Dry mode is half the job.

**Extra tip**
After finishing the room, do a final full-room pass in **Dry mode only**. That usually makes a noticeable difference to drying time.

**On your pictured carpet**
It looks like a medium-pile synthetic style carpet, so it should generally respond well, but I would still avoid over-wetting any one patch.

[M6] AI (2026-04-18 17:25):
That sounds normal. On many Shark upright carpet washers, the head only engages properly when the handle is **reclined back** and the floor head is bearing weight on the carpet. If it is standing fully upright, the base often lifts slightly and the spray/brush/suction action is reduced or effectively “parked.”

What to do:

- **Tilt the handle back into cleaning position** first.
- Keep the head flat on the carpet.
- Then use normal forward/back passes rather than trying to push down vertically by hand.

If you have to force the head downward while it is upright, you are probably not in the proper operating position.

A few likely possibilities:

**1. Upright lock position**
Many machines lock upright for storage and stop full cleaning action in that position. Put a foot lightly on the base and pull the handle back.

**2. Spray/cleaning only works under load**
Some heads seal better against the carpet when the machine is reclined and moving naturally. Upright, they do not sit right.

**3. Trigger or mode expectation**
On some models, liquid is only dispensed when the clean mode is selected and you are moving in the intended direction. If you are just pressing downward while stationary, it may seem inconsistent.

Best technique to try:
- Fill tank
- Set to **Deep Clean**
- Recline handle
- Push forward slowly with the head resting naturally on carpet
- Then pull back slowly
- Switch to **Dry mode** for extra extraction passes

Do not try to “lean” on it heavily. Just enough natural contact pressure from the machine is usually right.

If it still only works when you physically press the head hard into the floor even when reclined, then one of these may be wrong:
- clogged spray nozzles
- poor clean-tank seating
- blocked suction path
- brush/head not fully clipped in
- belt/brush engagement issue depending on model

Have a look for a **recline release** near the base or just behind the main body. If you want, send one more photo from the side and I’ll tell you exactly how it should be sitting when in use.
