[M2] AI (2026-08-05 06:16):
Short answer: **I wouldn't apply that mortar repair directly over the cured Bostik Aquaprufe** if you want it to stay put.

The Bostik Aquaprufe forms a flexible, waterproof bitumen/rubber coating. Cementitious products like the No Nonsense 644+ mortar repair generally **don't bond reliably to bitumen-based waterproof membranes**. They tend to debond or crack because:
- the Aquaprufe surface is non-porous,
- it's slightly flexible,
- cement wants to bond to mineral substrates such as concrete or masonry.

Looking at your photo, it appears you're trying to remove a shallow depression along the edge before continuing your wall build-up.

### Better options

- **If it's only 2–3 mm deep:** I'd use a **PU sealant/adhesive** (e.g. Sikaflex 11FC or CT1) and strike it off level. It bonds very well to the Aquaprufe and remains waterproof.
- **If it needs to be hard and flat:** Fill the depression **before** applying the Aquaprufe. That is the ideal sequence.
- **If the Aquaprufe is already on (as yours is):** A compatible polyurethane filler is the safest choice.

### Could you get away with it?

If the depression is tiny and isn't going to see movement, the mortar might appear to stick initially, but I wouldn't rely on it long term. It's likely to crack or lift from the coating.

One question: **Is this depression in the concrete slab itself, or is it in the Aquaprufe coating that's built up around the timber sole plate?** That will determine the best repair method.
