That’s a really good question — and it’s exactly the right thing to ask before going into an ASD assessment, because **psychiatrists** (or clinical psychologists) often build their understanding from a *broad developmental and behavioural picture*, not just current symptoms.  

Here’s a structured overview of the main areas they’ll want to know about before and during an Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) assessment — especially for **adult assessments** but also relevant for late-teen/adolescent cases:

---

## 🔹 1. Background & Context
**Purpose:** To understand your life circumstances and developmental history.
- **Basic details:** age, education/employment, family background, living situation.  
- **Why assessment is being sought:** e.g. lifelong difficulties, recent struggles, referral by GP or therapist.  
- **Existing diagnoses:** ADHD, anxiety, depression, OCD, etc.  
- **Medication history** and any relevant medical/neurological issues.  
- **Family history:** relatives with ASD, ADHD, anxiety, depression, or other neurodivergent traits.

---

## 🔹 2. Early Development & Childhood Behaviour
**Purpose:** To identify signs that were present early in life (since ASD is neurodevelopmental).  
- **Social behaviour as a child:** friendships, play style (e.g. solitary play, imaginative play, rigid play).  
- **Communication:** speech development, literal thinking, tone of voice, unusual vocabulary.  
- **Sensory sensitivities:** sound, light, textures, clothing, food, etc.  
- **Repetitive behaviours or routines:** lining things up, insistence on sameness.  
- **Emotional regulation:** meltdowns, shutdowns, comfort objects (e.g. blanket, toy).  
- **Interests:** any intense or unusual fixations, strong focus on specific topics.  
- **School reports:** comments about social withdrawal, concentration, perfectionism, or misunderstanding peers/teachers.

*(If you already have your old school reports, those are extremely useful to bring or summarise.)*

---

## 🔹 3. Social & Communication Style (Across Life)
**Purpose:** To explore social understanding and reciprocity.  
- **Friendships:** ease of making/maintaining them, social exhaustion, misunderstandings.  
- **Conversations:** tendency to monologue, difficulty with small talk, taking things literally.  
- **Reading people:** difficulty with tone, facial expressions, sarcasm, or “hidden meanings.”  
- **Eye contact:** comfort level and awareness.  
- **Masking:** how much effort goes into appearing “normal” in social settings.  

---

## 🔹 4. Cognitive & Behavioural Patterns
**Purpose:** To assess thinking style and behavioural tendencies.
- **Rigidity / need for control:** distress when plans change, preference for predictability.  
- **Perfectionism / overfocus:** hyperfocus on details, narrow interests, “black-and-white” thinking.  
- **Executive functioning:** organisation, time management, initiation of tasks, switching attention.  
- **Literal thinking / visual thinking:** how you process information.  
- **Monotasking vs multitasking:** tendency to struggle when interrupted.

---

## 🔹 5. Sensory Profile
**Purpose:** To identify sensory differences that influence comfort and behaviour.
- **Over- or under-sensitivity** to noise, lights, textures, smells, or touch.  
- **Body awareness:** clumsiness, coordination issues, fidgeting.  
- **Self-soothing habits:** repetitive movements, humming, tapping, rocking, etc.  

---

## 🔹 6. Emotional & Mental Health
**Purpose:** To separate what’s ASD-related from co-occurring mental health conditions.  
- **Anxiety / panic:** especially social or sensory-related.  
- **Depression:** burnout, exhaustion from masking, loneliness.  
- **Trauma or bullying:** common in undiagnosed ASD individuals.  
- **Sleep patterns:** irregularities, nighttime hyperfocus.  

---

## 🔹 7. Daily Functioning & Independence
**Purpose:** To understand the real-world impact.
- **Self-care / routines:** managing hygiene, meals, finances, appointments.  
- **Employment / education:** difficulties following instructions, dealing with feedback, sensory environments.  
- **Relationships:** romantic, family, work – communication challenges or misunderstandings.  
- **Coping mechanisms:** ways of managing stress or sensory overload.  

---

## 🔹 8. Special Interests / Focused Activities
**Purpose:** To understand your passions and how they relate to wellbeing.
- Nature of interests — depth and intensity.  
- Whether they provide comfort, structure, or escapism.  
- Any history of collecting, cataloguing, or pattern-tracking within those interests.  

---

## 🔹 9. Adult Presentation & Masking
**Purpose:** To explore how traits show up now that you’ve learned to cope or camouflage.
- **Adaptations:** what you do to “fit in” or hide discomfort.  
- **Social exhaustion / burnout:** after prolonged interaction.  
- **Identity confusion:** uncertainty about who you really are without masking.  
- **How much energy masking takes, and how it affects wellbeing.**

---

## 🔹 10. Goals & Expectations
**Purpose:** To clarify what you want from the assessment.
- Validation / understanding of lifelong patterns.  
- Access to workplace or academic adjustments.  
- Clearer mental health treatment planning.  
- Self-acceptance or understanding of differences.
