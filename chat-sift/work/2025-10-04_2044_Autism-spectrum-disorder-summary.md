### **Autism Spectrum Disorder (F84.0) — DSM-5-TR Summary**

**Definition**  
Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a **neurodevelopmental condition** characterized by persistent difficulties in:
1. **Social communication and social interaction** across multiple contexts, and  
2. **Restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities.**

These symptoms begin in the **early developmental period**, cause **clinically significant impairment** in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning, and are **not better explained by intellectual disability or global developmental delay**.

---

### **Diagnostic Criteria Overview**
To meet criteria for ASD, all of the following must be present:

**A. Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction**, including:
- Deficits in social-emotional reciprocity (e.g., abnormal social approach, failure to engage in normal conversation).  
- Deficits in nonverbal communicative behaviors (e.g., poor eye contact, gestures, facial expression).  
- Deficits in developing, maintaining, and understanding relationships.

**B. Restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities**, manifested by at least two of:
- Stereotyped or repetitive movements, use of objects, or speech.  
- Insistence on sameness, inflexible adherence to routines.  
- Highly restricted, fixated interests that are abnormal in intensity or focus.  
- Hyper- or hyporeactivity to sensory input or unusual interest in sensory aspects of the environment.

**C. Symptoms must be present in early development**, though they may not become fully manifest until social demands exceed limited capacities.

**D. Symptoms cause significant impairment** in functioning.

**E. Not better explained by intellectual disability or global developmental delay.**

---

### **Specifiers and Severity**
- **Severity levels:**  
  - Level 3 – Requiring very substantial support  
  - Level 2 – Requiring substantial support  
  - Level 1 – Requiring support  

- **Specify if:**  
  - With/without accompanying intellectual impairment  
  - With/without accompanying language impairment  
  - Associated with known medical/genetic condition or environmental factor  
  - Associated with another neurodevelopmental, mental, or behavioral disorder  
  - With catatonia  

---

### **Associated Features**
- May include intellectual disability, uneven cognitive profiles, motor coordination difficulties, and self-injury.  
- Abnormal sensory responses (e.g., sensitivity to sounds, textures).  
- Sleep and eating disturbances are common.

---

### **Development and Course**
- Symptoms typically emerge **between 12–24 months**.  
- Early signs may include lack of interest in peers, limited gestures, or absence of response to name.  
- Regression of language or social skills can occur in some cases.  
- Most individuals show improvement in adaptive functioning with support, though **social communication difficulties persist**.

---

### **Prevalence**
- Approx. **1% of the population worldwide**;  
- **Male-to-female ratio ≈ 3:1**, though underdiagnosis in females is noted.

---

### **Risk and Prognostic Factors**
- Strong **genetic component** (high heritability).  
- Associated with certain medical/genetic conditions (e.g., Fragile X syndrome, Rett syndrome).  
- Environmental risk factors include advanced parental age, low birth weight, or prenatal exposure to certain substances.

---

### **Functional Consequences**
- Challenges in **education, employment, independent living**, and **social relationships**.  
- Difficulties adapting to change, planning, and organization.  
- Increased risk for **social isolation, mental health disorders**, and **accidental injury** (e.g., drowning in children).

---

### **Differential Diagnosis**
- **ADHD:** Lacks restricted/repetitive behaviors.  
- **Intellectual Developmental Disorder:** Social communication not disproportionately impaired.  
- **Language Disorder/Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder:** No repetitive behaviors.  
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Social avoidance due to fear, not fundamental deficits.  
- **Rett Syndrome, Selective Mutism, Stereotypic Movement Disorder** — ruled out by specific criteria.

---

### **Key DSM-5-TR Change**
DSM-5 merged previous categories (**Autistic Disorder, Asperger’s Disorder, Childhood Disintegrative Disorder, PDD-NOS**) into a **single diagnosis: Autism Spectrum Disorder**, reflecting a continuum of severity and symptoms rather than distinct conditions【8:15†DSM-5.pdf】.
