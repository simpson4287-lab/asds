## What’s the best “cheap” model (i.e. best value) to aim for?

When buying used, you want to balance cost versus longevity, performance, and upgradeability. Here are my recommendations and trade-offs.

### What to aim for

- **10-core version is sweet spot.** The 8-core base is decent, but stepping up to 10 cores gives a better multicore boost for many pro workloads (video, rendering, etc.) without going full ham with power draw. Many in professional forums have cited that 10-core gives the best balance between cost and performance. citeturn0search13turn0search1  
- **Good GPU (Vega 56 / 64).** The GPU is often the bottleneck in creative workloads. The Vega 56 is good; if you can find a Vega 64 (or upgraded GPU) that helps.  
- **32 GB or more of ECC RAM.** 32 GB is arguably the minimum for many “pro” tasks today; more is useful if you do 4K video, large image editing, etc.  
- **Healthy SSD.** Since the drive will have been used, check the health (wear level) of the SSD. If possible, replace with a larger/fresh SSD.  
- **Thermal & power integrity.** Check for fans, heatsinks, condition (no liquid damage, good cooling).  
- **Good warranty or return window.** Because these are older machines, parts may fail.

Thus: a 2017 iMac Pro with **10 cores, Vega 56 or better GPU, 32+ GB RAM, 1 TB SSD** is likely the “best cheap” target.

### Trade-offs & caveats

- The iMac Pro is not user-friendly to upgrade (especially parts like GPU) compared to desktops.  
- The SSD, though replaceable in principle, may require special protocols or connectors.  
- Future macOS support: these are Intel systems; at some point Apple may drop support.  
- Power usage and heat: the machine is high performance, so ensure your setup can handle it.

## Tips & checks when buying a used one

When you go to buy:

1. **Check battery cycle count & condition** — if the battery is near end of life, that’s a costly replacement.
2. **Check keyboard / logic board / repair history** — especially for 2016–2017 butterfly keyboards.
3. **Check SSD health & storage capacity** — sometimes these are worn or small.
4. **Check for recalls** (especially for the 15″ battery recall).
5. **Check for external I/O compatibility** — do you need USB-A, HDMI, SD card reader? Some are limited (USB-C only, etc.).
6. **Ask for proof of operation** — boot up, run benchmarks, check performance under load.
7. **Ensure there’s some return window or warranty from seller** — with older machines, issues may crop up later.
