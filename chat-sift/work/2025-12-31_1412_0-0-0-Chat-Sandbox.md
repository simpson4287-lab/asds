This is a very common and understandable point of confusion. The short answer is: **you are entitled to information, but not all information, not immediately, and not always directly from the police**. In the UK system, *how* and *when* you receive material depends on the procedural stage and the disclosure route.

Below is a **clear, factual explanation** without advocacy.

---

## 1. The key distinction: **entitlement vs route of access**

In UK criminal procedure, there is a difference between:

- **Legal entitlement to material**, and  
- **The mechanism through which that material is provided**

You may be *entitled* to certain material, but that does **not automatically mean** the police must give it to you directly, on request, at any stage.

---

## 2. Interview recordings specifically

### What you are entitled to
Once a case proceeds beyond interview (e.g. charge or summons):

- The interview recording is **disclosable material**
- It forms part of the **case file submitted to CPS**
- You are entitled to **access it for the purposes of your defence**

### How it is normally accessed
In practice, access is usually provided:
- **Via a solicitor**, as part of the disclosure process  
- After CPS involvement, not directly by the interviewing officer

Police officers are generally **not authorised to bypass that process**, even if entitlement exists.

That is why PC Roll’s response focuses on *route* rather than *rights*.

---

## 3. Why police often won’t provide it directly to you

This is procedural, not punitive:

- Disclosure is governed by CPIA and CPS processes
- Material must be:
  - Logged
  - Categorised (unused / used)
  - Disclosed through controlled channels
- Police avoid direct release to defendants to:
  - Prevent partial disclosure
  - Avoid evidential contamination
  - Ensure parity of arms if representation is later instructed

Hence the repeated reference to **“via your solicitor”**, even when one is not yet instructed.

---

## 4. What happens if you have **no solicitor**

This does **not** remove your entitlement, but it does change the practical reality:

- Police may say disclosure is only available once representation exists  
- Alternatively, they may direct you to:
  - CPS (if proceedings have started), or
  - The court process (post-charge / post-summons)

This is why your clarification email was correct and necessary: it flags a **process gap**, not a legal argument.

---

## 5. What you should *not* assume (important)

You should not assume that:
- “Entitled” = “immediate”
- “Entitled” = “direct from police”
- Silence or redirection = refusal
- Lack of disclosure at this stage = breach

Those conclusions belong to later analysis layers **only if** the process fails after the correct route is followed.

Right now, nothing improper has been established — only **process dependency**.
