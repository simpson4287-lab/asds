## COURT DAY GUIDANCE & ORAL DISCIPLINE  
*(To be read the night before and again on the morning)*

### Purpose of This Section  
This section exists to ensure that your conduct on the day **supports** the written mitigation already before the court, and that nothing said or done undermines it.

Your written pack does the work.  
Your job on the day is **not to add**, **not to explain**, and **not to rescue** anything.

---

## PART A — Logistics on the Day

### Arrival & Preparation
- Arrive **at least 30 minutes early**
- Bring:
  - Your mitigation pack (even if already filed)
  - Photo ID
  - Any DVLA correspondence (only if requested)
- Dress:
  - Plain, professional, and conservative
  - Nothing that draws attention or signals protest, distress, or defiance

### Waiting Area Conduct
- Minimal phone use
- No rehearsing speeches aloud
- No visible agitation
- Polite, quiet, observant

This is not performance — it is containment.

---

## PART B — When You Are Called In

### How to Stand and Address the Bench
- Stand when speaking
- Address the court as **“Sir / Madam”**
- Speak slowly
- One idea per sentence
- No qualifying language (“just”, “really”, “to be honest”)

---

## PART C — If Asked: “Is There Anything You’d Like to Add?”

### One-Sentence Closing (Only If Asked)

You use **one sentence only**.  
You stop after it.

> **“Only that once I understood how unwell I had been, I took responsibility straight away, stopped driving voluntarily, and put safeguards in place to make sure this doesn’t happen again.”**

That’s it.  
No follow-up.  
No explanation.  
No second sentence.

**Why this works**
- Signals insight
- Reinforces voluntary action
- Demonstrates future safety
- Ends cleanly and confidently
- Invites the bench to move on

If they want more, they will ask.

---

## PART D — What Not to Say (This Matters)

This is the discipline that protects strong mitigation from collapsing into self-harm.

### ❌ Do Not Say — Even If True
- “I had no control over myself”
- “I didn’t know what I was doing”
- “I can’t remember much of that night”
- “The system failed me”
- “Work pushed me too hard”
- “I was desperate / at breaking point”
- “It was out of character”
- “I was trying to cope”

These statements:
- Reopen culpability
- Invite probing
- Sound like avoidance rather than insight

Your written pack already carries these **safely**.  
You do not need to.

---

## PART E — Do Not Elaborate if Interrupted

If a magistrate says:
- “Thank you”
- “That’s enough”
- “We’ve read the papers”

**You stop immediately.**

Continuing after this is one of the most common sentencing mistakes.  
Silence here signals respect and self-control.

---

## PART F — Do Not Correct or Clarify Facts

Even if something sounds slightly wrong.

This is sentencing, not a debate.  
If correction is needed, a solicitor handles it — not you.

---

## PART G — What You Can Safely Say (Only If Directly Asked)

Keep answers **short, factual, and final**.

**If asked about driving now:**
> “The DVLA has assessed me and issued a medical licence, and I continue to follow medical advice.”

**If asked about the dogs:**
> “They were affected by my voluntary decision to stop driving, but that was a consequence I accepted in the interests of safety.”

**If asked about health:**
> “It’s now stable and managed under supervision.”

Then stop.

---

## PART H — Mental Frame to Hold Throughout

- You are not here to persuade — the papers already do that
- You are not here to explain your life
- You are here to demonstrate:
  - insight
  - restraint
  - responsibility
  - future safety

Calm, brevity, and silence are doing work even when you are not speaking.
