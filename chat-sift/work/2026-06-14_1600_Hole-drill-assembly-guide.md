[M2] AI (2026-06-14 16:00):
That “extra piece” is the **arbor collar / flange washer** for the hole saw.

Your setup has three main parts:

1. **Arbor / mandrel** — the black hex shank bit holder.
2. **Pilot drill bit** — the small drill in the middle. This starts the hole and keeps the saw centred.
3. **Hole saw cup** — the toothed circular cutter.

The loose black ring normally sits on the arbor and acts as a **backing washer / spacer / flange** so the hole saw tightens up properly and runs square.

### How to assemble it

Slide the loose ring onto the arbor first, with the flatter face toward the hole saw.

Then screw the hole saw cup onto the threaded part of the arbor until it tightens against that ring/washer.

The pilot drill should stick out beyond the teeth by roughly **5–10 mm**.

Tighten the little grub screw on the collar so the pilot bit is locked in place.

### How to use it

Put the pilot drill on your mark and start slowly. Let the pilot drill bite first, then the teeth will start cutting the larger circle.

Use **low speed, light pressure**. Don’t force it. For timber, back it out occasionally to clear sawdust. For metal, go slow and use cutting oil.

### Important bit

That hole saw looks like a **32 mm** one, so it’ll make a 32 mm diameter hole. The pilot bit is not meant to cut the big hole — it only guides the saw.

The loose ring is not a separate cutter; it’s part of the arbor assembly so the hole saw clamps correctly.

[M8] AI (2026-06-14 17:13):
Yes, cement board will **blunt normal hole saws and wood/metal drill bits very quickly**. It’s abrasive, not “hard” like steel, but it chews cutting edges.

I **wouldn’t use paddle/spade bits**. They’re for timber and will probably go blunt even faster, chatter, tear the board, and maybe jam.

Better options:

### Best option
Use a **tungsten-carbide grit hole saw** or **diamond hole saw/tile hole cutter** in the size you need. These don’t rely on sharp teeth as much, so they survive cement board much better.

Search terms:
**“32mm diamond hole saw”**  
**“32mm carbide grit hole saw”**  
**“tile hole saw 32mm”**

### Cheap practical option
Use a **masonry bit** to drill a ring of small holes around your marked circle, then knock out the centre and tidy the edge with a file/rasp. Messier, but works fine for hidden ventilation/service holes.

### If you only need rough holes
A **multi-tool with carbide/grout blade** or a **jigsaw with carbide blade** can cut cement board, but it makes a lot of dust.

### Technique
Run the drill **slow**, don’t force it, and let the cutter grind. If using diamond, a little water helps keep dust and heat down, unless the board/product says keep dry.

Wear a mask/eye protection — cement board dust is nasty. If it’s fibre-cement, avoid breathing any of it.

For what you’re doing, I’d buy a cheap **32 mm diamond tile hole saw** rather than sacrifice more normal bits.

[M10] AI (2026-06-15 14:28):
I wouldn’t use **alcohol gel** for drilling cement board.

It won’t really lubricate the cut, and it can leave sticky residue that clogs the cutter. It’s also flammable, so not ideal around heat/friction from a blunting bit.

Use one of these instead:

**Best:** a few drops/spray of **water** while using a diamond or carbide-grit hole saw. It keeps dust down and stops the cutter overheating.

**If using your current toothed hole saw:** water may help a bit with dust/heat, but it still won’t stop it blunting because cement board is abrasive.

**Practical method:** drill slowly, light pressure, pull out often, and hoover/dampen dust as you go.

For cement board, the real fix is the cutter type: **diamond tile hole saw** or **carbide-grit hole saw**, not alcohol gel or spade/paddle bits.
