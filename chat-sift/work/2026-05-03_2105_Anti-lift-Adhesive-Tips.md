[M2] AI (2026-05-03 21:05):
Yes — **a small temporary screw/pin or clamp is better than relying on caulk/silicone to stop it lifting**.

If you mean **caulk/silicone**:

**Caulk:**  
Not really. Decorator’s caulk has poor structural strength and shrinks. It is for filling gaps, not holding timber down.

**Silicone / Sticks All / hybrid sealant:**  
Better than caulk, but still not ideal as the main anti-lift restraint. It has grab, but it can stay rubbery and allow the piece to creep or peel if the timber is slightly bowed.

For your light pieces on a gate, I’d do this:

1. **Use the grab adhesive as the main bond** — thick zig-zag or beads, not just perimeter.
2. **Press into place, pull off briefly, then re-seat** if the adhesive instructions say that. Some solvent grab adhesives work better this way because they flash off slightly.
3. **Use tape, clamps, temporary screws, or a few pins** to stop lift while curing.
4. Once cured, you can remove temporary screws/pins and fill the holes.

If you absolutely don’t want screws, use **strong tape across the piece**, a **prop/wedge**, or a **batten clamped across it** while it cures.

Best combination from what you have: **grab adhesive as the main body**, then **Sticks All around selected edges/gaps as a flexible seal**, but don’t expect the Sticks All perimeter alone to clamp a lifted piece flat.

[M4] AI (2026-05-03 21:06):
Ah — yes, I think you mean **liquid DPM**, and it’s **lifting because it stays tacky when you’re walking/tacking on it**.

**Chalk/talc can reduce the tack**, but I’d treat it as a **surface-dusting workaround**, not a proper fix.

Use **talc, chalk dust, or fine dry sand** only if:

- the liquid DPM is already properly cured underneath;
- you only need to stop it sticking to shoes/tools temporarily;
- you are not relying on the next coat bonding chemically to that dusty surface.

Best options:

**If you still need another coat:**  
Don’t chalk it yet. Let it cure longer, then overcoat. Dusting it can reduce adhesion of the next coat unless you clean it back off.

**If it is the final coat and just tacky:**  
Lightly dust with **talc/chalk/fine kiln-dried sand**, then sweep off the excess. Fine sand is often better if it may get boarded over because it gives a bit of mechanical key.

**If it is lifting when touched/walked on:**  
That usually means one of these:

- coat is too thick;
- surface underneath was damp/dusty/chalky;
- previous coat had not cured;
- cold weather slowed curing;
- bitumen/solvent layer is softening the layer below.

So yes, **chalk can reduce tack**, but if the DPM is actually peeling or smearing, it needs **more curing time or surface prep**, not just dusting. For a cabin/base job, I’d prefer **leave it longer, then dust very lightly with fine dry sand once cured enough**.
