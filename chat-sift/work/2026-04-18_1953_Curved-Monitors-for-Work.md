Yes, often — but it depends on the size and the kind of design work.

For **document work, CAD, spreadsheets, email, reports**, a curved monitor can be very nice because it makes a wide screen feel a bit more natural to scan across. On a **34" ultrawide** in particular, the curve usually helps reduce that “flat wall of screen” feeling.

For **design work**, it splits into cases:

- **General design / layout / UI / documents / presentations:** curved can be perfectly good.
- **Precision visual work** like photo editing, print colour work, or anything where perfectly straight lines and geometry matter visually: many people still prefer a **flat monitor**, especially if they are sensitive to subtle distortion at the edges.

The main pros are:
- more comfortable on larger screens
- better immersion on ultrawides
- edge areas can feel easier to view

The main downsides are:
- straight lines can feel slightly “off” to some people
- can be less ideal for exact visual judgement
- awkward if several people need to view it from different angles

My practical take:

- **27" or smaller:** flat is usually better and simpler
- **32" wide or 34" ultrawide:** curved often makes sense
- **colour-critical graphics/photo work:** flat usually safer
- **documents, engineering tools, coding, Altium/CAD, office work:** curved can be excellent

A lot depends on panel quality too. A **good flat monitor** is usually better than a **poor curved one**. Resolution, sharpness, colour accuracy, brightness, and text clarity matter more than the curve itself.

For your sort of use, if it is heavily **document + technical design + lots of windows open**, a **34" curved ultrawide** can work very well. If you want, I can narrow it down to **best monitor shape/size for electronics design, Altium, and document writing specifically**.

- **SSD** = startup disk, apps, active work
