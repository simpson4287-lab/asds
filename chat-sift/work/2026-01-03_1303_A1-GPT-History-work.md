The alleged offences under consideration are:

- **Driving a motor vehicle while unfit through drugs** (section 4(1), Road Traffic Act 1988)  
- **Being in charge of a motor vehicle while unfit through drugs** (section 4(2), Road Traffic Act 1988)

Both offences require the prosecution to establish specific statutory elements beyond mere presence in a vehicle or later toxicological findings.

---

### 7.4.2 Location and “Public Place” Requirement

A fundamental element of both offences is that the conduct must occur on a **road or other public place**.

At the material time, Hylands Park was subject to local authority by-laws providing for closure from dusk until dawn. During closure hours, lawful public access is withdrawn. The presence of an unlocked or open gate does not reinstate public access where access is otherwise prohibited by regulation.

CCTV footage confirms:
- No members of the public were present.
- No through-route or right of way was in use.
- The area was closed, dark, and deserted.

In these circumstances, the location does not satisfy the statutory definition of a “road or public place” for the purposes of section 4 of the Road Traffic Act 1988. This represents a threshold evidential weakness in the prosecution case.

The factual matrix distinguishes clearly between:
- **Confirmed driving**, and  
- **Subsequent stationary presence in the vehicle**.

The last confirmed movement of the vehicle occurred at approximately **01:00**, involving slow, controlled driving over a very short distance within the closed park to a gate area. From that point until police attendance at approximately **04:00**, the vehicle remained stationary.

The presence of keys in the ignition is explained by established ADHD-related behaviour patterns concerning object retention and habit, rather than any intention to drive. There is no evidence of preparatory conduct suggesting imminent driving while unfit.

On these facts, the case aligns, at most, with the concept of being “in charge” rather than active driving.

Section 4(3) of the Road Traffic Act 1988 provides a statutory defence where it can be shown that there was **no likelihood of the defendant driving while unfit**.
