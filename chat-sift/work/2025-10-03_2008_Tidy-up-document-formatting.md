# **ADOS-2: Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule – Second Edition**  
## **Part I: Modules 1–4**  
### **Section 1. Introduction**

The **Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule, Second Edition (ADOS-2)** is a standardized tool used to observe and assess behaviors related to **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**.  
It provides a structured setting in which a trained examiner can evaluate how a person communicates, interacts socially, and engages in play or imagination.

### **Purpose of the ADOS-2**
The ADOS-2 helps professionals:
- Identify behaviors consistent with autism spectrum disorder.  
- Support diagnostic decisions when used with other assessments and reports.  
- Observe how individuals respond to social situations and structured tasks.

It is not meant to be the only tool for diagnosis. Instead, it complements other clinical information such as developmental history, parent interviews, and psychological testing.

### **Structure of the ADOS-2**
The ADOS-2 is divided into **five modules**, each designed for a different **developmental and language level**:

| **Module** | **Language / Developmental Level** | **Typical Age Range** | **Focus of Activities** |
|-------------|------------------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Toddler Module (T) | Preverbal or early speech | 12–30 months | Play and simple interaction |
| Module 1 | Preverbal or single words | Any age | Early social communication |
| Module 2 | Phrase speech | Any age | Play and communication |
| Module 3 | Fluent speech (children/adolescents) | Typically 4–16 years | Conversation, social imagination |
| Module 4 | Fluent speech (older teens/adults) | 16+ years | Conversation, relationships, daily life |

Each module includes a set of **activities and observations** that encourage communication and social responses, allowing the examiner to assess specific behavioral patterns.

### **What the ADOS-2 Measures**
The ADOS-2 focuses on observable behaviors in three main areas:
1. **Communication** – how the person uses and understands language, gestures, or nonverbal signals.  
2. **Social Interaction** – how the person initiates and responds to social contact.  
3. **Restricted or Repetitive Behaviors** – actions or interests that may appear unusual, repetitive, or highly focused.

These behaviors are scored according to standardized coding rules. The scores contribute to diagnostic algorithms that help determine whether a person’s behavior fits within the autism spectrum.

### **Who Uses the ADOS-2**
The ADOS-2 should be administered and interpreted by **professionals trained in its use** — typically psychologists, psychiatrists, or other specialists in autism assessment.  
Training ensures the examiner understands how to observe, code, and interpret behaviors consistently.

### **Improvements in the Second Edition**
The **ADOS-2** builds on the original **ADOS** by:
- Adding the **Toddler Module (T)** for younger children.  
- Refining items and scoring for more accurate classification.  
- Improving algorithms to align better with updated diagnostic criteria (e.g., DSM-5).  
- Providing clearer administration and coding guidance.  

These updates make the ADOS-2 more flexible and reliable across a wider range of ages and abilities.

### **How ADOS-2 Is Used Clinically**
The assessment typically involves:
1. Selecting the appropriate module based on language ability.  
2. Conducting a one-on-one session (usually 30–60 minutes).  
3. Observing and recording the person’s responses.  
4. Scoring the behaviors after the session using standardized codes.  
5. Interpreting the scores alongside background information to inform diagnosis or treatment planning.

### **Ethical and Practical Considerations**
- The ADOS-2 should always be used with respect, empathy, and clinical judgment.  
- Results should never be used to label or stigmatize.  
- Feedback to families should be delivered clearly and sensitively, emphasizing strengths as well as challenges.

# **ADOS-2: Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule – Second Edition**  
## **Part I: Modules 1–4**  
### **Section 2. Materials and Description of the Instrument**

### **Overview**
The ADOS-2 is a structured assessment made up of several **standardized materials and activities**.  
Each item is designed to create opportunities for communication, play, and social interaction that help the examiner observe key behaviors associated with **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**.

The materials and procedures are selected so that results are **comparable** across examiners, modules, and sessions.  
Every examiner must use the **same materials** and follow **consistent guidelines** to ensure reliability.

---

### **Purpose of the Materials**
The objects, pictures, and toys used in the ADOS-2 are chosen to:
- Encourage **spontaneous social interaction**.  
- Stimulate **language and nonverbal communication**.  
- Support **symbolic and imaginative play**.  
- Provide opportunities to observe **repetitive or restricted interests**.  

All items are **standardized** – they should not be substituted or replaced with similar ones, since differences in shape, color, or familiarity could alter the individual’s response.

---

### **Testing Environment**
A quiet, distraction-free room is ideal.  
The room should allow comfortable movement, with a table and chairs, a floor area for play, and space for the examiner to sit opposite or beside the participant.  

Essential setup guidelines:
- Keep **all materials organized** and ready before the session.  
- Only bring out items when the corresponding activity begins.  
- Maintain a **natural but structured interaction style**.  
- Avoid excessive verbal prompting; the goal is to observe **spontaneous** behavior.  

---

Below is a simplified summary of the materials required for each module.

| **Module** | **Main Materials Required** | **Purpose / Activity Focus** |

| **Toddler Module (T)** | Bubbles, toy phone, blocks, doll, spoon, toy car, ball, picture book | Promotes early communication, imitation, shared play |

Each module includes an **Examiner’s Protocol** and a **Scoring Form**, which list the behaviors to observe and code during the session.

---

### **Description of the Instrument**
The ADOS-2 consists of several structured **tasks** or **activities**, arranged in a fixed sequence.  
Each task provides opportunities to observe behaviors in three areas:
1. **Communication**
2. **Reciprocal Social Interaction**
3. **Restricted and Repetitive Behaviors**

During the session, the examiner:
- Engages the participant through activities such as play, storytelling, or conversation.  
- Observes **how the person initiates, maintains, and responds** to social and communication opportunities.  
- Notes specific behaviors using brief written observations.  
- Codes behaviors afterward using standardized scoring rules.

---

### **Recording and Scoring Materials**
Each module has:
- A **Protocol Booklet** – lists each activity with observation boxes.  
- A **Scoring Form** – used to code behaviors numerically.  
- **Algorithm Sheets** – combine key items to produce diagnostic classification scores.  

Scoring involves assigning a number (usually 0–3) for each observed behavior.  
Higher scores indicate **greater deviation** from typical development.  
Scores are later entered into an algorithm that determines whether the person meets the cutoff for an ASD classification.

---

### **Examiner’s Role**
The examiner acts both as an **interaction partner** and an **observer**.  
They balance natural conversation and play with the need to follow a consistent procedure.  

Key examiner responsibilities:
- **Prepare** materials and room setup in advance.  
- **Follow** the standard order of activities.  
- **Avoid** unnecessary coaching or repetition.  
- **Record** all observations objectively and immediately after the session.  
- **Respect** the participant’s comfort and pacing.  

---

### **Summary**
The ADOS-2 materials and structure are designed to create a **reliable, standardized social environment** for observing behaviors relevant to ASD.  
When administered carefully, they provide valuable insights into how a person communicates and interacts in real-time situations.
