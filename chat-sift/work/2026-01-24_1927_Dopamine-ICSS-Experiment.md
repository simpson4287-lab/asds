## Core experiment: Intracranial Self-Stimulation

**Researchers:** entity["people","James Olds","neuroscientist"] and entity["people","Peter Milner","neuroscientist"] (1954)

### What they did
- Implanted a **thin electrode** into specific brain regions of rats.
- Connected the electrode to a **lever** the rat could press.
- Pressing the lever delivered a **brief electrical stimulation**.

### What they found
- When electrodes were placed in reward-related regions (later identified as part of the **dopamine system**), rats:
  - Pressed the lever **thousands of times per hour**
  - Preferred stimulation over **food, water, and sleep**
  - Sometimes ignored basic needs entirely

This revealed that certain brain circuits strongly **reinforce behaviour**.

## Refinement: Dopamine-specific experiments
image_group{"query":["dopamine microdialysis rat experiment","dopamine optogenetics mouse reward","rat nucleus accumbens dopamine graph"]}

Later experiments replaced crude stimulation with more precise tools:

### 1. Microdialysis / fast-scan voltammetry
- Directly **measured dopamine release** while animals performed tasks.
- Showed dopamine spikes during **anticipation**, not just pleasure.

### 2. Pharmacological manipulation
- Dopamine blockers (e.g. antagonists) **reduced lever pressing**
- Dopamine agonists **increased motivation**, even without reward

### 3. Optogenetics (modern)
- Light-activated dopamine neurons in mice
- Turning dopamine neurons **on/off** controlled learning and motivation in real time

---

## Key conclusion (important distinction)
Dopamine is **not simply “pleasure”**.

It primarily signals:
- **Motivation**
- **Reward prediction**
- **Learning what actions are worth repeating**

Pleasure (“liking”) and motivation (“wanting”) are separable systems.
