Yes — that space looks usable for a **lean-to micro canopy / bike bay**, but I’d treat it as a **lightweight weather canopy**, not a heavy “mini roof,” because of the awkward wall geometry, the nearby window, and the services already running through it.

Your basic idea is sound:
- **high end fixed to the main wall**, just below the existing gutter line
- **slight fall away from the house**
- cover the area in front of the little side window / door zone
- keep it shallow and visually tidy

A few things jump out from the photos.

### What looks workable
The easiest version is a **simple mono-pitch canopy** running from the main wall on the left toward the outer edge near the small window side. That would give you a dry slot for:
- 1–2 folded e-bikes or scooters
- charging point on the wall
- maybe a narrow floor rail / anchor point

A **5° fall** is reasonable for many sheet-type roofs, but it is **too shallow for ordinary shingles** unless there is a fully waterproof deck and membrane system underneath. If you want “shingle look,” you’d need to build it more like a proper roof rather than just decorative cladding.

### Best roof types for this
For this situation I’d rank them like this:

**1. EPDM rubber roof on plywood/OSB deck**  
Best if you want it to look neat and “proper.”  
- works well on low pitch
- clean edge trims
- durable
- easiest to gutter properly

**2. Corrugated metal / box profile sheet**  
Most practical and cheapest.  
- light
- easy fall
- sheds water well
- can look decent if done in anthracite/black

**3. Polycarbonate sheet**  
Only if you want light underneath.  
- less visually solid
- can look a bit conservatory-ish
- noisier in rain

**4. Shingles**  
Only if pitch is increased enough and the sub-roof is done properly.  
For your sketch idea, I would not choose shingles first.

### Main design constraint
The big issue is **where the water goes**.

You do not want runoff:
- splashing onto that small window
- pouring onto the passage
- draining back toward the wall
- interfering with the existing downpipe / cables

So the canopy should really have:
- fall **away from the house**
- a **front gutter**
- discharge either into a small downpipe or into the existing drainage path in a controlled way

### Structurally, the safe-looking approach
I’d avoid trying to span too much with just flimsy battens.

A sensible build-up would be:
- wall ledger fixed to masonry on the left/main wall
- outer front beam
- 2–3 rafters or light joists spanning out
- roof deck
- waterproof finish
- gutter on the low side

Because the right side is messy with the small roof, fascia, wiring, and window, I would try to make the canopy **primarily supported from the main wall and front edge**, rather than depending heavily on the little projection roof.

### The thing I’d be cautious about
From the photos, there are already:
- cables clipped along the fascia
- a vertical conduit/downpipe
- existing guttering
- a flue/pipe above
- a small window that could become hemmed in

So I would **not**:
- trap the small window so it can’t open or be maintained
- box in the cables
- fix into the fascia as if it were structural
- create a hidden damp trap between canopy and render

### Best visual proportion
I’d keep it:
- **narrow and deliberate**
- just deep enough for handlebars / scooter stems
- not trying to fully roof the whole recess unless you really need that

That will look more intentional and less like an afterthought.

### Charging / USB point
A protected charging point is sensible, but I’d do:
- **outdoor-rated socket or fused spur**
- inside a proper weatherproof enclosure
- ideally mounted on the wall well above splash zone
- cable route clipped neatly, not dangling

For e-bikes, I’d usually prefer:
- bring battery inside to charge, or
- charge in a more controlled enclosure

That is less about weather and more about battery/fire risk.

### My honest take on your current concept
**Concept: good.**  
**5° pitch: fine if using EPDM or suitable sheet roof.**  
**Shingles at that pitch: not my first choice.**  
**Biggest success factor: drainage + tidy detailing around the existing services.**

### What I would build here
If it were mine, I’d do:
- black/anthracite timber or metal frame
- EPDM on 18 mm OSB3 or plywood
- slim black fascia
- front mini gutter
- side cheeks only if needed for wind-driven rain
- simple bike anchor rail on wall/floor
- lockable slatted front or half-height gate later if wanted

That would suit the house better than trying to make a tiny fake tiled roof.

### One practical warning
Because it’s attached to the house and near doors/windows/drainage, this is one of those jobs where **water management matters more than the carpentry**. A slightly crude frame can still work fine; bad flashing and bad runoff will be annoying very quickly.
