[M4] AI (2026-01-06 15:27):
**Short answer:** *Usually yes — provided the email contains the required details.*  
However, it is **not automatically accepted** just because it confirms “paid in full.”

### What Medicash generally accepts
Medicash will normally accept an **email receipt or invoice** (including one sent from a linked personal or business account) **if it clearly functions as a receipt**, meaning it includes all of the following:

- Your **name**
- **Date** of treatment or purchase
- **Description of the treatment or service**
- **Amount paid**
- **Confirmation that it has been paid** (e.g. “paid in full”)
- **Provider / practitioner name** (and ideally address or contact details)

If the email reads like a proper receipt or invoice confirmation, it is usually treated the same as a PDF or printed receipt when uploaded through the Medicash app or portal.

### What is *not* acceptable
Medicash is likely to **reject** the email if:

- It only says “paid in full” with **no treatment description**
- It does not identify **what the payment was for**
- It lacks the **date of treatment**
- It looks like an informal message rather than a transactional receipt
- It relies on a **bank transaction reference alone**

In those cases, it is treated no differently from a bank statement.

### Practical guidance (to minimise rejection)
If you have the email already:

- Upload it as-is **if it includes the details above**
- If it is missing details, ask the provider to:
  - Re-send it as an **invoice/receipt**, or
  - Add a short confirmation line (e.g. “This payment covers [treatment] on [date]”)

### How Medicash will view this
Medicash assesses **whether the claim evidence, taken as a whole, proves**:

1. The treatment or service took place  
2. The cost of that treatment  
3. That **you have paid** for it  

An **invoice alone** shows (1) and (2).

When both are submitted together, this is generally treated as equivalent to a receipt.

  - Your name
  - Date of treatment
  - Description of treatment
  - Provider/practitioner name
  - Amount charged
- Ensure the email clearly:
  - References the same invoice or treatment
  - States “paid in full” or equivalent wording

- The sender’s address
- The date of the email
- The wording “paid in full”

- The email does not clearly link to the invoice
- The invoice looks like a *pro-forma* rather than a final invoice
