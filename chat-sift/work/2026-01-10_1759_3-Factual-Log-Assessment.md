## Definition: A Log Entry

A **log entry** is **one externally verifiable change** that meets **all** of the conditions below.

If **any** condition is not met, it is **not an entry**.

---

## The Five Conditions (All Must Be True)

### 1. Something **changed**
Not a feeling.  
Not a concern.  
Not a continuation.

Examples of change:
- A message was sent or received
- A decision was communicated
- Access was granted or removed
- A meeting was scheduled, cancelled, or altered
- A document was issued, amended, or withdrawn

If nothing changed, nothing is logged.

---

### 2. The change is **external**
It did not occur only in your head.

Valid:
- Email
- Calendar invite
- System notification
- Verbal instruction that altered a process
- Document delivery

Invalid:
- Realisation
- Suspicion
- Interpretation
- Emotional response
- “I noticed a pattern”

---

### 3. The change can be described **without adjectives**
You must be able to write it using neutral, literal language.

Valid:
- “Meeting scheduled for 14 March”
- “Request declined”
- “Access removed from system X”

Invalid:
- “Unexpected meeting”
- “Dismissive response”
- “Delayed again”
- “Unreasonable decision”

If it needs tone words, it does not belong.

---

### 4. You can point to **evidence**
You can name a thing that exists.

Examples:
- “Email dated 10 Jan 2026”
- “Screenshot IMG_1423”
- “Calendar invite ‘HR Review’”
- “Document ‘Revised Duties v3’”

If you cannot name evidence, do not log it.

---

### 5. It stands **alone**
It is one event, not a situation.

Valid:
- “Email received confirming X”
- “Access removed from system Y”

Invalid:
- “Ongoing lack of response”
- “Pattern of exclusion”
- “Repeated delays”

Patterns are never logged. Only atoms.
