Yes — there are **fast-track / intensive plumbing courses**, but I’d be wary of anything marketed as “become a plumber in 4 weeks.” You can learn useful domestic plumbing skills quickly, but becoming properly employable normally needs **recognised qualifications plus real site experience**.

In the UK, the sensible routes are:

### 1. DIY / basic domestic plumbing crash course  
Good for: fixing taps, toilets, radiators, pipework basics, compression fittings, soldering, waste pipes, fault-finding.

These can be weekend or 1–2 week courses. Useful, but they **do not make you a qualified plumber**.

### 2. Fast-track Level 2 plumbing course  
This is the more serious “career change” route. Look for **City & Guilds Level 2 Plumbing Studies / Plumbing & Domestic Heating**. City & Guilds describes the NVQ Level 2 plumbing and heating qualification as covering the basic domestic heating and plumbing training needed to start work in the industry. citeturn258577search0

Some providers offer intensive routes, often around **6–16 weeks full-time**, while part-time routes can run longer. citeturn258577search7

### 3. NVQ route / on-site portfolio  
A classroom diploma is not the same as being fully occupationally competent. For site/commercial work, you usually want the **NVQ Level 2**, which requires evidence from real work. Some providers state that if you want to work on site or in commercial environments, a Level 2 Plumbing NVQ is normally needed. citeturn258577search19

### 4. Gas is separate  
Plumbing does **not** automatically qualify you to work on gas. For gas, you need recognised gas qualifications and Gas Safe registration. Gas Safe says you must have a recognised qualification for the specific areas of gas work you intend to carry out before applying for registration. citeturn258577search1

So the honest answer is:

**Yes, crash courses exist.**  
**No, a short course alone does not make you a fully fledged plumber.**  
**Best practical route:** do an intensive Level 2 course, then work as a plumber’s mate / trainee while building NVQ evidence.

For you, with an engineering background and practical building experience, you’d probably pick it up quickly. I’d avoid “too-good-to-be-true” courses and look for:

**City & Guilds / LCL / BPEC recognised training**, real workshop time, clear NVQ progression, and no promise that you’ll be “qualified” after a few weeks.
