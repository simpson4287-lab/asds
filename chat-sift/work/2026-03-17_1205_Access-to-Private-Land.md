[M2] AI (2026-03-17 12:05):
Yes — a short, polite letter is probably the best first move.

It gives the owner space to consider it without feeling put on the spot, and it lets you frame the request carefully: respectful, low-pressure, and specific. Then, if they seem open, a brief face-to-face follow-up can help.

What will maximise your chances is not just asking for “access,” but making it feel safe, limited, and easy for them to say yes to.

The strongest approach is:

1. **Keep it humble and neighbourly**
   Introduce yourself as local, explain that since losing your licence you are trying to keep your dogs exercised without using the busier main-road route, and that this quieter route would make a big difference.

2. **Ask for limited permission**
   Do not ask in a broad or entitled way. Ask for something narrow, such as:
   - permission to walk through along a defined route
   - or permission to use the field only for dog walking at sensible times
   - always with dogs under control

3. **Reduce the owner’s perceived risk**
   Reassure them on the points they will care about:
   - you would keep to an agreed route
   - pick up after the dogs
   - keep dogs away from livestock, crops, machinery, or private garden areas
   - keep gates shut
   - not bring other people with you unless agreed
   - stop immediately if they ever withdraw permission

4. **Show you understand it is a favour**
   Make clear you know it is private land and that you are asking, not assuming any right.

5. **Mention the local connection carefully**
   You can say you understand some nearby residents have previously been allowed access, but do not make it sound like a precedent they must follow. Just use it to show your request is not out of the blue.

6. **Make replying easy**
   Include your name, address, phone number, and invite them to ring, text, or knock if they want to discuss it.

A letter is usually better than knocking cold at the door first, because a cold approach can feel intrusive. Best sequence is:

- post a letter
- wait about a week
- if no reply, a very brief polite knock or follow-up note

A few things that would weaken your chances:
- sounding as though the route is already informally established
- overexplaining your whole situation
- mentioning legal rights, fairness, or what others are allowed to do
- asking for permanent unrestricted access
- letting it sound like off-lead dogs near a private residence unless that is expressly permitted

I would pitch it as either:
- **a permissive path / cut-through for dog walking**, or
- **occasional permission to use the field for dog exercise**

The first is often easier for an owner to agree to than full use of the field.
