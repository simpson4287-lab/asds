[M2] AI (2026-06-21 13:54):
Yes — **fold the membrane back over the studs and secure it**. Don’t cut it off and leave the timber bare, unless that timber is definitely inside a dry, protected zone.

For your setup, the better detail is:

1. **Wrap the Tyvek over the top/face of the studs**
   This gives the timber some protection from wind-driven moisture, drips, and condensation in that air-gap area.

2. **Keep it shingled**
   Any upper layer should lap over lower layers so water naturally sheds outward/downward, not behind the membrane.

3. **Secure it mechanically**
   Use stainless staples, clout nails, or screws with washers/strips. Tape alone probably won’t be reliable long term on dusty timber/cement board.

4. **Avoid tight pockets or troughs**
   Don’t fold it in a way that creates a little gutter that can hold water. It should lie reasonably flat and slope/drain outward if anything gets behind it.

5. **Then trap it with a batten/trim if possible**
   Best finish would be membrane folded over, stapled, then held down with a timber batten/cover strip. That stops wind flutter and stops the edge peeling back.

So in plain terms: **wrap and secure, don’t cut it flush.** Bare wood in that cavity/edge zone is the weaker option.

[M6] AI (2026-06-21 13:56):
Ah — **liquid DPM / liquid membrane over that folded Tyvek patch?**

I’d say **no, not as the main approach**.

Liquid membrane over loose/folded Tyvek is likely to be a bit unreliable because the Tyvek can move, crease, flex, and has a textured/non-absorbent surface. The liquid membrane may skin over it, but it may not bond well long term. It could also trap moisture in the fold.

Better detail:

**Fold the Tyvek over the timber, mechanically secure it, then leave it as membrane.**  
Staples/pins plus a batten/cover strip is better than painting liquid DPM over it.

Where liquid membrane *is* useful:

- on the cement board
- over fixed joints
- over timber faces that are exposed and static
- around vulnerable lower edges
- over screw heads/penetrations after they are fixed

Where I’d avoid it:

- over floppy folded membrane
- over loose tape edges
- over anything that may peel, wrinkle, or move
- anywhere it creates a sealed pocket that can hold water

For that exact patch in your photo, I’d do:

1. Fold Tyvek neatly over the stud.
2. Staple or pin it tight.
3. Tape only the awkward edge if needed.
4. Trap with a thin batten if you can.
5. Use liquid membrane on the rigid surrounding board/timber edges, **not heavily over the folded Tyvek itself**.

A small smear/brush over the very edge to stop fraying or flapping is probably fine, but I wouldn’t rely on liquid membrane as the bond or waterproofing over the folded patch.
