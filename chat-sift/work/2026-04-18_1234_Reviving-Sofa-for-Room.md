[M2] AI (2026-04-18 12:34):
Yes, usually salvageable for a spare room, but treat it as a **deep dry-out and decontaminate job**, not just a clean.

Best process:

### 1. Get it somewhere dry and ventilated
A garage with door cracked open, conservatory, or indoors with windows open and a fan/dehumidifier is ideal. Do not leave it damp while you “wait and see” or mildew will keep growing.

### 2. Strip everything removable
Take off:
- cushion covers
- loose cushions
- feet if they’re trapping moisture underneath
- any fabric stapled dust cover underneath if it is soggy or mouldy

Check the underside carefully. That is often the worst area.

### 3. Dry vacuum it first
Use a vacuum with upholstery attachment and go over all surfaces, seams, piping, and underneath. This removes dirt, spores, dead leaves, grit, and stops you rubbing filth deeper in later.

### 4. Inspect honestly before spending time on it
If you find any of these, it may not be worth reviving:
- strong musty smell deep in the frame
- black mould throughout the fabric
- chipboard/frame swelling or crumbling
- springs rusting badly
- cushion foam that feels clammy, brittle, or smells sour

If the **smell is in the frame or foam core**, that is the hardest part to fix.

### 5. Clean the fabric
For the upholstery itself:
- use a fabric/upholstery cleaner, or
- warm water with a small amount of mild detergent

Do not soak it. Work with a lightly damp cloth or soft brush. Clean a small test area first.

For mildew marks, a **white vinegar and water mix** can help on many fabrics, but test first on a hidden patch. Lightly mist or dab, do not saturate.

Avoid over-wetting because the real battle is drying the inner padding.

### 6. Wash removable covers properly
If covers unzip:
- check labels
- usually cool wash / gentle cycle is safest
- air dry fully before refitting

Do not tumble dry unless the label allows it, or they may shrink and become a nightmare to get back on.

### 7. Deal with the foam cushions separately
This is the key bit.

If the cushion inserts are damp:
- stand them on edge
- press towels into them to pull moisture out
- use fan + dehumidifier
- rotate them regularly

If they still smell musty after fully drying, they may need replacing. Foam holds odour and spores very well.

### 8. Treat the frame and underside
If the wooden frame underneath is accessible:
- wipe hard surfaces with a mild disinfecting cleaner or diluted vinegar
- let it dry thoroughly

If the bottom dust cover fabric is mouldy, torn, or smells bad, remove and replace it later with cheap black cambric dust cover fabric.

### 9. Deodorise after it is fully dry
Only once dry:
- bicarbonate of soda over fabric and cushions
- leave for several hours
- vacuum off

This helps with stale smell, but it does not replace drying and cleaning.

### 10. Dry it aggressively
This is the make-or-break step:
- fan on it continuously
- dehumidifier in the room if possible
- lift it off the floor slightly for airflow underneath
- keep turning cushions

Give it at least a day or two of proper drying if it got damp over winter.

---

## Practical triage
If it’s only:
- dusty
- slightly musty
- surface mildewed
- generally a bit tired

then it is probably worth saving.

If it has:
- deep mould smell
- wet or stained foam cores
- internal rot
- persistent mildew returning after drying

then for a bedroom I would be cautious. You do not want a spare room smelling faintly fungal forever.

---

## Best realistic approach
For a good result:
1. Vacuum
2. Remove and wash covers
3. Clean upholstery lightly
4. Dry cushions and frame thoroughly with fan/dehumidifier
5. Bicarb and vacuum
6. Reassess smell after 48 hours dry
