**Reasoning:**

**What I would still do (low effort, low risk):**
1. **Leave the wrap off for a few hours once rain stops**, if conditions are dry and breezy. This is not “airing” in the corrective sense—just equalising surface humidity.
2. **Re-cover loosely**, not shrink-tight:
   - Cover the top fully.
   - Leave the sides open or tented so moisture cannot be trapped.
3. **Check end grain tomorrow**:
   - If ends are still pale and dry to the touch, you’re done.
   - If ends feel cool/damp while faces don’t, give it half a day uncovered.

**When airing *would* be required (not your case):**
- Timber left wrapped while visibly wet
- Darkened end grain or patchy staining
- Condensation inside the wrap
- Soft raised fibres on planed faces

Given the stack condition shown, **this is storage-safe timber**, not wet timber. Re-wrap sensibly and proceed as planned.

### How to stack the rest safely

**1. Location**
- Inside the house is fine **short-term**.
- Choose the **coolest, driest room** available.
- Avoid direct heat sources (radiators, UFH zones, fires).

**2. Stack method (important)**
- Keep boards **flat and fully supported**.
- Use **spacers/battens** every ~600–800 mm if the stack is more than ~300 mm high.
- Align ends flush to avoid banana-ing.
- Weight the top of the stack (a few heavy boards or sheets).

**3. Order of stacking**
- Stack **longest boards at the bottom**.
- Keep similar lengths together.
- Do not lean boards vertically unless absolutely unavoidable (last resort only).

**4. Wrapping**
- Indoors: **do not fully wrap**.
- If dust protection is needed, drape loosely with a sheet or breathable cover.
- Outdoors: keep top covered, sides open.

**5. Moisture equalisation**
- If indoor air is warmer than outside, leave the indoor stack **uncovered for 24–48 h**, then keep it stacked and weighted.
- This avoids one-sided drying, which causes cupping.

### Key risk to avoid
The only real danger here is **rapid uneven drying**, not wetness. Fast drying on one face or one end is what causes twist and cup.
