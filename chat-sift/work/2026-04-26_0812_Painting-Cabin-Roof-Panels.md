[M2] AI (2026-04-26 08:12):
Yes — **you can paint the cabin roof panels with DPM**, but I’d treat it as a **temporary protection / belt-and-braces primer**, not as the main roof waterproofing layer.

For cabin roof boards/panels, the proper waterproofing still wants to be:

**timber roof deck → roofing membrane/felt/underlay → shingles/felt/EPDM/roof covering**

### Where DPM paint makes sense

DPM/bitumen paint is useful on roof panels if:

- the panels may sit exposed briefly before the final roof covering goes on
- you want extra protection on edges, joints, screw holes, and vulnerable cuts
- the underside/edges are at risk of splash, damp air, or trapped moisture during assembly
- you are using OSB/ply and want to slow moisture uptake before covering

I’d especially coat:

- cut edges
- board ends
- panel joints
- around screw positions
- any underside edge near eaves or drip lines

### Main caution

Don’t create a roof sandwich that traps water.

If you paint the **top face** of the panels with DPM, then put a **breather membrane** over it, then shingles, it is probably okay as a temporary/weather-resisting layer, but the DPM layer itself is not breathable. Any water that gets into the timber from below or from edges will dry more slowly.

So I’d avoid fully sealing **both faces** unless there is a specific reason. Better approach:

**Top face:** light/moderate DPM coat, mainly for temporary protection  
**Edges:** heavy DPM coat  
**Underside:** leave mostly breathable, unless it is an exposed external edge or high-risk area

### Best practical approach

1. **Dry panels first** — don’t paint DPM over damp timber/OSB.
2. **Seal cut edges well** — this matters more than the broad face.
3. **Use one or two thin coats**, not a thick tarry layer that stays soft.
4. Let it cure properly before fitting membrane/shingles.
5. Once fitted, still use proper roof membrane and a proper drip/eaves detail.

### My judgement

For your cabin project, yes, I’d be comfortable using DPM paint on the roof panels **as added temporary protection**, especially on edges and joints. I would not rely on it as the actual roof system, and I would not bury wet timber under it.

[M4] AI (2026-04-26 08:13):
Only **a little bit**, and I wouldn’t rely on that as meaningful “breathing”.

With tongue-and-groove roof boards, the joints/grooves are not airtight in the way a sheet good is, but once you add:

**DPM paint + membrane/felt + shingles**

…the roof deck becomes mostly **vapour-closed from above**. The tongue/groove gaps may allow tiny local movement, but they won’t provide proper drying capacity if moisture is trapped in the timber.

So the safer thinking is:

**The roof should dry inward/downward, not upward through the covering.**

That means:

- **Do not DPM both sides** of the roof boards.
- If using DPM, favour **cut edges, board ends, exposed eaves, and maybe a light coat on the top face**.
- Leave the **underside/internal face unsealed or only breathable-painted**.
- Make sure the roof covering above has good **drip/eaves detail**, so water is shed rather than sitting at board edges.
- Fit the final membrane/shingles as soon as practical after assembly.

For your cabin, I’d probably do this:

**Top face:** light DPM coat or skip broad-face coating if roof covering is going on soon.  
**Edges/ends:** definitely seal with DPM.  
**Underside:** leave bare timber or use breathable timber treatment only.  
**Final roof:** membrane/felt/shingles does the waterproofing.

So yes, the grooves help a tiny bit, but **not enough to treat a DPM-painted roof as breathable**.

[M6] AI (2026-04-26 08:15):
Yes — that plan is broadly sensible **as a temporary holding strategy**, provided you don’t confuse the DPM-painted roof deck with the final roof.

I’d do it like this:

**Now / temporary phase**

Paint the **external/top faces** with DPM/bitumen paint, especially:

- tongue/groove edges if accessible before fitting
- cut ends
- eaves edges
- board ends
- screw holes after fixing, if exposed
- any knots/splits/gaps that might wick water

Then let it weather for the couple of months while you wait to do membrane and shingles.

**Later / final phase**

Before fitting membrane and shingles:

1. Brush/wash off dirt, algae, dust, chalkiness, pollen, loose bitumen, etc.
2. Make sure the roof boards are dry.
3. Check for cupping, gaps, lifted tongues, or soft areas.
4. Touch up any damaged DPM areas.
5. Fit your semi-breathable membrane/underlay.
6. Fit shingles/final covering with proper eaves/drip detail.

The important bit: **leave the internal underside unsealed** if possible. That gives the timber somewhere to dry towards. The tongue/groove gaps might give tiny drying paths, but the real drying path will be inward/downward through the timber and internal air.

One caution: if it is going to sit for **two months**, DPM paint alone will help but is not a perfect roof covering. If water can pond, sit in grooves, or get driven into joints, it may still creep in. A better temporary version would be:

**DPM-painted roof boards + cheap temporary tarp / temporary roofing felt / temporary membrane**, weighted or fixed so it cannot flap.

So my judgement would be:

**DPM on the external faces for a couple of months = acceptable.**  
**DPM as the only weatherproofing for a couple of wet months = risky but probably manageable if the roof pitch sheds water and the edges are well sealed.**  
**DPM plus temporary cover = much safer.**
