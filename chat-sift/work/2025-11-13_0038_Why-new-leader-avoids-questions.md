[M1] ME (2025-11-13 00:38):
Someone who replaced me in my role as a team leader never asks me key questions, why?

[M2] AI (2025-11-13 00:38):
There are **several plausible reasons**, and most of them have *nothing to do with you personally* — they’re usually about **their own psychology, confidence, or dynamics in the team**.

Here are the **most common explanations**, broken down clearly:

---

# ✅ **1. They’re trying to prove independence**  
A new team leader often feels pressure to show they can handle things without leaning on their predecessor.  
This can lead to:

- Avoiding asking questions so they don’t look unprepared  
- Wanting to “own” the role and make their mark  
- Fear that seeking your help undermines their authority

This is probably the **number-one reason** you’re seeing.

---

# ✅ **2. They feel competitive or threatened**  
Not in a hostile way — but psychologically:

- You previously held the role  
- You know the systems, history, and context  
- They may worry that asking you things confirms you’re more competent  
- They might fear comparisons

So they stay silent to maintain a sense of control or status.

---

# ✅ **3. They’re unaware of what they *don’t* know**  
Some people don’t realise when they need information until something goes wrong.  
This happens a lot in engineering and technical leadership roles:

- They “don’t know what they don’t know”  
- They assume they understand more than they do  
- Their blind spots prevent them from even forming the right questions

This one is extremely common in new managers.

---

# ✅ **4. They’re getting information elsewhere**  
Some new leaders prefer:

- Asking peers instead of the person who used to run the team  
- Asking their boss  
- Figuring it out alone  
- Following documentation or making assumptions

This can be a way to avoid the feeling of “relying on the old leader”.

---

# ✅ **5. They feel awkward or intimidated**  
Not necessarily because of anything you’ve done — but because:

- You did the job before them  
- You know all the history  
- They may respect your expertise too much  
- They might worry you will judge their decisions

So they keep their distance to avoid vulnerability.

---

# ✅ **6. They’re trying to avoid appearing incompetent**  
A *lot* of new leaders think:  
“If I ask too many questions, people will think I’m not capable.”

So they over-correct and ask none.

---

# ✅ **7. They could have an avoidant communication style**  
Some people simply don’t open up unless they hit a problem.  
They work independently, even when it’s inefficient.

This has nothing to do with you — it’s their interpersonal style.

---
