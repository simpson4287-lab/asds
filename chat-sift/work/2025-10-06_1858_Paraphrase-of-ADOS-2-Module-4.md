### **Overview of Module 4**

Module 4 of the ADOS-2 is intended for **adolescents and adults who use fluent speech**. It is a **semi-structured, standardized assessment** that observes **communication**, **reciprocal social interaction**, and **restricted or repetitive behaviors** linked to Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).

Unlike earlier modules, which are more play-based, Module 4 involves **discussion-oriented and abstract tasks**, reflecting the social and cognitive demands of adult life. Topics and activities are age-appropriate, focusing on **relationships, emotions, personal experiences**, and **future plans** rather than toys or play.

---

### **Setting up the Assessment Environment**

The room should be arranged to promote **comfortable face-to-face communication** without distractions.  
Key points include:

- **Two chairs and a table** positioned to facilitate natural conversation.
- Materials such as **books, pictures, and puzzles** should be within the examiner’s reach but out of the participant’s direct control until required.
- Avoid barriers between examiner and participant.
- Lighting should allow clear observation of **facial expressions** and **eye contact**.

The examiner should minimize unnecessary movement or speech so that the participant’s **spontaneous behavior** can be observed.

---

### **Beginning the Session**

At the start, the examiner engages in **light conversation** to reduce anxiety and establish rapport.  
The goal is to observe:

- **Ease in social interaction**  
- **Spontaneity of communication**  
- **Emotional responsiveness**  

Unlike modules for younger individuals, **rapport-building is not scored**, but it sets the tone for subsequent interactions.

---

### **Administration During Module 4**

The examiner must **balance flexibility with standardization** — allowing natural dialogue while following specific prompts.  
The participant is encouraged to **elaborate freely**, and the examiner must observe:

- How information is conveyed (tone, fluency, nonverbal cues)
- How the person **responds to social cues** (facial expressions, questions, pauses)
- The presence of **unusual speech patterns**, restricted interests, or rigid thinking.

---

### **Core Activities of Module 4**

Each activity is designed to elicit social-communicative behaviors under lightly structured conditions.  
Below are detailed paraphrased descriptions of each task.

---

#### **1. Construction Task (Optional)**

The examiner presents puzzle pieces and asks the participant to replicate a model.  
Purpose: Not to assess visuospatial skill, but to **see whether the person requests help**, maintains attention, and uses **appropriate social overtures** when facing difficulty.  
The examiner notes **eye contact**, **requests**, and **reactions to challenge**.

---

#### **2. Story from a Book**

The participant is asked to **look at a picture book** and **tell the story**.  
This activity elicits **narrative skills**, **emotional interpretation**, and **perspective-taking**.  
The examiner observes:

- Whether the story is logical and coherent  
- Use of emotional and mental-state language (e.g., “He felt sad because...”)  
- Gestures, prosody, and engagement

---

#### **3. Description of a Picture (Optional)**

A picture depicting people in social situations is shown.  
The participant is invited to describe it, giving insight into their **social perception**, **empathy**, and **interpretive ability**.  
The examiner watches for **detail vs. big-picture focus**, **literal vs. inferential thinking**, and **interest in people’s emotions**.

---

#### **4. Conversation and Reporting of Events**

A semi-structured discussion about **recent life events**, such as work, studies, or hobbies.  
It assesses **social reciprocity** — whether the participant **initiates**, **sustains**, and **balances** conversation.  
The examiner tracks how naturally the participant responds to **social prompts** and whether speech appears **pedantic**, **formal**, or overly **self-focused**.

---

#### **5. Discussion about Emotions**

The examiner asks about **emotional experiences**, such as situations that make the person happy, angry, or embarrassed.  
This probes **emotional insight** and **expression of empathy**.  
The participant’s ability to **recognize and label emotions** in self and others is key.

---

#### **6. Relationships and Friendships**

Questions explore **friendships, romantic relationships, and marriage** — their meaning, importance, and challenges.  
This helps identify whether the individual understands **social reciprocity**, **boundaries**, and **emotional connection**.  
Examiners look for **abstract understanding** versus factual or detached descriptions.

---

#### **7. Imagination and Creativity**

Participants are asked to **invent a story or scenario**, allowing observation of **creativity**, **flexibility**, and **use of imagination**.  
This activity can reveal **restricted thinking** or **literal interpretation**, typical of ASD profiles.

### **Scoring and Interpretation**

After administration, behaviors are coded into numerical values using the **ADOS-2 algorithm** for Module 4.  
Scores are grouped into:

1. **Communication (C)**
2. **Reciprocal Social Interaction (ISR)**
3. **Restricted and Repetitive Behaviors (RRB)**  
   → Together forming **Total (C + ISR)**

Each domain has specific cut-off points distinguishing between **autism**, **autism spectrum**, and **non-spectrum profiles**.
