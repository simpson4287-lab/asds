[M2] AI (2026-06-15 17:17):
I **wouldn’t use a PVA/water wash before DPM** unless the DPM manufacturer specifically says it’s compatible.

PVA can **re-emulsify with moisture**, can form a weak film, and may actually make the DPM bond worse. On cement board, I’d treat PVA as a risky bodge rather than a primer.

What’s probably happened is:

1. **Cement board dust** is stopping the tape bonding.
2. The **alcohol gel may have left residue** — many gels contain thickeners/moisturisers, not just alcohol.
3. Duck/Gorilla tape adhesive often doesn’t love **porous, dusty, alkaline boards**, especially if there is dampness or cold.

Best route before liquid DPM:

1. **Scrape/peel off failing tape.**
2. Wash the board with **clean water only**, ideally with a stiff brush/sponge.
3. Let it dry properly.
4. Vacuum or wipe off dust.
5. Use either:
   - the **manufacturer’s primer for the DPM**, best option, or
   - a proper **SBR/acrylic tile/DPM primer**, if compatible.
6. Then apply the liquid DPM.

For joints/gaps, don’t rely on duck tape as the main waterproofing. Better is usually:

- **alkali-resistant scrim tape** over board joints,
- embedded into the first coat of DPM or tile adhesive,
- then DPM over the top.

If the tape is only there temporarily to hold overlaps/membrane while sandwiched behind timber, fine. But if it’s meant to be part of the waterproof layer, I’d replace that idea with proper scrim/tanking tape or DPM-compatible jointing tape.

So: **no PVA**, clean off the gel residue, dry it, then use a compatible primer or go straight on if the DPM says primer isn’t needed.

   Get loose dust off the cement board and off the cured DPM.

2. **Use small pieces of tape, not long strips**
   Shorter pieces have less tendency to pull themselves back up.

3. **Press it down hard**
   Rub it down with the back of a screwdriver handle, roller, scrap timber edge, or similar. Work from the middle outwards.

4. **Paint over it straight away**
   Don’t leave it overnight loose. Get the diluted DPM/binding coat over it while it’s freshly stuck.

5. **Repeat only where needed**
   Once dry, check for lifted corners. If any corners lift, cut them off rather than trying to bury a loose flap.

For this air-gap situation, that’s fine. I’d still avoid relying on duck tape as a structural or waterproof jointing system, but as a temporary bridging/reinforcing aid that gets buried under the binding coat, it’s acceptable.
