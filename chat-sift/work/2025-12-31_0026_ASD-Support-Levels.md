[M2] AI (2025-12-31 00:26):
Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is commonly described using **three support levels** in clinical frameworks (e.g., DSM-5). In a nutshell, they describe **how much day-to-day support a person typically needs**, not intelligence, insight, or worth.

---

### **Level 1 – Requiring Support**
Often described as “high-functioning,” though that label can be misleading.

- Difficulties with social communication (e.g., back-and-forth conversation, reading social cues)
- Inflexibility, sensory sensitivities, or executive-function strain
- Can live independently, work, and mask traits—but at significant cognitive/emotional cost
- Burnout, anxiety, or depression are common when unsupported

**Key idea:** Looks “mostly fine” from the outside; effort is invisible but constant.

---

### **Level 2 – Requiring Substantial Support**
More visible and impactful challenges.

- Marked difficulty with verbal and non-verbal communication
- Limited social initiation and response
- Repetitive behaviours, rigidity, or sensory issues that interfere with daily life
- Needs regular support to function across work, education, or home settings

**Key idea:** Can function, but not consistently or independently without structured support.

---

### **Level 3 – Requiring Very Substantial Support**
The highest support needs.

- Severe communication challenges (may be minimally verbal or non-verbal)
- Extreme difficulty with change, transitions, or sensory input
- Requires significant assistance with daily living and safety
- Support is typically continuous and lifelong

**Key idea:** Cannot function safely or meaningfully without intensive support.

---

### **Critical clarification**
- These **levels are not fixed**—they can change with environment, stress, health, trauma, or support quality.
- They describe **support needs**, not insight, intelligence, self-awareness, or emotional depth.
- Many adults—especially those with long histories of masking, anxiety, or substance use—are **under-classified** at Level 1 despite high internal impairment.

[M8] AI (2025-12-31 00:31):
**Masking (in ASD):**  
The conscious or unconscious process of **suppressing autistic traits** and **performing socially expected behaviours** in order to fit in, avoid negative judgement, or function in non-accommodating environments.

**In practice, this can include:**
- Forcing eye contact, rehearsing conversation, copying social scripts  
- Hiding sensory distress or regulating behaviours  
- Over-monitoring tone, facial expression, posture, and responses  

**Cost:** masking is cognitively and emotionally demanding and, over time, is strongly associated with **exhaustion, anxiety, depression, burnout, and identity confusion**.

**Key point:** masking improves outward appearance of “functioning” but does **not** reduce internal difficulty.
