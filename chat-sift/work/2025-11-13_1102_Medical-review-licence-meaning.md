A **“medical review licence”** usually refers to when the DVLA (in the UK) issues or renews your driving licence **under medical review**.  

Here’s what it typically means:

### **1. Your licence is valid, but monitored**
You *can legally drive*, but the DVLA wants to keep track of a medical condition that **could affect driving safety** (e.g., neurological conditions, mental health conditions, diabetes, cardiac issues, ADHD/ASD meds, etc.).

### **2. The licence is usually short-term**
Instead of the normal 10-year licence, you might get a licence that lasts:
- **1 year**
- **3 years**
- **5 years**

The exact period depends on the condition and stability.

### **3. You will be reviewed again**
Before the licence expires, the DVLA will send forms asking for:
- Updated medical information  
- GP/consultant reports  
- Possibly a driving assessment, depending on the condition  

They then decide whether to:
- Renew again for another short term  
- Return you to a normal 10-year licence  
- Or, in rare cases, not renew

### **4. It does *not* mean you are unsafe**
It simply means the DVLA wants **periodic reassessment**, not that you’ve done anything wrong or that you’re considered unfit.

### **5. Insurance considerations**
You must still:
- Inform insurance of the relevant medical condition  
- Not necessarily that it’s a “medical review licence”  
Insurers generally don’t penalise medical review licences unless the underlying condition affects risk.
