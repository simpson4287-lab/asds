[M4] AI (2026-04-19 10:20):
That’s actually a pretty good base for it.

Those golf trolleys are built to carry a tall, fairly heavy load over uneven ground, so for a **stacked toolbox carrier** they can work well if you sort out three things:

**1. A solid base platform**
The current frame is meant for a golf bag, not flat boxes. You will want a rigid platform fixed low down between the main rails.
Good options:
- 12–18 mm plywood, sealed/painted
- aluminium sheet over a timber frame
- phenolic ply if you want it tougher

Best approach is a **lower deck** sitting just above the axle line so the weight stays low.

**2. Retention**
Toolboxes will want to slide or tip, especially when kerbs or ramps are involved.
Use:
- side lips or angle aluminium on the platform edges
- ratchet straps or cam straps
- maybe a vertical back plate so boxes can lean safely against something

If you plan multiple boxes stacked, I would definitely add **at least one proper strap point high up**.

**3. Balance**
A golf trolley is happiest with load centred over the wheels, not hanging far forward or high up.
So:
- heaviest box at the bottom
- keep the stack close to the axle/wheel line
- avoid too much weight high up near the handle

Otherwise it will feel tippy and nasty to manoeuvre.

A sensible conversion would be:

- **bottom platform** for the main heavy toolbox
- **upright backboard** fixed to the handle/frame
- **2 strap points**
- maybe a **small upper shelf** for lighter items only

Watch-outs:
- the plastic joints and folding pivots may become the weak point
- if the wheels are hard plastic, they may be noisy and harsh with tools
- narrow track width means tall stacks can become unstable
- if you overload it, the handle joints may flex

A good design rule is to treat it as a **medium-duty mobile carrier**, not a van sack truck. Fine for a few boxes and kit, less ideal for extreme loads.

I’d be inclined to make it like this:
- one plywood deck low down
- one plywood or aluminium back panel
- U-bolts/P-clips to fix into the frame without crushing tubes
- black webbing straps with quick-release buckles
- maybe a hook or tray for drill, charger, fixings, tape, etc.

If you send a couple more photos from the side and front, plus the toolboxes you want to carry, I can sketch out the best layout and where to mount the deck and backboard.
