Here’s a breakdown written in clear solicitor-style English:  

---

## ⚖️ **1. Driving vs. Being “In Charge” – The Core Distinction**

### **Driving Under the Influence (s.4(1) Road Traffic Act 1988)**
You are charged with *“driving or attempting to drive a motor vehicle on a road or other public place while unfit through drink or drugs.”*

**To convict**, the prosecution must prove **beyond reasonable doubt** that:
- You **drove or attempted to drive** the vehicle, and  
- You were **unfit to drive** through drink or drugs at that time.  

**Key point:**  
They must have *direct or clear circumstantial evidence of driving* — for example, CCTV of you moving the vehicle, witness evidence of driving, or mechanical signs (engine warm, gear in drive, etc.) that clearly link you to a recent drive.

---

### **Being in Charge While Unfit (s.4(2) Road Traffic Act 1988)**
This is a *lesser but still serious* offence. You are *“in charge of a motor vehicle on a road or public place while unfit through drink or drugs.”*

**To convict**, the prosecution must show that:
- You were **in control or capable of controlling** the vehicle (keys present, sitting in driver’s seat, or nearby), and  
- You were **unfit through drink or drugs** at that time.  

**However**, you have a **statutory defence**:  
If you can demonstrate that **there was no likelihood of you driving while unfit**, you *cannot* be convicted.

That burden of proof is on you, but only on the **balance of probabilities** (i.e. “more likely than not”), not beyond reasonable doubt.  

---

## ⚖️ **2. Penalty Difference**

| Offence | Maximum Penalty | Disqualification | Typical Conviction Impact |
|----------|------------------|------------------|----------------------------|
| **Driving under the influence** | Up to **6 months imprisonment**, **unlimited fine**, and **mandatory driving ban** (minimum 12 months) | **Mandatory** | Treated as a serious criminal offence with strong road safety implications |
| **In charge while unfit** | Up to **3 months imprisonment**, **fine up to £2,500** | **Discretionary** – court may impose 10 points or short ban | Often treated as lower-level or borderline control offence if no evidence of driving |

---

## ⚖️ **3. How Courts Interpret “In Charge”**

Courts usually consider you *“in charge”* if:
- You are **in or near the vehicle**,  
- You have **the keys**, or  
- There is a **real possibility** that you *might* drive.  

But they will also weigh:
- Whether the **engine was running**,  
- Whether you **intended to drive soon**,  
- Whether you were **asleep, seeking safety, or waiting for assistance**,  
- Your **degree of impairment**, and  
- **Time elapsed** since any alleged driving.
