## 1. Treat Entries as Data, Not Venting

Unstructured emotional dumping is low-yield for pattern recognition. Instead, log entries in **repeatable fields** so the system (and you) can compare across time.

**Recommended minimal schema (5–7 minutes):**
- **Date / time**
- **Context** (where you were, what you were doing)
- **Trigger** (what initiated the thought or reaction)
- **Internal response** (thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations)
- **Behavioural response** (what you did or avoided)
- **After-effect** (hours later: mood, clarity, fatigue, relief)
- **Open question** (optional)

Consistency matters more than depth.

---

## 2. Separate *Logging* From *Analysis*

Do **not** ask for insight every time you write. That creates noise and confirmation bias.

**Two-phase approach:**
- **Phase A – Capture**  
  “Log this without analysis.”
- **Phase B – Review (weekly / bi-weekly)**  
  “Analyse the last 7 entries for recurring themes, triggers, or distortions.”

This mirrors clinical and research practice and dramatically improves signal quality.

---

## 3. Use Explicit Review Prompts

When reviewing, be precise about *what kind* of pattern you want identified. Vague prompts yield vague insights.

High-value prompts include:
- “Identify repeated emotional triggers across these entries.”
- “What assumptions appear repeatedly but are never tested?”
- “Where does behaviour contradict stated values?”
- “Which situations reliably precede fatigue or shutdown?”
- “Flag language that indicates threat-response vs reflection.”

This turns ChatGPT into a **pattern classifier**, not a therapist.

---

## 4. Maintain a Stable Vocabulary

Patterns only emerge if terms are consistent.

For example, decide once:
- “Overwhelm” vs “fatigue”
- “Avoidance” vs “deliberate delay”
- “Irritation” vs “anger”

Then **reuse the same words**. This dramatically improves longitudinal insight.

---

## 5. Periodic Meta-Summaries (Critical)

Every 4–6 weeks, request a **meta-summary**:

> “Summarise the last month into:
> - dominant themes  
> - recurring triggers  
> - stable strengths  
> - unresolved loops  
> - questions worth exploring next month”

This creates a **memory spine** rather than isolated entries.

---

## 6. Externalise Memory, Not Identity

Important principle:  
You are **not** journaling to define who you are — you are journaling to **offload memory so you can think more clearly**.

Treat outputs as:
- hypotheses, not truths  
- observations, not diagnoses  
- drafts, not conclusions  

This prevents narrative lock-in.

## 3. What Actually Happens When You Reference an Attachment

When you explicitly reference an attachment:
- It is treated as **authoritative context**
- Analysis is constrained by it
- Contradictions are surfaced
- Language tends to align with its framing

If you do not reference it, none of that happens.

So precision matters:
> “Analyse the last two weeks **against Attachment A**.”

---

## 4. Attachment Governance (This Is Critical)

### Version discipline
- Never overwrite an attachment silently
- If it changes, attach a new version
- State explicitly:  
  “This supersedes the previous version.”

### Attachment roles (recommended)
You should mentally classify each attachment as:
- **Baseline**
- **Evidence**
- **Output**
- **Archive**

Do not mix roles.

## 5. Where Attachments Fit in Your Two-Project Model

### Project: Personal Pattern Log
Attachments should be rare and stable:
- Monthly summaries
- Longitudinal overview
- Vocabulary definitions

Most content stays conversational.

---

### Project: ASD Trait Management Log
Attachments are central:
- Trait profiles
- Formal summaries
- Assessment prep documents
- Communication artefacts

This project should be **attachment-heavy but update-light**.

## 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Attaching raw journals “for context”
- Re-attaching the same evolving document repeatedly
- Expecting attachments to replace summaries
- Using attachments instead of review chats
- Treating attachments as memory rather than reference

Attachments **support reasoning**; they do not *become* it.

---

## 7. The Right Mental Model

Think of attachments as:
- **Exhibits in a case file**
- **Appendices to a report**
- **Pinned reference points**

Chats do the thinking.  
Attachments keep the thinking honest.

## Bottom Line

- They work best as **stable baselines and curated evidence**
- They are essential for **formal, trait-management work**
