Good question — they look identical, but **Thunderbolt 3** and **USB-C** aren’t the same thing. Here’s a clear breakdown:

---

### 🔌 **1. The Connector**
- **Both use the same physical port:** the small, reversible **USB-C** connector.  
- So visually, they’re identical — but what’s *inside* the port (and how fast it is) can differ.

---

### ⚡️ **2. Data Speed**
| Feature | **USB-C (USB 3.1/3.2)** | **Thunderbolt 3** |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| Max data speed | 5 Gbps (USB 3.1 Gen 1) or 10 Gbps (Gen 2) | **40 Gbps** |
| PCIe support | ❌ No | ✅ Yes (for external GPUs, SSDs, docks) |
| DisplayPort support | ✅ Often 1 monitor (4K) | ✅ Up to 2× 4K or 1× 5K monitor |

---

### ⚙️ **3. Power Delivery**
Both can **charge devices** via **USB Power Delivery (PD)**:
- Up to **100 W** on both (depending on cable/device).
- But **Thunderbolt 3 devices often guarantee higher power throughput and more reliable charging**.

---

### 🖥 **4. Display & Expansion**
- **USB-C**: May carry DisplayPort signal (depends on laptop/device).  
- **Thunderbolt 3**: Always supports DisplayPort 1.2 natively, and can connect to powerful docks, eGPUs, and multiple displays.

---

### 🧠 **5. Compatibility**
- A **Thunderbolt 3 port** is **always compatible with USB-C devices**.  
- A **USB-C port** is **not necessarily Thunderbolt-compatible** — it depends on the internal controller.

Example:
- Plug a USB drive into a Thunderbolt 3 port → works fine.  
- Plug a Thunderbolt 3 dock into a plain USB-C port → likely won’t work (or only partially).

---

### 💻 **6. How to Tell Which You Have**
Look for the **lightning bolt symbol ⚡️** beside the port — that marks **Thunderbolt 3**.  
If you only see a **USB trident symbol (⎓ or SS)**, it’s **USB-C** only.

---

**In short:**  
> **USB-C** = the shape of the plug.  
> **Thunderbolt 3** = a *supercharged protocol* that uses the same plug but adds massive bandwidth and versatility.
