Possibly, but not in a reliable or well-supported way.

What the evidence suggests is:
Caffeine may help some aspects of creative performance, especially alertness and convergent problem-solving. In one placebo-controlled study, 200 mg caffeine improved problem-solving, but it did **not** improve divergent thinking, which is the “idea generation” side people usually mean by creativity. citeturn335377search0turn335377search22

Melatonin is different. It is mainly a sleep/circadian hormone. Research does not support the idea that it directly boosts moment-to-moment creative thinking; older human work found no immediate direct improvement in cortical information processing, and newer studies mostly show benefit when melatonin helps sleep or sleep-related cognitive impairment rather than creativity itself. citeturn563418search3turn563418search4turn563418search0

Putting them together is not a good “creativity stack.” They tend to work against each other: NHS guidance says caffeine has the opposite effect of melatonin and can stop it working properly. citeturn335377search1turn335377search3

So in practice:
You might feel a strange mix of “calm but stimulated,” but that does not mean better creative output.
More often, the combo is likely to give inconsistent effects, such as jitteriness, sleepiness, brain fog, or disrupted sleep timing, especially if taken later in the day. Those effects can hurt creativity overall. citeturn335377search1turn335377search22

A fair summary:
Caffeine alone can sometimes help focused problem-solving.
Melatonin is more likely to help creativity indirectly only if better sleep improves your thinking the next day.
Taking them together is unlikely to be a dependable way to become more creative. citeturn335377search0turn563418search4turn335377search1
