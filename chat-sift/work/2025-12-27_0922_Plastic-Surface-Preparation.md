### Recommended approach (best practice)

**1. Light keying is advisable**
- Use **fine abrasive only**: ~600–1000 grit wet-and-dry.
- Aim to **break the gloss**, not to create visible scratches.
- A uniform, matte finish is ideal.
- Do **not** gouge or create directional grooves.

**2. Clean thoroughly after keying**
- Degrease with **isopropyl alcohol (IPA)**.
- Avoid white spirit or household cleaners—they can leave residues.
- Allow to fully dry before bonding.

**3. Tape choice matters**
- If you are using a **high-performance acrylic foam tape** (e.g. VHB-style):
  - Light keying + IPA cleaning gives the best long-term bond.
- If using **cheap foam or generic double-sided tape**:
  - Keying helps, but ultimate strength may still be limited.
  - Consider upgrading tape if accuracy and permanence matter.

### Plastic-specific notes (important)
- **Acrylic / Perspex / Polycarbonate**:  
  Light keying is strongly recommended. These plastics are smooth and benefit greatly.
- **ABS / PVC**:  
  Keying helps, but less critical than acrylic.
- **Polypropylene / Polyethylene (PP / PE)**:  
  Keying alone often does **not** help much—these are low-surface-energy plastics. Adhesion can still fail unless a primer or specialist tape is used.

### Steel rule side
- If the steel rule is polished:
  - Lightly key the **back of the rule as well** (same grit).
  - Degrease thoroughly.
  - This can double bond reliability.

### Clamping / pressure
- After positioning:
  - Apply **firm, even pressure** along the full length.
  - Ideally leave undisturbed for **12–24 hours** for full bond strength.

### Bottom line
✔ Yes, lightly key the plastic  
✔ Clean with IPA  
✔ Use fine grit only  
✔ Apply firm pressure and allow cure time
