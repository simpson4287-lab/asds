A calmer, more senior version could read like this:

---

### Re – Communication Competency

This is the second consecutive year in which I have received feedback against the communication competency. However, I have found it difficult to identify clear objectives, examples, or developmental guidance that would help me understand specifically how improvement is expected to be demonstrated.

For 2025, I understand that some of the feedback may relate to informal Teams messages sent during a particularly difficult period involving significant workload pressure alongside developing ill health. Looking back, I recognise that some of those communications were not reflective of how I would ordinarily present myself professionally.

That said, I believe those interactions were more representative of the circumstances and pressures I was experiencing at the time, rather than a broader or persistent issue with my communication capability. I also note that no concerns were formally raised with me during the year prior to the appraisal outcome being reached.

For that reason, I found it difficult that these isolated examples appear to have materially influenced the overall competency assessment without fuller consideration of the wider context in which they occurred.

I would appreciate clearer clarification regarding:
- the evidence used,
- how the competency threshold was assessed,
- and what specific, measurable improvements would constitute meeting expectations going forward.

---

That version does a few important things strategically:

- accepts some responsibility without self-incrimination,
- frames the issue as contextual rather than character-based,
- introduces the absence of prior feedback,
- asks for objective criteria,
- sounds professional and composed,
- avoids sounding accusatory or emotional.

I would also strongly recommend avoiding wording like:
- “traumatically unwell”
- “insensitive”
- “erratic”
- “cloud this competency”
because those phrases will emotionally colour the response and reduce credibility with HR/management readers.

The calmer and more structured you sound, the stronger the argument becomes.
