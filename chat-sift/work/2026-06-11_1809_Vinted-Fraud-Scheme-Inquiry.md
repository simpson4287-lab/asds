I understand why repeated “sold, then suddenly unavailable, then refunded” transactions would look coordinated. But **on Vinted’s normal payment flow, the seller does not receive spendable money immediately when you buy**.

The buyer’s payment appears in the seller’s **pending balance** and is held by Vinted’s payment provider. It becomes available only after delivery and completion of the transaction. If the sale is cancelled before that, the buyer is refunded instead. citeturn799682search2turn799682search3turn799682search4

Vinted also says refunds go back to the **same e-wallet or payment account originally used**. Therefore, a legitimate Vinted refund cannot normally be redirected into cash, another bank account, or an unrelated person’s wallet. citeturn799682search1turn799682search5

So the precise scheme you describe—seller receives your clean money immediately, then repays you using dirty cash—should not work through Vinted’s standard integrated system.

There are, however, other plausible fraudulent mechanisms behind repeated unavailable listings:

- **Moving transactions off-platform:** asking the buyer to pay or accept reimbursement by bank transfer, PayPal Friends and Family, cash, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.
- **Testing stolen cards or accounts:** creating purchases and cancellations to determine which payment credentials or accounts remain usable.
- **Account or identity farming:** building transaction history, collecting buyer details, or making accounts appear active before using them for more serious fraud.
- **False or duplicate inventory:** sellers list the same desirable item repeatedly across platforms, perhaps hoping to persuade one buyer to transact outside Vinted.
- **Cash-flow misuse elsewhere:** some marketplaces or payment methods may release funds faster than Vinted, even though Vinted itself ordinarily holds them.
- **Ordinary poor selling behaviour:** cross-listing, lost items, incorrect stock records, or sellers changing their minds. Repetition alone makes this less satisfying as an explanation, but it remains possible.

For money laundering, the important issue is not simply that money gets refunded. A launderer usually needs the refund or onward payment to go to **a different funding source, beneficiary, or financial instrument**. A refund mechanically returned to your original card largely reverses the transaction and offers little laundering value.

Your suspicion becomes materially stronger if the sellers:

- asked you to cancel rather than cancelling themselves;
- offered reimbursement outside Vinted;
- asked you to repurchase through a different account or payment method;
- repeatedly relisted the identical supposedly unavailable item;
- used several new accounts with similar photos or wording;
- provided false tracking;
- or asked you to mark an undelivered item as received.

Keep screenshots of the listing, seller profile, messages, cancellation reason, dates, item photographs and any relisting. Report the pattern as **suspected linked fraudulent listings**, rather than alleging proven money laundering. The repeated behaviour may be genuine evidence of platform abuse, but the normal Vinted payment and refund structure means the straightforward laundering mechanism you proposed is unlikely.
