## 10. Legal Analysis and Defence Positions (Consolidated)

### 10.1 Allegations referenced
- **s.4(1) Road Traffic Act 1988:** driving while unfit through drugs  
- **s.4(2) Road Traffic Act 1988:** being in charge while unfit through drugs  

### 10.2 “Road or public place” argument (location)
The defence manual / anchor documents assert:

- Hylands Park is closed from dusk to dawn under Chelmsford City Council by-laws / local authority by-laws; during closure hours, lawful public access is withdrawn  
- an open side gate does not reinstate lawful public access  
- cited authorities: **DPP v Vivier (1991)** and **May v DPP (2005)** for the proposition that a “public place” requires lawful public access  
- CCTV is described as showing no public presence; no public right of way in use; a closed/dark/secluded environment  
- therefore the statutory element of “road or public place” is said not to be satisfied  

### 10.3 Driving vs being “in charge” and statutory defence
The consolidated materials state:

- movement described as limited, slow, controlled, within the closed park  
- vehicle then stationary for hours; Mr Simpson asleep when approached  
- keys in ignition explained as an ADHD-linked habit to avoid misplacement / ADHD-related habit  
- purpose of remaining in the vehicle described as safety and stability  
- no public endangered  
- reliance placed upon the **s.4(3) statutory defence** (no likelihood of driving), asserted to be satisfied on the balance of probabilities  
- interpretive note included:  
  - s.4(1) requires proof of actual driving while impaired; no statutory defence  
  - s.4(2) concerns being in charge; statutory defence under s.4(3) applies if there was no likelihood of driving  

### 10.4 Timing gap / post-driving consumption principle
Across the materials, the four-hour gap is central:

- last confirmed driving ~01:00  
- blood sample ~05:00  
- roadside tests negative  
- argument: later toxicology does not establish impairment at the time of driving and is consistent with post-driving consumption or residual presence  

A further drafting note preserved across the text:
- some documents refer to “post-driving consumption” in connection with **s.5A RTA 1988**; the broader point advanced is the evidential requirement to prove impairment / relevant substance presence at the time of driving, not merely at the time of sampling.

### 10.5 Public interest and mitigation framing
The anchor/defence documents assert:

- closed park; no public risk; no harm; cooperative interaction  
- behaviour consistent with crisis/vulnerability rather than criminal intent  
- immediate rehabilitation and clinical re-engagement
