[M2] AI (2026-06-15 22:00):
Yes — **that IKOpro Stickall is suitable for sealing around the base**, especially where timber/base rail meets DPM/concrete/metal brackets. From the label it’s a **bitumen mastic/sealant**, intended for sealing joints, seams, cracks and repairs on bituminous surfaces, wood, metal, stone, plastic and concrete, even on damp substrates.

For your use, I’d treat it as a **belt-and-braces weather/DPM seal**, not as a structural adhesive.

Best way to use it there:

1. **Brush/vacuum loose dust and grit off first.** It will tolerate damp, but it won’t bond well to loose dusty muck.
2. **Run a bead along the base joint**, especially gaps where water could track behind the timber or under the plate.
3. **Tool it in firmly** with a gloved finger/scraper so it actually wets both surfaces.
4. **Don’t completely block intentional drainage/ventilation paths.** Seal the vulnerable edge, but avoid making a water trap behind the timber.
5. Around those metal straps, it’s fine to smear over screw heads/edges, but don’t rely on it to hold anything mechanically.

One caution: because it is **bitumen-based**, it stays a bit tarry/flexible and can be messy. Paints, tapes and some coatings may not like sticking to it afterwards. So use it where you want a permanent black seal, not where you still need a clean surface for tape or a neat finish.

For the base in your photo: **yes, I’d use it**, especially along that timber/concrete/DPM junction and around the bracket penetrations. Just don’t cake it everywhere so thick that any water that does get in has nowhere to escape.

Yes, that approach makes sense: **bitumen mastic into the actual vulnerable joint first**, then your **DPM coating lapped up the wood about 100mm and out across the floor about 100mm** as a secondary “tank” seal.

A few important points though:

**Let the Stickall skin/cure first** before coating over it. If you paint DPM straight onto fresh mastic, the solvents/bitumen can stay soft and smear. I’d leave it at least overnight if you can, longer if it’s cold.

**Don’t make a thick proud bead everywhere.** Push the Stickall into cracks, screw heads, bracket edges and the timber/floor junction, then tool it smooth. The DPM will bridge better over a flatter fillet than over lumpy tar.

**The DPM coat wants a reasonably clean, keyed surface.** On dusty cement/concrete, your diluted first/binding coat is fine. On the timber, brush dust off and don’t over-thin the final coat too much if it needs to act as the waterproofing layer.

For that exact area, I’d do it like this:

1. Clean loose dirt/dust.
2. Stickall into the joint, bracket edges, screw heads, gaps and any obvious water paths.
3. Tool smooth into a small fillet.
4. Let it firm up.
5. Brush/roll DPM up the timber/base rail about **100mm** and out over the floor about **100mm**.
6. Second coat if you’ve got enough, especially over the floor/wall corner.

The only thing I’d avoid is fully sealing both sides of timber in a way that traps water inside. But for the **bottom vulnerable edge inside an air gap**, your plan is sensible. It’s basically creating a small upstand/kerb so water cannot wick into the base joint.
