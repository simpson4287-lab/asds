Of course. The **12-step program** is best known from Alcoholics Anonymous (AA), but it has been adapted for many issues, including drug use, gambling, overeating, and more. It’s not a rigid formula but a spiritual and practical framework for recovery, designed to help people change their relationship with substances, behaviors, and themselves.  

Here’s a step-by-step overview in plain language:  

---

### **The 12 Steps (explained simply)**
1. **Admit powerlessness**  
   – Recognize that you can’t control your drinking/using/behavior by willpower alone.  
   – It’s about honesty: *“I can’t do this on my own.”*

2. **Believe in a greater power**  
   – Accept that help is possible, even if it comes from something bigger than yourself (not necessarily religious—can be community, nature, or your own higher values).  

3. **Turn over control**  
   – Make a decision to trust that higher power, or the program, to guide you.  

4. **Take a personal inventory**  
   – Write down an honest list of your resentments, fears, mistakes, and harms done to others.  

5. **Admit wrongs**  
   – Share what you wrote with yourself, your higher power, and another trusted person.  

6. **Be ready to change**  
   – Become willing to let go of the flaws and behaviors that hold you back.  

7. **Ask for help with change**  
   – Humbly ask your higher power to remove those shortcomings.  

8. **List those you’ve harmed**  
   – Make a list of people you hurt through your addiction or behaviors.  

9. **Make amends**  
   – Wherever possible, directly repair the damage (apologize, repay debts, rebuild trust) unless doing so would cause more harm.  

10. **Daily inventory**  
   – Keep track of your behavior each day; admit mistakes quickly and correct them.  

11. **Spiritual practice**  
   – Use prayer, meditation, or reflection to grow spiritually and stay connected to your higher power.  

12. **Helping others**  
   – Share your recovery journey and help others who are struggling, which strengthens your own sobriety.  

---

- Meetings are central—people share stories, struggles, and progress.  
- It’s not about perfection—it’s about progress, honesty, and community support.

  - You miss out on the healing power of shared experiences and support.
