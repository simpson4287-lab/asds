## UK legal baseline (what applies by default)

A road-legal e-bike (EAPC) must meet all of the following:

- **Motor assist cuts out at 15.5 mph (25 km/h)**
- **Pedal-assist only** (no throttle beyond walking speed)
- **≤250 W continuous rated motor**
- Rider pedals to get assistance

Within this definition, the bike is treated as a **normal bicycle**.

---

## Impact on riding experience

### 1. On-road riding
- **Up to ~15.5 mph:** very easy cruising, minimal effort  
- **Above 15.5 mph:** you can go faster, but **the motor stops helping**
- Expect:
  - More leg effort
  - Heavier feel due to motor + battery mass
- Realistically:
  - 16–18 mph is comfortable on flats
  - 20+ mph requires fitness or downhill help

**Net effect:**  
The limit mainly affects **top cruising speed**, not acceleration or usability.

---

### 2. Off-road / trail riding
- Speed limit matters **much less**
- Typical trail speeds are:
  - 6–12 mph climbs
  - 10–18 mph flowing sections
- The motor limit mostly affects:
  - Long fire-road transitions
  - Fast gravel sections

**Net effect:**  
Almost no downside for trail riding; torque and control matter more than top speed.

---

### 3. Climbing & control
- Speed limits **do not restrict torque**
- A good mid-drive motor will:
  - Climb steep hills easily
  - Allow very slow, controlled progress
- You can ride **very slowly** with assist — ideal for technical terrain

**Net effect:**  
Excellent low-speed control; no penalty here.

---

## What happens if the bike exceeds the limits

If an e-bike:
- Assists beyond 15.5 mph, or
- Has a throttle that propels without pedalling, or
- Uses a higher-powered motor

Then legally it becomes a **motor vehicle**, requiring:
- Insurance
- Registration
- Helmet (motorcycle standard)
- MOT-style compliance

**Practical consequences:**
- Not legal on public roads
- Not legal on cycle paths
- Often not allowed on public trails

---

## Real-world summary (at £1,500 budget)

- It mainly affects:
  - Fast road commuting
  - Long flat sections where you want to cruise at 20+ mph
- It does **not** affect:
  - Hill climbing
  - Off-road capability
  - Slow riding
  - Fitness-friendly riding

For mixed road + off-road use, a legal 15.5 mph e-bike is usually the **best balance** of:
- Access (everywhere you want to ride)
- Simplicity
- Safety
- Insurance avoidance
