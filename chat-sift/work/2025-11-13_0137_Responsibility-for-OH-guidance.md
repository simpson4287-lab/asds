In UK workplaces, responsibility for ensuring **Occupational Health (OH) guidance is followed accurately** is *shared*—but not equally. Here’s the breakdown:

---

## **1. Employer – **Primary Legal Responsibility**
The **employer** carries the main duty under the **Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974** and related regulations.

They must:
- **Act on Occupational Health recommendations** (e.g., reasonable adjustments, phased return plans, health surveillance actions).
- **Ensure managers implement those recommendations**.
- **Provide safe systems of work** and adapt them based on OH input.
- **Document decisions** if they cannot follow a recommendation (and give a lawful reason).

If OH guidance is ignored or misapplied, **the employer is accountable**, not the employee.

---

## **2. Line Manager / HR – Operational Responsibility**
Managers are responsible for:
- Understanding the OH advice accurately.
- Implementing the recommended adjustments or controls day-to-day.
- Reporting if something is unclear or unachievable.
- Monitoring that adjustments are actually happening consistently.

HR’s role often includes:
- Coordinating communication between OH and managers.
- Ensuring policies are followed.
- Keeping documentation aligned with employment law and equality law.

Failure at this level is usually an **employer process failure**, not an employee issue.

---

## **3. Occupational Health – Professional Responsibility**
OH practitioners must:
- Provide **clear, evidence-based, accurate** advice.
- Ensure recommendations are practical and within the scope of workplace realities.
- Communicate anything that is critical for safety.

However, *OH cannot enforce* their advice. They advise; the employer must act.

---

## **4. Employee – Limited Responsibility**
An employee is expected to:
- Follow agreed adjustments.
- Report if something isn’t working.
- Attend OH assessments.

But they **are not responsible** for ensuring the employer implements OH advice.

---

## **In short**
### **It is the employer’s legal responsibility** to ensure Occupational Health guidance is implemented accurately.

Line managers and HR help operationalize it, and OH provides the expert advice, but the **accountability rests with the employer**.
