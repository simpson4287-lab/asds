# Reviving Rusty Drill Bits

[M1] ME (2026-04-17 16:10):
What’s the best way to revive rusty drill bits

[M2] AI (2026-04-17 16:10):
Best way is **rust removal first, then sharpening if needed, then oiling before storage**.

### 1. Check if they’re worth saving
Keep them if:
- the shank is straight
- the flutes are not deeply pitted
- the cutting lips are still mostly intact
- they are decent HSS / cobalt bits

Bin or demote them to rough work if:
- the cutting edge is heavily pitted
- the tip is chipped
- the bit is bent
- the rust has eaten into the flutes badly

Surface rust is easy. Deep rust on the cutting edge usually means it will never cut nicely again without regrinding.

### 2. Remove loose rust mechanically
Start simple:

- Wire brush
- Scotch-Brite pad
- Fine wet-and-dry paper, around 400–800 grit
- Small brass brush for flutes

Spin the bit gently in a drill while holding Scotch-Brite around the shank/flutes if you want a quick clean-up. Don’t squeeze near the tip aggressively or you can round the cutting edges.

### 3. Soak for heavier rust
For rusty but salvageable bits, use one of these:

**Best gentle option:** white vinegar  
Soak for a few hours, check often, scrub, rinse, dry.

**Better controlled option:** citric acid solution  
A spoonful or two in warm water, soak, scrub, rinse, dry.

**Best commercial option:** Evapo-Rust or similar rust remover  
This is usually kinder to decent tools than harsh acids.

After any wet treatment, rinse, dry immediately, then oil. A hairdryer or warm radiator helps drive moisture out of the flutes.

### 4. Neutralise and dry
After vinegar/citric acid:

- rinse in clean water
- optionally wipe with a little bicarbonate of soda solution
- rinse again
- dry thoroughly

Then coat lightly with **3-in-1 oil, WD-40, light machine oil, camellia oil, or similar**.

WD-40 is fine as a water-displacer, but for longer storage a light oil is better.

### 5. Sharpen the cutting edge
Rust removal alone won’t make a dull bit cut well.

For twist drills, the critical part is the **two cutting lips at the tip**. They need to be equal length and angle. You can use:

- a bench grinder, carefully
- a drill-bit sharpening jig
- a dedicated sharpener
- diamond file for small touch-ups

For general metal/wood twist bits, aim for roughly **118° point angle** unless they are special bits. Cobalt/stainless-type bits are often 135° split-point.

If the rust was on the cutting lips, sharpening is probably the step that actually “revives” them.

### 6. Prevent it coming back
Store them dry and lightly oiled. Best options:

- keep them in an indexed drill case
- add silica gel sachets
- avoid damp sheds/garages if possible
- wipe after use, especially after drilling masonry, wet timber, or treated wood
- don’t put damp bits straight back in the case

For a quick practical workflow:

**Wire brush → vinegar/citric soak if needed → scrub → rinse → dry with heat → light oil → sharpen → store dry.**

For cheap small bits, I’d only spend time on the larger or better-quality ones. A badly rusted 2–4 mm bit usually isn’t worth the faff.
