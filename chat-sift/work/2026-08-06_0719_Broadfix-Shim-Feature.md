[M2] AI (2026-08-06 07:19):
Yes, I know the feature you mean. Looking at your photo, that little raised tongue inside the rectangular slot isn't really a feeler gauge. It's primarily a **retention feature** that's moulded into the Broadfix shim.

It serves a few purposes:

- **Keeps stacked shims together.** When you slide Broadfix shims into each other, that tab provides a small amount of friction so they don't fall apart while you're positioning them.
- **Prevents slipping.** It gives a slight interference fit when something bears on the shim.
- **Allows slight compression.** Because it's a flexible tongue rather than a solid lump, it can deflect a little under load.

Regarding your idea:

> Could it act as a barb if something is close to that gap?

**Yes, to a limited extent.** If you push the shim into a tight gap (for example between timber and concrete or beneath a bearer), that little tongue can press against the adjacent surface and provide a small amount of grip. It isn't a true one-way barb like you'd find on a cable tie, but it can help resist the shim sliding back out under its own weight or from light vibration.

It **isn't intended to provide structural retention**. Once the load from your cabin bearer is on the shim, the clamping force is doing essentially all of the work. The tab just makes installation a bit easier.

For your cabin project, once the bearer is sitting on the Broadfix pack, that little tab won't affect the load capacity or long-term performance. The compressive load is carried by the body of the shim, not by the raised tongue.
