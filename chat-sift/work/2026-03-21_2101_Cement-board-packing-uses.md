Can cement board be used as packing pieces 

[M2] AI (2026-03-21 21:01):
Yes, **it can be**, but only for the right kind of packing.

Cement board is **non-structural** in most product literature, so I would only use it as a **small, stable spacer/packer** where the load is modest and well supported, not as a primary load-bearing shim. Some cement-board installation guidance explicitly mentions using shims or spacer strips with cement board assemblies, and fibre-cement systems also use spacer/gasket materials in practice. citeturn727728search1turn727728search15turn727728search9turn727728search6

Good uses:
- levelling out a **cladding/battens/board interface**
- small packers behind trims or fixings
- moisture-tolerant spacing where timber might swell

Less good uses:
- under **high point loads**
- where the piece is **very thin and narrow**, so it may crack
- anywhere you need a **precise, durable structural shim** for long-term compression

Main catches:
- edges can be **brittle**, especially small offcuts
- it should not sit where it will stay **wet without detailing**, because many cement boards are moisture resistant but **not waterproofing in themselves** citeturn727728search8turn727728search12
- use **full bearing area** rather than tiny slivers

Practical rule:
- **Okay for packers:** flat, reasonably wide pieces, lightly to moderately loaded
- **Use plastic packers instead:** doors/windows, posts, heavy frames, concentrated loads, or anything you may need to adjust accurately

For your kind of build work, I’d say: **fine as improvised packing pieces for cement board / cladding-type details, not ideal as structural shims**. If you want, send a photo of where you want to use them and I’ll tell you whether cement board is sensible there.

[M4] AI (2026-03-21 21:04):
Tiles can work **better than cement board** as packers in some situations, because they are **hard, dimensionally stable, and moisture-proof-ish**, but they’re also **brittle**.

So:

**Good as packers**
- under **light to moderate compression**
- where they sit **fully supported and flat**
- as small levelling pieces behind boards, trims, battens, or at the base of something non-structural
- especially **porcelain** or dense ceramic offcuts

**Not good**
- where the load is a **point load**
- where screws/fixings will bear through them awkwardly
- where they may get knocked during assembly
- for anything structural like posts, heavy frames, door linings, etc.

Compared with cement board:
- **tile is harder and less compressible**
- **cement board is a bit less glassy/brittle**
- **both are a bit makeshift**
- **plastic packers are still the proper option**

Practical view:
- A **full tile offcut** used flat as a spacer = usually fine
- A **tiny sliver of tile** = crack risk
- Don’t rely on tile fragments stacked loosely

So yes, **tiles can be used as packing pieces**, and in many cases I’d trust a solid flat tile offcut **more than a crumbly cement-board sliver**, but only where the load is spread nicely.

How to attach feather edge board to cement board 

[M10] AI (2026-03-21 22:16):
Not **directly to the cement board alone** in the usual sense.

For feather-edge timber cladding, the normal build-up is:

**frame/studs → membrane if needed → cement/fibre-cement board → vertical battens/counter-battens fixed back to the framing → feather edge boards fixed to the battens**

That is the standard kind of arrangement shown in fibre-cement sheathing guidance: the sheathing board goes on the frame first, then **timber cladding is installed onto counter battens**, not simply nailed into the board itself. citeturn662785search1turn662785search4

Why:
- cement board is **not a great fixing substrate** for repeated cladding nails/screws on its own
- feather edge wants a **ventilated cavity/rainscreen gap**
- battens give you a straight fixing line and let the cladding dry properly. citeturn662785search1turn662785search6

So the practical method is:

1. **Fix the cement board properly** to the structural frame.  
2. Add a **breather membrane / WRB** in the right position for your wall build-up if the board itself is only sheathing, not the weatherproof layer.  
3. Fix **treated battens** through the board and back into the studs/frame.  
4. Leave a **drained/ventilated cavity** behind the feather edge.  
5. Fix the feather edge boards to the battens with suitable exterior fixings.

A few important points:
- Use **treated battens** and durable fixings.
- Fix the battens into the **structure behind**, not just the board. NHBC notes that where cladding attachments are needed, support/fixing back to structure or added fixing blocks is the right idea, rather than relying on the face board by itself. citeturn662785search7turn662785search8
- Don’t sandwich timber tight against a board with **no cavity**, or moisture can get trapped.
- If your “cement board” is actually a **tile backer board**, that is even more a sign not to treat it as the cladding fixing substrate.

So the answer is:

**Feather edge should usually be attached to battens, and the battens should be fixed through the cement board into the studs/frame behind.**  
I would **not** rely on screwing or nailing feather edge straight to the cement board itself.

- Don’t fill the vent zone with sealant or membrane flaps that end up blocking airflow.

- So the lowest feather edge acts a bit like a rain skirt over the opening.
- That makes wind-driven rain less likely to shoot straight in.

**cement board stops above the base
