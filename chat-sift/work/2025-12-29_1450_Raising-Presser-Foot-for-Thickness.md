[M2] AI (2025-12-29 14:50):
What you are looking at is the **presser foot assembly**. The clear plastic part itself does **not** independently raise; it moves only as part of the presser foot mechanism. To get thicker material underneath, you need to use the presser foot’s **extra-lift capability**, not try to lever the plastic foot.

Proceed as follows, in order of effectiveness:

---

## 1. Use the presser foot lever fully (including extra lift)
At the **back or side of the machine** there is a presser foot lever.

- Raise it **all the way up** as normal.
- Then gently push it **slightly higher than its usual stop**.

Most machines have a **second-stage “extra lift”** specifically for thick fabrics (multiple layers, hems, foam, vinyl, etc.). This gives several extra millimetres of clearance.

If it feels like it stops, do not force it hard—just a firm additional upward push.

---

## 2. Lower the feed dogs temporarily (if needed)
If the material is still catching:

- Look for a **feed dog drop lever or switch** (often near the bobbin area or back of the machine).
- Lowering the feed dogs lets the fabric slide under more easily.
- Raise them again before sewing.

---

## 3. Use a “hump jumper” / leveling tool (recommended for thick steps)
When sewing thick sections (folded hems, seams crossing seams):

- Place a **small plastic leveling tool**, folded card, or purpose-made “hump jumper” **behind the presser foot**.
- Lower the foot onto it.
- This keeps the foot level and prevents it from jamming at the front.

This is standard practice and avoids stressing the foot or needle bar.

---

## 4. Check you are not already at maximum lift
If the foot will not lift higher even with extra pressure:

- The machine may already be at its **maximum presser bar height**.
- In that case, forcing material under risks:
  - Bent needle
  - Skipped stitches
  - Presser bar damage

---

## 5. What **not** to do
- Do **not** pry or lever the clear plastic foot upward.
- Do **not** loosen screws on the presser bar unless doing a formal adjustment.
- Do **not** force thick material under with the foot down.

---

That determines whether your machine has an extra-lift feature or whether a different presser foot (high-shank / walking foot) would be the correct solution.

### What’s happening
You have found the **temporary extra-lift lever**. It is **spring-loaded**, so:
- It will raise the presser foot higher,
- but it is **not designed to latch** in the up position.

It is only meant to be held up **while you slide the fabric underneath**.

---

## Correct way to use it (step-by-step)

1. **Raise the main presser foot lever fully** (the one that normally stays up).
2. **Hold the extra lever up with one hand**.
3. With your other hand, **slide the thick fabric fully under the foot**, past the needle.
4. **Release the lever** once the fabric is positioned.
5. Lower the main presser foot lever to sew.

Do not try to make the extra lever “stay up” — that is not how it is designed.

---

## If the fabric still won’t go in cleanly
This usually means the front of the foot is hitting a step in thickness.

Use one of these approved techniques:

### Option A — leveling shim (best practice)
Place a folded piece of card, plastic, or a proper **hump jumper** behind the presser foot, then lower the foot.  
This keeps the foot level and allows the thick section to feed correctly.

### Option B — needle down first
- Turn the handwheel to put the **needle down into the fabric**.
- Then lower the foot.
This stabilises the work and often gives you just enough clearance.

---

## Important warning
Do **not** attempt to:
- Tape the lever,
- wedge it,
- or force it to latch.

That can bend the presser bar or damage the lift cam.

---

### Bottom line
Nothing is broken.
- The lever is **working exactly as intended**.
- It is a **momentary extra-lift**, not a locking mechanism.

Short answer: **no** — the loose thread ends do **not** get cut off automatically by sewing. They must be dealt with deliberately, otherwise they will either get pulled into the seam or unravel.

From your photo, the loose top thread tail is exactly at the point where problems start.

---

## What should happen (correct practice)

### At the start of a seam
1. **Pull both threads (top and bobbin) to the back**, under the presser foot, about 8–10 cm.
2. Hold them lightly for the **first 2–3 stitches**.
3. Then sew normally.

This prevents:
- Thread nests underneath
- Loose ends being dragged into the fabric
- Skipped or messy first stitches

---

### At the end of a seam
You have two correct options:

#### Option A — Backstitch (most common)
- Sew forward a few stitches
- Press reverse for 2–3 stitches
- Sew forward again
This locks the seam.  
You then **cut the threads manually**.

#### Option B — Lock stitch + trim
- Use the machine’s lock function (if it has one)
- Raise the presser foot
- **Cut threads with scissors or the built-in cutter**

---

## What does *not* happen automatically
Unless your machine explicitly has:
- **Automatic thread cutter**, or
- **Programmable lock-and-cut**

…the machine **will not trim threads by itself**.

From your photos, this machine appears to rely on **manual trimming** (very common and completely normal).

---

## Important for your thick strap material
Because this is dense webbing:
- Loose ends can get **pulled back into the seam**, causing bunching
- Or get **stitched into the side**, looking untidy

Best practice here:
- Always start with threads pulled back
- Backstitch at start and end
- Trim flush with sharp scissors

---

## One more critical point (visible in your photo)
Your presser foot is riding up a thickness step. Before sewing:
- Put the **needle down manually**
- Then lower the foot
This stabilises the fabric and prevents the loose thread from being dragged forward.

---

### Bottom line
- Loose ends are **not cut automatically**
- They must be **secured (backstitch or lock)** and **trimmed**
- What you’re seeing in the photo is normal — just needs the correct handling
