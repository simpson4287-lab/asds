[M1] ME (2026-06-22 11:39):
When sand on dpm as a topcoat to stop peeling, can it be watered down 

[M2] AI (2026-06-22 11:39):
Yes — **but only slightly, and only if the product allows it**.

For liquid DPM/bitumen/acrylic damp-proof coatings, the usual approach is:

**Best method**
1. Apply the DPM **neat** as the final coat.
2. While it is still wet/tacky, **broadcast dry kiln-dried sand** into it.
3. Let it cure.
4. Brush/vacuum off loose sand.

That gives a grippy mechanical surface and helps stop later coats/adhesives/overlaps peeling.

**Watering down**
- A **very light dilution** can help it soak into dusty/porous cement board or concrete as a *primer/binding coat*.
- But for the **sand-holding topcoat**, don’t water it down much. Too much water weakens the film and the sand may not lock in properly.
- If the tub says something like **“first coat may be diluted 50/50 on porous surfaces”**, that is normally for the first/primer coat, not the final sealing coat.

For your use, I’d do: **neat or barely diluted final coat + dry sand broadcast while wet**. Don’t use wet sand if you can avoid it, because it can sit on the surface rather than bedding in properly.

[M3] ME (2026-06-22 11:42):
The floor has already been dpm. I’m trying to prevent it peeling in the heat

[M4] AI (2026-06-22 11:42):
Yes, in that case I’d treat it as a **sacrificial grip/binding layer**, not as another waterproofing layer.

Because the floor has already had DPM, I’d do this:

1. **Let the existing DPM cure as much as possible first**  
   If it is still soft/tacky underneath, extra coats can skin over and trap moisture/solvent, making peeling worse in heat.

2. **Lightly key the surface if it is glossy**
   A quick rub with coarse sandpaper/abrasive pad helps the next coat grip. Don’t go mad and cut through the DPM.

3. **Apply a thin coat of DPM**
   I would use it **neat or only very slightly watered down**.  
   For stopping heat peeling, too much water is not ideal because it makes a weaker film.

4. **Throw dry kiln-dried sand into it while wet**
   Don’t mix loads of sand into the tub. Brush/roll a thin coat, then broadcast sand onto the surface so it beds into the wet DPM.

5. **After curing, brush off loose sand**
   Then you’ll have a rougher surface that is much less likely to peel, stick to feet, or lift in warm conditions.

The important bit: **do not make a thick, wet, rubbery layer**. Thin coat + dry sand is better. Thick DPM layers are more likely to stay soft and peel when heated.
