[M3] ME (2026-05-26 14:59):
What is salary protection insurance 

[M4] AI (2026-05-26 14:59):
**Salary protection insurance** usually means **income protection insurance**: a policy that pays you a regular monthly amount if you cannot work because of **illness or injury**.

It is not the same as redundancy cover in most cases.

How it typically works:

You pay a monthly premium. If you become too unwell to work, the policy starts paying after a chosen waiting period, often called the **deferred period**. That could be 4 weeks, 13 weeks, 26 weeks, etc. The longer the waiting period, the cheaper the policy usually is.

It normally pays a percentage of your earnings, often around **50–70% of gross salary**, because the payout is usually designed to replace take-home income rather than your full salary.

There are two broad types:

**Short-term income protection** pays for a limited period, often 12 or 24 months per claim.

**Long-term income protection** can keep paying until you return to work, retire, or the policy ends, depending on the policy terms.

The important wording is whether it covers you for being unable to do:

**Your own occupation** — best cover. It pays if you cannot do your actual job.

**Suited occupation** — pays if you cannot do a job suited to your skills/experience.

**Any occupation** — weakest cover. It may only pay if you cannot do almost any work.

It usually covers things like long-term sickness, injury, serious mental health conditions, back problems, recovery from surgery, and similar work-preventing conditions, subject to exclusions.

It usually **does not** cover normal job loss/redundancy unless it is a specific **accident, sickness and unemployment** policy. It also may exclude pre-existing conditions, self-inflicted injury, drug/alcohol-related issues, or conditions not properly disclosed when applying.

In plain terms: **it is insurance against losing your income because your body or health stops you working**, not usually because your employer lets you go.

[M5] ME (2026-05-26 15:00):
What is redundancy cover

[M6] AI (2026-05-26 15:00):
**Redundancy cover** is insurance that pays you a monthly amount for a limited time if you lose your job through **involuntary redundancy**.

It is often sold as part of:

**Payment Protection Insurance** — covers a specific loan, mortgage, or credit card payment.

**Mortgage Payment Protection Insurance** — helps cover your mortgage if you are made redundant.

**Accident, Sickness and Unemployment cover** — can cover redundancy as well as illness/injury.

The key point is that it normally only pays if you are made redundant **through no fault of your own**. It usually will not pay if you resign, are dismissed for misconduct, take voluntary redundancy, knew redundancy was likely when you bought the policy, or are on a temporary/contract job that simply ends as expected.

It also usually has:

**A waiting period**, such as 30, 60, or 90 days before payments start.

**A maximum payout period**, often 12 or 24 months.

**A cap on monthly benefit**, either a fixed amount or a percentage of your salary/payment.

So compared with salary protection/income protection:

**Income protection** = pays if you cannot work due to illness or injury.  
**Redundancy cover** = pays if your employer makes your role redundant.

For most people, the strongest setup is checking what sick pay, redundancy pay, savings, and mortgage/rent obligations they already have, then deciding whether they need cover for sickness, redundancy, or both.
