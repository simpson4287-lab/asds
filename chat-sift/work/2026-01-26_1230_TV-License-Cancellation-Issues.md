## 1. Cancelling the direct debit vs cancelling the licence
- **Stopping the Direct Debit does not notify TV Licensing** that you no longer need a licence.
- It simply stops payment.
- Their system then flags the address as **“no licence + no declaration”**, which triggers letters and visits.

This is very common.

---

## 2. When you legally need a TV licence (UK)
You **do NOT need a TV licence** if **all** of the following are true:

- You do **not** watch or record **live TV** on *any* channel, on *any* device  
- You do **not** watch or stream live TV via apps or websites (ITV, Sky Go, Amazon live channels, etc.)
- You do **not** watch or download **BBC iPlayer**

Streaming services like Netflix, Prime (on-demand), Disney+, YouTube (non-live) are fine.

If that’s your situation, you are fully within the law.

---

## 3. The “visit” letter you received
Key points:

- The “I visited today” wording **does not mean they entered your property**
- Enforcement officers **have no right of entry without a warrant**
- They often:
  - Knock and get no answer
  - Or do not knock at all
  - Then leave this letter

No warrant = no entry = no evidence.

This letter is primarily **pressure**, not enforcement.

---

## 4. You have NOT committed an offence by:
- Cancelling the Direct Debit
- Ignoring letters
- Not calling them
- Not responding previously

An offence only occurs if:
- You **actually** watch/record live TV or BBC iPlayer **and**
- They can prove it (which usually requires admission or evidence)

---

- State that you **do not need a TV licence**

You are not admitting anything. You are asserting your legal position.

### Option B — Do nothing
Legally allowed, but:
- Letters will continue
- Occasional visits may continue
- No escalation unless you admit to watching live TV / iPlayer
