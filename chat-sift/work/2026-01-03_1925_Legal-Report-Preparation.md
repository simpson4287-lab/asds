## 10. Legal Analysis and Defence Positions (Consolidated and Expanded)

### 10.1 Allegations referenced

- s.4(2) Road Traffic Act 1988: being in charge while unfit through drugs  

### 10.2 Legal basis: “road or other place to which the public have or are permitted to have access”
Under sections **4** and **5A** of the Road Traffic Act 1988, the prosecution must prove that any alleged driving occurred **“on a road or other place to which the public have or are permitted to have access.”** The meaning of “public place” turns on the access position at the material time rather than ownership alone.

### 10.3 Ownership and permissive access basis (Open Spaces Act and by-laws)
Hylands Park is owned and managed by Chelmsford City Council under **Open Spaces Act 1906, section 12**. Public access is granted on a **permissive basis** via Council by-laws, including **Regulation 26 of the 1966 Hylands Park Regulations**, which limit opening hours and authorise the Council to close and withdraw permission after a specified time each evening.

The consolidated materials further state:
- Hylands Park is council-owned estate land, not a highway  
- Regulation 26 states the park is closed one hour after sunset  
- published closing times (June/July: 22:00) confirm practical enforcement  
- the Council retains express power to close access to the park  

### 10.4 Open gate scenario: physical entry does not equal lawful permission
If a gate or pedestrian entrance remains unlocked after closure, this does **not** constitute continued public permission to access the park. Physical entry may be possible, but such access is without lawful authority and amounts to civil trespass. A failure to secure the boundary is an enforcement lapse and does not convert the land back into a public place for Road Traffic Act purposes.

### 10.5 Case law summary (consolidated list)
The consolidated materials cite and rely upon authorities confirming the lawful-access principle at the material time:

- **DPP v Vivier [1991] Crim LR 637** – “public place” depends upon public access, not ownership  
- **DPP v MacDonagh [1974] RTR 382** – once a gated car park is closed it ceases to be a public place even if physical entry remains possible  
- **Laurie v DPP [1989] Crim LR 108** – the question is determined at the time of the alleged offence  
- **Robson v Hallett [1967] 2 QB 939** – permission can be revoked; continued presence thereafter is trespass  
- Additional authority referenced across materials: **May v DPP (2005)** for the proposition that lawful public access is central  

### 10.6 Legal consequence (public place element)
Once Hylands Park is formally closed under its by-laws, the public no longer have permission to enter or remain. The area is therefore not a “public place” within the meaning of the Road Traffic Act 1988. An open gate does not alter this status; it creates an opportunity for unlawful entry, not lawful access. Any alleged driving within the park after closure thus falls outside the statutory definition required for Road Traffic Act offences.

|---|---|
| Park open within hours | Public place – lawful access by permission |
| Park closed (gates locked) | Private/restricted land – access withdrawn; not a public place |
| Gate left unlocked after closure | Private/restricted land – physical access possible but unlawful (trespass) |
| Public unaware of closure | Still private/restricted; may mitigate culpability but does not restore public status |

**Prepared for:** Chris Simpson

**1. Legal basis**  
Under sections 4 and 5A of the Road Traffic Act 1988, the prosecution must prove that any alleged driving occurred “on a road or other place to which the public have or are permitted to have access”. The term “public place” has been interpreted by case law as depending upon factual access at the time of the alleged incident rather than ownership alone.

**2. Ownership and permission status**  
Hylands Park is owned and managed by Chelmsford City Council under the Open Spaces Act 1906, section 12. **3. The “open gate” scenario**

- *Laurie v DPP* [1989] Crim LR 108 – The question of public access is determined at the specific time of the alleged offence.  
- *Robson v Hallett* [1967] 2 QB 939 – Permission to enter property may be revoked at any moment; continued presence thereafter is trespass.

**5. Legal consequence**
