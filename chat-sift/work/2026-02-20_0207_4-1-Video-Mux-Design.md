A “4:1 video mux” usually means **selecting 1 of 4 analogue video channels** (e.g., CCD output, composite-ish baseband, etc.) onto a single output with acceptable **bandwidth, linearity, low charge injection, and low crosstalk**.

Here are the sensible ways to do it, depending on what you mean by “video”:

## 1) Analogue baseband video (fast edges / CCD video, tens of MHz+)
Use a **video-rated analogue switch / mux** (or 4 individual SPST switches) and then **buffer with a proper video amp**.

Good “classic” parts families to look at (keywords you can search in parametric tables):
- **ADG/ADG7xx / ADG8xx (Analog Devices)**: lots of high-speed, low-charge-injection muxes/switches.
- **TS3A / TS5A / TS5V (TI)**: some are specifically positioned for high-speed analogue switching.
- **MAX45xx / MAX46xx (Maxim/ADI legacy)**: high-speed analogue mux/switch options.
- **74HC4052/4051** are *cheap* but usually **not great for video quality** (Ron flatness, charge injection, bandwidth/crosstalk) unless your requirements are forgiving.

**Topologies that actually behave well:**
- **4:1 mux → clamp/anti-alias RC → video buffer (low noise, wide BW)**  
- Or **buffer each input first** (4 buffers), then switch (reduces source impedance sensitivity and charge injection artefacts).

Key specs to sanity-check:
- **-3 dB bandwidth** comfortably above your highest significant content (often 5–10× your pixel rate / edge content expectation)
- **Charge injection** (glitch when switching)
- **Ron flatness** (distortion vs signal level)
- **Crosstalk / isolation** at frequency
- Input/output voltage range vs your rails (and whether signal is centered on some offset)

## 2) Differential “video” (LVDS / SLVS / HDMI-ish)
Don’t use an analogue mux. Use a **differential crosspoint / mux / switch** designed for high-speed serial/differential.
- For **LVDS**, there are dedicated **LVDS crosspoint switches** and **high-speed differential multiplexers**.
- For **HDMI/DP**, you’d use **TMDS/DisplayPort switches** (completely different category).

## 3) Composite video (CVBS) / SD video
There are dedicated **video multiplexers/switches** with 75 Ω drive considerations, often with integrated buffering. These are easiest if you’re literally doing CVBS.

## How to do “pre + potential inversion” cleanly

### Non-inverting buffer (pre)
Use a **voltage follower** *or* a mild gain (e.g. +2) if you need swing.

**Stability trick that saves hours:**  
Put **33–68 Ω** in series at the op-amp output before it sees the mux / trace / any capacitance.

### Inverting option (same BOM, just change R’s)
Inverting is often nicer around muxing because the **inverting node impedance is well-defined**.

- Set **Rin = Rf** for gain = −1 (pure inversion)
- Start with **Rin/Rf = 200–499 Ω** range if you’re treating it like “video” (keeps Johnson noise low and controls pole locations)
- Add **small Cf** across Rf if you see peaking/ringing (start **0.5–2 pF**, tune on scope)

### If you need “selectable inversion”
Two practical ways:
1) **Use a second channel** of the dual op-amp: one configured +1 buffer, the other −1 inverter; then mux selects which output you take. (Very clean.)
2) **Use one op-amp and switch the feedback network** (works, but the switch injects charge directly into the feedback node — more glitchy at 25 MHz unless you’re careful).
