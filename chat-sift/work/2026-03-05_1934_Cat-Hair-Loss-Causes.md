My cat seems to be losing hair on his front legs

Hair loss on a cat’s **front legs** can happen for several different reasons. The key thing to determine is whether the hair is **falling out by itself** or whether your cat is **over-grooming or chewing the area**. Those lead to different causes.

Here are the most common possibilities:

---

### 1. Over-grooming (very common)
Cats sometimes **lick or chew the fur off their legs**, leaving smooth bald or thin patches.

**Common triggers**
- Fleas or flea allergy
- Skin irritation
- Stress or anxiety
- Pain in a joint or paw

**Signs**
- Hair looks “barbered” or very short rather than completely gone  
- You see the cat licking the legs a lot  
- Skin underneath usually looks normal

---

### 2. Fleas or flea allergy
Even **one or two fleas** can cause intense itching in sensitive cats.

**Signs**
- Excessive licking of legs, belly, or base of tail  
- Small scabs or red spots  
- Black “flea dirt” in the coat

Even indoor cats can get them.

---

### 3. Allergies (food or environmental)
Cats can develop allergies that cause itching and hair loss.

**Signs**
- Hair loss on legs, belly, or face  
- Red or irritated skin  
- Sometimes ear issues or scratching

---

### 4. Skin infections or parasites
Things like **mites, ringworm (fungal infection), or bacterial infections** can cause patches of hair loss.

**Signs**
- Circular bald patches  
- Flaky or scaly skin  
- Redness or crusting

Ringworm is contagious to humans, so this one matters to check.

---

### 5. Pain or arthritis
Sometimes cats lick a specific limb because **something hurts underneath** (joint, tendon, or paw).

**Signs**
- Licking only one leg  
- Slight limp or reduced jumping  
- Sensitivity when touched

---

✅ **Things you can check now**
- Look closely for **fleas or flea dirt**
- See if the skin is **red, scabby, or flaky**
- Watch whether the cat is **licking the area repeatedly**
- Check if it’s **both legs or just one**

---

⚠️ **When to see a vet**
- Bald area is getting bigger  
- Skin is red, sore, or crusty  
- Cat seems itchy or uncomfortable  
- Hair loss lasts **more than ~1–2 weeks**

Hair loss itself isn’t usually dangerous, but the **underlying cause needs identifying**.
