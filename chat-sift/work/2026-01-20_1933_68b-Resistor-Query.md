What resistor is 68b

[M2] AI (2026-01-20 19:33):
Short answer: **it depends on the marking system**—and this is one of those delightful little electronics booby-traps.

Most commonly, **“68B” on a small SMD resistor means 680 Ω**.

Here’s why.

In many **SMD resistor codes**, the format is:
- two digits = significant figures  
- a letter = multiplier  

Typical multipliers:
- **A = ×1**
- **B = ×10**
- **C = ×100**
- **D = ×1 000**

So:

**68B → 68 × 10 = 680 Ω**

That’s the interpretation you’ll see on a lot of chip resistors, especially older or industrial marking schemes.

---

There *is* another, rarer possibility:

In some contexts (mainly precision or documentation, not body markings), the letter **B** can refer to **tolerance**, historically meaning **±0.1%**. In that case, “68B” would mean **68 Ω ±0.1%**.  
But this is **very uncommon on the resistor body itself** and much more likely in a BOM or parts list.

---

**Practical engineer’s rule of thumb**  
If this is a **physical SMD resistor marking**, assume **680 Ω** unless proven otherwise. If it’s from a **datasheet or BOM**, check the tolerance column.

If you want to be absolutely ruthless about truth (and physics approves): stick a meter across it and let reality settle the argument.
