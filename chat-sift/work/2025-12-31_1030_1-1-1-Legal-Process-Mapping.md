## Layer 1 — End-to-End Legal Lifecycle  
**Driving / Being in Charge Under the Influence of Drugs (England & Wales)**

This lifecycle runs from the **initial event** through to the point where the matter is **legally closed and the penalty fully spent**.

---

## 1. Pre-Incident Context (Non-Legal but Foundational)

**Status:** No criminal process yet  
**Importance:** Context only; later used for mitigation or diversion

Typical elements:
- Mental-health state (acute crisis, psychosis, exhaustion)
- Medication / substance history
- Sleep, nutrition, hydration
- Location context (public vs restricted land)
- Behaviour before any police involvement

This material has **no immediate legal force**, but later becomes critical evidence.

---

## 2. The Event Itself

**Trigger:**  
- Vehicle movement **or**
- Being found “in charge” of a vehicle

**Key legal questions formed immediately (even if no one says so):**
- Was the person *driving* or merely *in charge*?
- Was the location a *road or public place*?
- Was there observable *impairment*?
- Was there *risk to the public*?

At this point:
- No charge exists
- No offence is yet proven
- Facts are still fluid

---

## 3. Observation and Report

**Who may observe:**
- CCTV operator
- Member of the public
- Police patrol

**What is logged:**
- Behaviour (e.g., unsteady on feet)
- Vehicle position and movement
- Time stamps
- Location description

This stage is crucial because **early interpretation errors** often persist through the case.

---

## 4. Police Engagement

**Legal status:** Investigatory, not accusatory (initially)

Possible actions:
- Welfare check
- Breath test (alcohol)
- Roadside drug swab
- Field Impairment Test (FIT)

**Critical distinction:**
- Failure or concern ≠ guilt  
- Roadside tests are **screening tools**, not proof

Mental-health vulnerability *should* be identified here, but often is not.

---

## 5. Detention or Voluntary Attendance

Two routes:

### A. Arrest
- Used if police believe offence suspected and necessary

### B. Voluntary Interview
- You attend by agreement
- Still under caution
- Still evidence-gathering

**Legal status:**  
You are now **under investigation**, but **not charged**.

---

## 6. Sampling (Blood / Urine)

**Purpose:** Establish presence and level of drugs

Key characteristics:
- Samples often taken **hours after** any alleged driving
- Results do **not** prove impairment at the time of driving by themselves
- Chain of custody and timing become legally critical

This stage often creates **apparent seriousness** even when the evidential link is weak.

---

## 7. Interview Under Caution

**Function:**  
- Record your account
- Lock in explanations
- Capture inconsistencies (from police perspective)

**Important:**  
- This is *not* a trial
- Silence or confusion is not evidence of guilt
- Written statements later can clarify and contextualise

Your attached interview summaries sit squarely here fileciteturn0file1.

---

## 8. Release Under Investigation (RUI)

**Status:**  
- No charge
- No bail conditions
- Investigation ongoing

This period can last **months**.

Police now:
- Await toxicology
- Review CCTV
- Assess legal thresholds
- Refer file internally

---

## 9. Charging Decision

**Decision-maker:**  
- Police (minor cases) **or**
- Crown Prosecution Service (CPS)

They apply the **Full Code Test**:
1. **Evidential Test** — Is conviction more likely than not?
2. **Public Interest Test** — Should this be prosecuted?

At this point, the case can:
- Be **No Further Action (NFA)**
- Be **charged**
- Be **downgraded** (e.g., driving → in charge)

---

## 10. Charge (If It Occurs)

If charged, you receive:
- Charge sheet
- Court date
- Allegation wording (precise wording matters greatly)

Still:
- No conviction
- No guilt established

---

## 11. Magistrates’ Court Process

**Stages:**
1. First appearance
2. Plea entered
3. Case management
4. Evidence disclosure
5. Hearing or sentencing

Most drug-driving cases remain in Magistrates’ Court.

---

## 12. Outcome Paths

### A. Acquittal / Dismissal
- Case ends
- No conviction
- No penalty

### B. Conviction
- Fine and/or
- Points or disqualification
- Costs and surcharge

The sentence becomes **live**.

---

## 13. Penalty Period (“Spent” Phase)

This includes:
- Disqualification duration
- Points validity
- Fine payment
- Rehabilitation courses (if applicable)

Only **after this completes** is the penalty “spent”.

---

## 14. Legal Closure

**When the matter is fully closed:**
- No active penalty
- No further court obligations
- Rehabilitation of Offenders timelines apply
- Case becomes historical, not live

At this point, the incident transitions from **legal risk** to **personal record**.

## 1. What the Duty Solicitor Actually Is (and Is Not)

A **duty solicitor** is:

- A **fully qualified criminal defence solicitor**
- Independent of the police and the court
- Funded by **legal aid for that hearing**
- There to ensure **fair process**, not just to “stand next to you”

A duty solicitor is **not**:
- Less qualified
- A “junior” lawyer
- Someone who only speaks if things go wrong

The difference between duty and private is **continuity and preparation time**, not competence.

---

## 2. How a Duty Solicitor Works in Court (Step by Step)

If your case goes to Magistrates’ Court and you use the duty solicitor:

### Before Court (Same Day)
- You arrive early
- You ask for the **duty solicitor**
- They will:
  - Read the charge sheet
  - Review CPS summary
  - Ask you key questions
  - Identify vulnerabilities (mental health, neurodivergence)
  - Confirm plea position

This usually takes **20–40 minutes**, sometimes longer if the list is light.

---

### In the Courtroom
The duty solicitor will:

- Stand with you
- Speak **on your behalf**
- Enter pleas
- Request adjournments if appropriate
- Make submissions on:
  - bail
  - sentencing reports
  - mitigation scope
  - suitability for alternatives (e.g. reports, deferrals)

They **do not** rush you into pleading guilty unless *you* choose that route.

---

### If You Plead Not Guilty
- The duty solicitor can:
  - Enter the plea
  - Secure disclosure
  - Get directions
- They usually **cannot stay on for trial** unless legal aid is granted later

At that point, you decide:
- continue self-prep
- apply for legal aid
- instruct privately later

---

### If You Plead Guilty (or Indicate Guilty)
The duty solicitor:
- Delivers **oral mitigation**
- Flags mental health
- Requests reports
- Argues proportionality
- Protects you from procedural mistakes

This is *very* valuable, even if you prepared your own pack.
