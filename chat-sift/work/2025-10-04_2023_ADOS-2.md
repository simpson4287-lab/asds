### **Purpose and Overview**

The ADOS-2 (Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule, Second Edition) is a **standardised tool** used to observe and assess social and communication behaviours that are central to the diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder (ASD).  
It provides a **semi-structured environment** in which an examiner interacts directly with the individual being assessed.  

The goal is to create situations that **naturally prompt communication, play, imagination, and social responses** — behaviours that help identify autism-related differences.  

ADOS-2 expands upon the first ADOS by:
- Refining its structure and scoring algorithms.  
- Improving diagnostic accuracy for **both children and adults**, and across **different levels of spoken language**.  
- Adding a **Toddler Module (Module T)** for very young children.  

It’s designed for use by **qualified professionals** — psychologists, psychiatrists, or clinicians trained in autism assessment.

---

### **Structure of the ADOS-2**

The manual explains that ADOS-2 consists of **five modules**, each tailored to an individual’s **expressive language level and age**:

| Module | Intended for | Typical communication level |
|---------|---------------|-----------------------------|
| Toddler (T) | 12–30 months | Preverbal or using a few words |
| Module 1 | Children (31+ months) | Few or no words |
| Module 2 | Children | Simple phrases |
| Module 3 | Children/adolescents | Fluent speech |
| Module 4 | Adolescents/adults | Fluent speech |

Each module contains a **set of activities and “presses”** — situations that elicit social and communicative responses.  
Examples include play tasks, conversation prompts, story telling, and demonstrations.

The examiner observes and codes specific **behavioural indicators**, which are later scored using algorithms that reflect diagnostic domains such as:
- **Social affect** (communication and reciprocal interaction)  
- **Restricted and repetitive behaviours**  

The results are then used to support a clinical diagnosis in combination with other information (e.g., developmental history, caregiver interviews).

---

### **Materials**

The manual lists specific materials used in each module (e.g., toys, books, objects). These are chosen to be **familiar, interesting, and socially engaging**, allowing spontaneous behaviour to emerge.  
While each module has its own set, the materials broadly serve to:
- Encourage **social interaction** (e.g., shared play, turn-taking).  
- Support **imaginative or symbolic play**.  
- Provide opportunities for **joint attention** and communication.  

Materials must be presented **in the same way every time**, to preserve standardisation across assessors and participants.

---

### **Instrument Description**

The ADOS-2 is both a **direct observation tool and a coding system**. It is not a questionnaire and does not rely on self-report; instead, it depends on **behaviour observed in real time**.  

Each task or activity provides the examiner with an opportunity to observe specific behaviours — such as **eye contact, gestures, shared enjoyment, imitation, empathy, and language use**.  

Responses are coded using **a 0–3 scale**, reflecting the degree to which each behaviour is typical, limited, or absent.  
These codes are then used to generate scores that correspond to **diagnostic algorithms**.

The manual stresses that the ADOS-2 should not be used in isolation. It must be integrated with:
- Developmental and medical history,  
- Cognitive and language assessments,  
- Information from caregivers and teachers.  

This ensures that the diagnosis reflects the full context of the individual’s development.

---

### **Principles of Use**

ADOS-2 is **semi-structured**, meaning the examiner follows a set framework but adapts naturally to the participant’s responses.  
This balance ensures consistency across examiners while still allowing for flexibility and rapport.

Key principles include:
1. **Creating a natural social context.**  
   The examiner aims to make the session engaging, using playful or conversational cues rather than rigid questioning.
2. **Consistency of administration.**  
   Standardised procedures are essential for comparing results between sessions or assessors.
3. **Active participation by the examiner.**  
   The professional interacts as part of the task, rather than observing silently. This encourages authentic communication attempts.
4. **Systematic observation and note-taking.**  
   Detailed notes about spontaneous behaviours are crucial for accurate coding.
5. **Objective scoring.**  
   Codes are based only on observable behaviour during the session, not on general impressions or reports from others.

---

### **Updates for ADOS-2 Users**

The manual describes improvements made in the second edition:
- Revised **algorithms** to better distinguish autism from non-autism developmental conditions.  
- Added **“comparison scores”** that allow progress to be tracked over time.  
- Expanded **normative data** and improved reliability across modules.  
- Inclusion of the **Toddler Module** for early detection.  
- Clarified and refined **coding instructions**.  

ADOS-2 maintains the structure of the original ADOS but increases **diagnostic precision and flexibility** for a wider age and language range.

- Direct observation of spontaneous speech.

1. **Objectivity:** Use direct evidence, not assumptions.  
2. **Consistency:** Follow manual definitions precisely to ensure inter-rater reliability.  
3. **Context awareness:** Code according to how behaviour appears within the task, not compared to the examiner’s personal expectations.  
4. **Clarifying notes:** Brief written examples beside each score aid future review.  

Examiners must avoid “lenient” or “strict” biases by adhering to the criteria exactly.
