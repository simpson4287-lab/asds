## ⚖️ 1. The Legal Test: “Public Place” under Road Traffic Law

For offences such as **driving while unfit through drink or drugs** (Road Traffic Act 1988 s.4 or s.5A), the prosecution must prove **beyond reasonable doubt** that the driving occurred:  

> “on a road **or other place to which the public have or are permitted to have access**.”  

The phrase “public place” has been interpreted in case law — not by ownership, but by **actual public access** *at the time of the alleged driving*.

---

## ⚖️ 2. Key Case Law Principles

| Case | Principle Established |
|------|-----------------------|
| **DPP v Vivier (1991)** | A “public place” is anywhere where the public actually go, whether or not they have a legal right to do so. |
| **Laurie v DPP [1989] Crim L R 108** | Even private land can be a public place if the public habitually use it without challenge (e.g., a supermarket car park). |
| **DPP v MacDonagh [1974] R.T.R. 382** | A gated car park closed to the public at the time of the offence was *not* a public place. Once access was withdrawn, it ceased to be “public.” |
| **Director of Public Prosecutions v Owen [1992] Crim L R 924** | The test is “whether the public had access, not whether they had permission in law.” However, when access is *physically or clearly restricted*, it’s *not* public. |
