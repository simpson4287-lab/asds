[M2] AI (2026-05-23 10:47):
With concrete screws, the drill size is normally based on the **shaft/body diameter**, *not* the outer thread diameter.

That one looks very much like a typical **M8-ish concrete screw / masonry screw**, probably requiring a **6 mm drill bit** — but the easiest reliable check is this:

- Measure the **solid core** between the threads.
- The drill bit is usually about the same size as that core.

From your photo:
- Outer thread looks roughly ~8 mm
- Core looks roughly ~6 mm
- Therefore likely:
  - **6 mm masonry bit**
  - Usually a **10 mm spanner/socket** on the hex head

Typical mappings are:

| Concrete screw size | Pilot drill |
|---|---|
| 7.5 mm | 6 mm |
| 8 mm | 6 mm |
| 10 mm | 8 mm |

A few practical tips:
- Use a **hammer drill + masonry bit**
- Drill slightly deeper than screw length (about +10 mm)
- Blow/vacuum dust out of hole — concrete screws grip much better when clean
- Don’t overtighten; they can strip the concrete surprisingly easily

Looking at yours specifically, I’d start with a **6 mm SDS/masonry bit**.

[M6] AI (2026-05-23 10:50):
Yes — and in your case it’s actually a sensible approach.

You effectively want the screw to:
- pass freely through the 45 mm timber
- then bite into the concrete underneath

That’s exactly what a clearance hole / partial counterbore achieves.

With:
- 80 mm screw
- 45 mm timber

…you only have ~35 mm left into the concrete, and some of that disappears if the threads grip the timber before fully seating.

A good method would be:

1. Drill the concrete pilot first
   - likely 6 or 8 mm depending on actual screw type

2. In the timber:
   - drill a **clearance hole** roughly equal to the screw outer diameter
   - only through the timber thickness

3. Optionally counterbore the top:
   - maybe 10–15 mm deep
   - wide enough for socket/washer/head

This gives you:
- more effective embedment into concrete
- proper clamping force
- easier tightening
- less chance of the timber hanging up before pulling tight

However — with only 80 mm total length and 45 mm timber:
- your concrete engagement may end up marginal depending on counterbore depth.

Example:
- 45 mm timber
- 10 mm counterbore
- leaves only ~25 mm into concrete

That’s getting shallow for a large concrete screw.

Honestly, if possible, I’d be tempted to:
- move up to **100 mm or 120 mm screws**
- especially if this is structural or outdoors.

Rule of thumb:
- aim for at least **40–50 mm embedment into solid concrete** for decent holding.
