When sunglass or tinted prescription lenses are described by a **percentage tint**, they’re usually referring to how much **light is blocked** (or conversely how much light is *transmitted*) through the lens. The higher the percentage of tint, the darker the lens and the less light that gets through. citeturn0search16

Here’s how common tint densities are typically categorised (European/ISO standard): citeturn0search3

| **Category** | **Approx Visible Light Transmission (VLT)** | **Description** |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Cat 0 | 80–100% | Very light tint (almost clear) |
| Cat 1 | 43–80% | Light tint |
| Cat 2 | 18–43% | Medium tint |
| Cat 3 | 8–18% | Dark tint (standard sunglasses) |
| Cat 4 | 3–8% | Very dark (specialist, not for driving) |

If your current lenses are labelled as **35% grey tint**, that’s commonly interpreted as letting through ~35% of visible light (so blocking ~65%), placing them in the **medium-to-dark range** (roughly Cat 2/3). citeturn0search3turn0search16

### Next Shade Down (Lighter)
The next lighter (less dark) tint you would typically see below **35%** would likely be around:

- **25% tint** — allows more light in (around 25% blockage more than 35%), meaning they feel noticeably lighter in brightness compared with 35%. citeturn0search6

Below that, you would find:

- **20% tint** — significantly lighter; often chosen if you want protection from glare but still good visibility in less intense light. citeturn0search6

### Summary
- **35% tint** = darkish, standard sunglass level for bright sunlight (often in the Cat 2/3 range).  
- **25% tint** = slightly lighter/dimmer tint than your current one — a common next step down.  
- **20% tint** = lighter still, suitable if 35% feels too dark in typical conditions you encounter. citeturn0search3turn0search6

If you want something noticeably lighter but still protective in daylight, **25%** is a logical next shade down; **20%** is even lighter.
