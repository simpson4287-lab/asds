## **B5 – Investigation & Interview**

**Purpose**  
To capture how the case was investigated and constructed by the police, strictly from a procedural and evidential perspective. This layer explains *process*, not opinion.

**Scope and Content**

This layer may include:

- **Interview Process**
  - Date, location, and type of interview (e.g. voluntary interview under caution)
  - Attendees (officers, duty solicitor)
  - Interview format (audio/video recorded)
  - Duration and breaks

- **Materials Shown or Referenced**
  - CCTV footage shown during interview
  - Images, documents, or statements referenced by officers
  - What was disclosed verbally vs physically provided
  - Any materials *not* shown but later referenced

- **Police Lines of Enquiry**
  - What the officers stated they were investigating
  - How the incident was framed during questioning
  - Any stated assumptions or hypotheses put to you
  - Clarification requests made by you or your solicitor

- **Requests and Follow-ups**
  - Requests made by police during or after interview
  - Requests made by you (e.g. access to recordings, disclosure)
  - Outcomes of those requests (granted, refused, pending)

- **Investigation Steps**
  - Sequence of investigative actions as known
  - How evidence appears to have been gathered or relied upon
  - Any notable gaps, delays, or procedural features (factual only)

**Explicit Exclusions**

This layer must **not** include:
- Personal recollections or emotional responses
- Legal argument or defence strategy
- Mental health context
- Opinions on fairness, accuracy, or conduct

Those belong elsewhere:
- Personal recollection → **Project A**
- Mental health → **B4**
- Legal analysis or challenge → **B6 or Defence layer**

**Tone and Rules**
- Factual, neutral, chronological
- No speculation
- No advocacy
- Written as if it could be read by a third party unfamiliar with the case
