[M3] AI (2026-06-13 17:51):
For **Tyvek Supro / Supro Plus**, join sheets by **lapping first, then taping if you need a sealed joint**.

**Normal roof laps:**

- **Horizontal laps:** minimum **150 mm** overlap.
- **Vertical/end laps:** minimum **300 mm**, ideally positioned **over a rafter**.
- At the **ridge:** carry it over and lap at least **150 mm down each side**, giving a **300 mm double layer** over the ridge.
- At eaves/abutments, keep the lap arranged so water always sheds **over** the lower sheet, not behind it.

DuPont’s roof installation sheet gives **150 mm horizontal laps** and **300 mm vertical laps over rafters** for Tyvek Supro. citeturn519987search1

**To actually bond/join the sheet:**

Use one of these:

1. **Tyvek Double-sided Tape** between the two overlapped sheets — best for hidden sealed laps.
2. **Tyvek Tape 2060B single-sided tape** over the face of the joint — useful for cuts, repairs, penetrations, or where you can’t get double-sided tape between layers.

DuPont’s technical manual says Tyvek underlay laps and perimeters can be sealed using **Tyvek Tape 2060B** or **Tyvek Double-sided Tape**. citeturn519987search6

**Practical method:**

Lay the lower sheet first, then overlap the next sheet above it by 150 mm. Make sure the membrane is dry/clean, no dust, no wet treatment, no sawdust. Apply the tape smoothly with pressure; don’t leave wrinkles or fish-mouths. Then batten over it so the joint is mechanically held, not just relying on adhesive.

If you’ve got **Supro Plus**, it may have an **integral adhesive tape strip**, so you peel and press the overlap rather than adding separate tape. citeturn519987search4

For your cabin/wall use rather than a pitched roof, I’d still use the same logic: **150 mm laps minimum, tape the laps if you want windtightness, and keep the upper layer lapping over the lower layer like roof tiles.**

Yes — **some Tyvek Supro products do**, but the distinction matters:

**Tyvek Supro**: usually **does not have built-in self-adhesive lap strips**. You overlap it and use separate Tyvek tape if you want sealed laps.

**Tyvek Supro Plus**: commonly **does have integrated self-adhesive tape strips** for sealing overlaps.

So if your roll says **“Tyvek Supro Plus”**, you should find adhesive strips along the sheet edges, normally protected by peel-off backing. If it just says **“Tyvek Supro”**, it may simply be the membrane without self-adhesion.

Look for:
- a peel-off backing strip along one/both long edges;
- wording like **“Plus”**, **“integrated tape”**, **“self-adhesive lap”**, or **“taped version”** on the roll label;
- a slightly different edge band where the adhesive is.

For fitting, even with the self-adhesive version, still do the proper lap: typically **150 mm horizontal lap**, then peel, press, and roll/firmly rub the adhesive strip down. It needs to be **dry, clean, and not dusty** to bond properly.

[M7] AI (2026-06-13 17:53):
I wouldn’t use **Gorilla duct tape** as the proper joint tape for Tyvek, especially if the membrane is part of your weather/air-tight layer.

It will probably **stick initially**, but the issues are:

- **Adhesive compatibility:** duct tape glue is not designed for breather membranes.
- **Ageing:** it can dry out, creep, or peel over time.
- **Temperature/moisture:** roof/wall cavities get hot, cold, damp, and dusty.
- **No warranty/system approval:** it is not the tested Tyvek jointing method.

For a proper job use:

**Best:** Tyvek single-sided tape / Tyvek acrylic tape  
**Also good:** Tyvek double-sided tape for laps  
**Acceptable alternatives:** good-quality membrane tapes such as SIGA, Pro Clima, Tescon, Illbruck, Gerband, etc.

For a **temporary hold while fitting**, Gorilla duct tape is fine. For the actual sealed lap, I’d remove it or leave it only as temporary assistance and then use the correct membrane tape.

If this is on your **cabin wall membrane**, where you mainly want windtightness and weather shedding, I’d still use proper membrane tape. It is one of those small-cost things that avoids a hidden failure later.

[M9] AI (2026-06-13 17:53):
If it’s **sandwiched/clamped between timber**, then Gorilla duct tape becomes much less risky — but I’d still separate two jobs:

**1. Weather-shedding lap:**  
The important thing is the membrane overlaps correctly, like tiles. If water ever gets behind the cladding/logs, it should run **down the outside face** of the lower sheet, not into the joint. For that, the timber sandwich helps hold the lap in place.

**2. Airtight/windtight seal:**  
This is where duct tape is weaker. If you want the membrane to act as a proper windtight layer, use real membrane tape. Duct tape may not be a long-term adhesive seal, even if clamped.

So the practical answer:

**Yes, you can probably use Gorilla duct tape as a positioning/extra-help tape if the joint is mechanically clamped between timber.**  
But **don’t rely on it as the actual long-term seal** unless failure would not really matter.

For your cabin, I’d be comfortable with:

- **150 mm overlap minimum**
- overlap facing the right direction for water run-off
- timber battens/studs clamping the lap
- Gorilla tape only to hold things while assembling

I’d be less comfortable if:

- it is the only thing sealing a butt joint
- the lap is small
- the joint could flap in wind
- water can sit on the tape edge
- it is on the cold/exposed side where condensation or rain tracking could reach it

Best cheap compromise: use the Gorilla tape temporarily, then put **proper breather membrane tape** on the main laps/corners/tears/penetrations. You don’t need to tape absolutely every square inch, but the vulnerable joints are worth doing properly.
