Date: 20 Jan 2026  
Context: Upcoming appraisal; tension between disclosure and optionality.

Summary:  
There are effectively two parallel appraisal systems in play. The formal appraisal is an organisational artefact designed to assess competence, delivery, and forward-looking development. It rewards clarity, restraint, and outcome-focused narrative. It does not reward emotional honesty, retrospective attribution of fault, or detailed context about organisational failures.  

In parallel, a private appraisal exists as a personal system of record. This contains detailed notes, timelines, evidence, reflections, and health context. Its purpose is narrative integrity, self-validation, and future optionality (HR escalation, legal advice), not immediate disclosure.

Risk Assessment:  
The primary risk is cross-coupling these systems—allowing personal evidence or emotive context to leak into the formal appraisal unnecessarily. This creates exposure without benefit and may foreclose options rather than protect them.

Positioning Decision:  
No pre-emptive defence is required. The optimal stance is competence-forward, calm, and bounded. Evidence is retained privately and only deployed if the organisation introduces challenge, misrepresentation, or adverse action.

Operating Principle:  
Silence is not passivity here; it is containment. Optionality is preserved by keeping the formal record clean and the personal record complete.

Log established. Evidence retained. Formal narrative constrained by design.
