[M8] AI (2026-06-15 14:00):
No — **your dad being fully comprehensive on the car does not mean anyone can drive it third-party**.

There are two separate ideas that often get mixed up:

**1. Your dad’s policy insures the car for named/permitted drivers**  
That usually means **your dad**, plus any **named drivers** listed on the policy. If you are not named, his policy normally does **not** cover you to drive it.

**2. “Driving Other Cars” / DOC cover**  
This is the clause where *a person’s own policy* may let them drive someone else’s car third-party. It applies to the **driver’s own insurance**, not the owner’s. The ABI says DOC cover is where your own policy may cover you to drive another person’s vehicle, usually third-party only. citeturn493686search2 AXA also says DOC only applies if your certificate specifically says the policyholder has that cover, and the other car usually needs its own insurance. citeturn493686search1

So for **you** to drive your dad’s car legally, one of these needs to be true:

| Situation | Are you likely covered? |
|---|---|
| You are a named driver on your dad’s policy | Yes, subject to policy terms |
| You have your own policy with DOC cover | Possibly, usually third-party only |
| You take temporary insurance on his car | Yes, if accepted |
| Your dad is fully comp but you are not named and have no DOC | **No** |
| You only have his permission | **No** |

Also, DOC cover is **not automatic with fully comprehensive insurance** anymore. Insurers and comparison sites repeatedly warn that fully comp does not automatically mean you can drive other cars. citeturn493686search5turn493686search8

Given the drug-driving charge/conviction issue, be extra careful. DOC cover is often excluded or restricted for people with convictions, under-25s, people driving a car they own or regularly use, or cars in the same household. Some policies also say DOC is for emergency/occasional use only. The AA specifically warns that regular use of someone else’s car should be handled by being a named driver instead. citeturn493686search7

The safest answer is: **you cannot assume you can drive it.** Check the actual certificate/policy wording. Look for a line like:

> “The policyholder may also drive, with the owner’s permission, a private motor car not owned by or hired to them…”

If that line is on **your own** insurance certificate, you may have DOC cover. If it is only on **your dad’s** certificate, it lets **him** drive other cars — it does not let you drive his.

For you, the clean options are **named driver**, **temporary insurance**, or **your own policy covering that specific car**.
