What does acetone do to cotton

[M6] AI (2025-11-29 14:26):
Acetone **doesn’t dissolve cotton**, but it *does* affect it in some useful (and sometimes surprising) ways.

### 🧪 Why?
Cotton is made of **cellulose**, and cellulose is generally resistant to acetone. Acetone mainly dissolves plastics, synthetic fibres, oils, paints and adhesives—not natural cellulose.

---

# So what actually happens?

## ✔️ Most common effects:
### **1. It removes coatings, dyes, oils and finishes**
Acetone can strip:
- colour/ink
- fabric coatings
- waxy finishes
- glue
- stains

So the cotton itself stays intact, but what’s *on* the cotton may dissolve or fade.

---

### **2. It slightly dries and stiffens the fibres**
Acetone evaporates fast and can temporarily make cotton feel:
- crisp
- dry
- slightly tighter

Once evaporated, the cotton goes back to normal.

---

### **3. It won’t melt cotton**
Unlike polyester, nylon or acrylic, cotton will not get soft or melt because of acetone. You can soak cotton wool balls in acetone and they remain as fibres.

---

# When **damage** can happen
Not from the acetone itself, but from:
- dyes running/bleaching
- stitching or elastics melting (if synthetic)
- fabric shrinking if very hot

If the cotton material has plastic mixed in, the plastic may dissolve—but the cotton won’t.

---

# Quick summary
| Substance | Effect on cotton |
|---|---|
| Acetone on pure cotton | No melting, no dissolving |
| Acetone on dyes/inks | Can fade/remove |
| Acetone on coatings | Removes finishes/glues |
| Acetone on synthetic blends | May dissolve the synthetic part |

---

# Bonus fact
Acetone **does dissolve nail varnish, polyester, superglue, and some rubbers**, but **not cellulose fibres**—which is why acetone is often used to clean cotton wipes in labs.
