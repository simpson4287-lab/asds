[M2] AI (2025-12-09 10:05):
When parents are negative, unsupportive, and the relationship is strained, it can feel isolating and exhausting — especially if you’ve been trying but communication consistently breaks down. There isn’t a single solution that fixes everything, but there *are* approaches that protect your wellbeing, reduce conflict, and help you navigate the situation without relying on support that isn’t there.

Here’s a structured way to think about it:

---

### **1. Protect Your Mental and Emotional Boundaries**
You can’t control how they behave, but you *can* control how much power it has over you.

- Limit conversations that turn hostile or critical  
- Decide what topics are safe, and what topics you no longer engage in  
- Use short, neutral responses when negativity starts  
- Prioritise self-respect over approval  

> You don’t need to absorb every opinion just because it’s from family.

---

### **2. Shift from Seeking Validation to Self-Validation**
It’s painful when parents don’t recognise your efforts or struggles — but basing your self-worth on their approval keeps the wound open.

A healthier internal replacement might look like:

- *“I’m proud of myself even if they don’t say it.”*  
- *“I know my reality — I don’t need them to understand it to make it valid.”*  

Recognition from others is powerful, but self-recognition is liberating.

---

### **3. Communicate Only Where It’s Productive**
If conversations always lead to conflict, you can step back without cutting off completely.

Useful approaches:

- Keep exchanges factual and neutral, not emotional  
- Share information only when necessary  
- Avoid expecting them to respond in the way you wish they would  
- Use writing instead of talking if verbal discussion becomes heated  

Sometimes **reduced contact = more peace**.

---

### **4. Rely on Alternative Support Systems**
If you can’t lean on them, lean sideways.

Support can come from elsewhere:

- friends  
- partners  
- community / groups  
- therapy  
- online spaces  
- mentors  
- people who *choose* to understand you  

Sometimes the family you build is more supportive than the family you’re born into.

---

### **5. Accept That They Might Not Change**
Not in a pessimistic way — in a freeing way.

When you stop trying to win approval from people who aren’t able/willing to provide it, your energy becomes available for things that *will* nurture you.

Acceptance doesn’t mean liking it.
It means you stop fighting what you cannot control.

---

### **6. Maintain Self-Compassion**
Growing without support is harder, but many people build strong, resilient identities from exactly this environment.

You’re not weak for struggling.
You’re strong for growing anyway.

Some internal affirmations that help in these situations:

- *I can love them and still protect my peace.*  
- *I can care without carrying everything.*  
- *Family can matter, but so do I.*
