I’ve membranes a subbase for cabin but realised when I started that the kiln sand was wet as had been left out. I continued finishing at 10pm. I plan to cover it with dry around 7am when stick tacky. This be ok?

[M2] AI (2026-06-12 21:05):
It should be **salvageable**, provided the membrane is still tacky at 7am.

Wet kiln-dried sand is not ideal because the moisture can stop it embedding properly into the membrane. In the morning:

- Brush or gently scrape away any obviously loose, clumped or saturated sand.
- Let visible surface water evaporate first.
- Broadcast plenty of genuinely dry kiln-dried sand while the membrane is still tacky.
- Do not press heavily or walk on it; allow the membrane to cure, then sweep/vacuum away anything that has not bonded.

The main concern is **not the damp sand itself**, but whether the membrane has skinned over before the dry sand is applied. If it is still sticky enough for dry grains to remain attached when lightly sprinkled, you should get the key you need. If it has become completely hard, another thin compatible coat may be needed before applying dry sand.

Also keep it covered from rain overnight, but avoid laying plastic directly onto the wet membrane.

[M4] AI (2026-06-12 21:07):
That actually makes it **more likely it will still be tacky at 7am**, so adding dry kiln-dried sand then should work.

Because it went on thick:

- Do not add more membrane unless parts are completely cured and smooth.
- Remove any visibly wet clumps of the first sand where practical.
- Broadcast dry sand generously over every shiny/tacky area.
- Leave it undisturbed for a full cure; a thick coat may remain soft underneath longer than expected.
- Before building over it, check that it is firm and does not move, wrinkle or leave black material on your footwear.

The main risk from applying it thick is **extended curing**, rather than failure. Avoid trapping it under the next layer merely because the surface feels dry—the underside can still be soft. Give it at least the manufacturer’s stated full curing time, and longer in cool or damp weather.

[M6] AI (2026-06-12 21:08):
Pools are the part to deal with. A thick final coat is usually acceptable, but **deep puddles can skin over while remaining soft underneath**, then wrinkle or squeeze when the cabin load goes on.

At 7am:

- Check the pooled areas with a disposable stick, not your fingers.
- If they are still liquid or very soft, spread them out with the broom or a squeegee into thinner surrounding areas.
- If they have already skinned, do not break the skin and leave a crater. Let them cure fully.
- Apply dry kiln-dried sand generously to all tacky areas, especially the pools, but do not expect sand alone to cure a deep puddle.
- Delay covering or loading it until the deepest areas are firm throughout and no longer deform under moderate thumb pressure through a piece of plastic.

A few shallow glossy patches are unlikely to matter. Pools several millimetres deep may need an extra day or more, particularly overnight in cool, damp conditions. Since this is the final layer, the safest approach is to **flatten any still-mobile pools in the morning, sand the surface, and give it longer before placing the cabin base on it**.

[M8] AI (2026-06-12 21:11):
Yes—**a roller is probably better than the broom for flattening shallow pools**, provided the membrane is still wet and mobile.

Use it lightly:

- Use a clean roller that is compatible with the membrane.
- Roll slowly, without pressing hard.
- Work the excess outward into thinner areas rather than repeatedly rolling the whole surface.
- Stop if the coating starts lifting, stringing, wrinkling or pulling away.
- Then broadcast the dry kiln-dried sand while it is still tacky.

Do not roll over a pool that has already formed a skin. That can trap soft material underneath or tear the surface. In that case, leave it to cure rather than disturbing it.

The objective is not a perfectly smooth finish—just to eliminate obvious deep puddles and leave a reasonably even continuous membrane.
