## How the entity["company","Facebook","meta social network platform"] algorithm actually works

Facebook does **not** rely on a single signal (such as phone numbers or mutual friends). It operates a **multi-signal likelihood model** designed to answer one core question:

> *“How likely is it that these two accounts are socially connected, even if they are not currently connected?”*

When the probability crosses a threshold, the person may surface as a suggestion or appear more prominently.

---

## Signals that matter (even years later)

### 1. **Historical graph data (very important)**
Even if:
- You deleted her number years ago  
- You are not Facebook friends  
- You share no visible mutual friends  

The system **does not forget past proximity**. Signals such as:
- Past contact syncing (even once, long ago)
- Past message threads
- Being viewed as part of the same “social cluster” historically  

remain **low-weight but persistent features** in the model.

These are *decayed*, not erased.

---

### 2. **Second-order network overlap**
You may not share *direct* friends, but:
- You may share friends-of-friends
- You may share people who interact with the same accounts
- You may exist in overlapping geographic or demographic cohorts  

Facebook models this as *latent social distance*, not visible friend links.

---

### 3. **Location and routine coincidence**
If both of you:
- Live in the same town again
- Visit overlapping locations
- Have recently re-entered similar life phases (age band, relationship status, activity patterns)

The model boosts likelihood **without any explicit interaction**.

---

### 4. **Cross-platform or device-level inference**
Even without sharing numbers:
- Past address book syncing
- Device co-presence (same Wi-Fi historically)
- Shared login environments (workplace, university, events)

These can persist as anonymised graph edges.

Importantly:  
This does **not** require current tracking — only that the relationship once existed.

---

### 5. **Engagement-seeking resurfacing**
Facebook deliberately resurfaces:
- Dormant but emotionally salient ties
- People you once interacted with but never “resolved” socially  

Why? Because they statistically increase:
- Profile clicks
- Time on platform
- Emotional engagement

This is not personal — it is optimisation.

---

## What it is *not*

To be explicit, this is **not**:
- Mind reading
- Accessing your private thoughts
- Someone searching you up recently (that’s a common myth)
- Her number being re-added secretly

It is **pattern completion** over a massive social graph.

---

## Why it feels unsettling

Humans experience this as eerie because:
- The tie is emotionally meaningful
- The timing feels subjectively significant
- The system offers no explanation  

But from the algorithm’s perspective, it is simply resurfacing a **high-confidence dormant edge**.

---

## Bottom line

This happens because:
- You once occupied a close position in each other’s social graph
- That relationship never fully collapsed to zero probability
- Recent contextual signals made the edge “worth testing” again

It says **nothing** about intent, fate, or renewed interest — only that the model believes a reconnection would be statistically plausible.

## If she has your number, what happens

When **her** phone contacts are synced to entity["company","Facebook","meta social network platform"] (which is the default for many users):

1. Your phone number is uploaded (hashed, not stored in plain text)
2. Facebook matches that hash to your account
3. A **one-way edge** is created in the social graph:
   > *“This person knows this account holder.”*

You do **not** need to reciprocate.

From that point:
- You may appear in **People You May Know** to her
- She may appear to you as a **suggested person**, profile preview, or resurfaced presence
- This can happen **years later**, even if the number was saved long ago

The edge decays slowly, but it does not drop to zero unless explicitly broken.

---

## Why you might see her (even without mutuals)

Facebook does not require symmetry. The logic is:

> *“If Person A has Person B’s number, there is a non-trivial chance of a meaningful social relationship.”*

That is enough.

If *any* of the following are also true, the probability spikes:
- You were once geographically co-located
- You interacted in the past (messages, views, reactions)
- You fall into similar demographic or life-stage cohorts
- Either of you recently became more active on the platform

At that point, the system “tests” the connection by surfacing it.

---

## Why it can happen suddenly

These suggestions are **event-triggered**, not continuous. Common triggers include:
- One of you re-syncs contacts (new phone, OS update)
- One of you becomes more active after inactivity
- Location patterns realign (same city again)
- Relationship status or usage pattern changes

That is why it can feel abrupt or uncanny.

---

## What this does *not* mean

It does **not** imply:
- She recently searched for you
- She recently added your number
- She is intentionally trying to reconnect
- Facebook is reading messages or thoughts

It is a **passive inference**, not an action.

- Disable contact syncing in Facebook settings
- Remove your phone number from Facebook entirely
- Clear “People You May Know” data periodically

These actions reduce future resurfacing, but they do not instantly erase historical edges.
