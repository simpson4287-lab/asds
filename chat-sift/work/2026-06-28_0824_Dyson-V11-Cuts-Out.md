[M3] AI (2026-06-28 08:24):
For a **Dyson V11 cutting out after some runtime, or cutting out on higher suction/Boost**, the most likely causes are:

## 1. Restricted airflow: blockage or clogged filter

This is the first thing I’d suspect, especially if it **pulses on/off** or cuts out more quickly on Boost. Higher suction demands much more airflow; if the filter, wand, head, cyclone, or bin is restricted, the motor can overheat or the control system detects abnormal load.

Check:

- Main filter: wash it, then let it dry **fully** — ideally 24+ hours.
- Bin/cyclone inlet: fluff, plaster dust, dog hair, fine powder can clog hidden paths.
- Wand: look straight through it.
- Floor head: hair round the brush bar, blocked elbow joint, jammed roller.
- Small tools: try running the vacuum with **no wand/head attached**.

Dyson/common troubleshooting advice consistently points to filter clogging, full bin, brush-bar debris, wand/tool blockage, and battery condition as the main causes of cutting out or poor runtime. citeturn430803news44

## 2. Battery voltage sag under high load

This fits your symptom very well: **works on Eco/Auto but cuts out on Boost**. Boost pulls far more current from the pack. An ageing V11 battery can show “charged” but dip below the allowed voltage when loaded hard, so the BMS shuts it down.

Signs it’s battery-related:

- Runs longer on Eco than Boost, but Boost dies quickly.
- Restarts after a short rest.
- Cuts out more when the battery is below half.
- Fully charged display looks normal, but runtime is much shorter than it used to be.

If the machine runs fine with no restriction but still dies on Boost, battery is high on the list.

## 3. Filter not seated properly or still damp

The V11 is quite sensitive to the rear filter assembly. A slightly damp filter increases restriction and can also risk motor damage. A poorly seated filter can also upset airflow sensing.

Good test: remove and refit the filter firmly, then test with a known clean/dry filter if possible.

## 4. Overheating motor/control board

If it runs for a few minutes then stops, and only comes back after cooling, the motor assembly or PCB may be overheating. This can be caused by a blockage, but if airflow is definitely clear, it may be a failing motor/control module.

Typical pattern:

- Runs normally when cold.
- Cuts out after a predictable period.
- Won’t restart immediately.
- Restarts after cooling down.

## 5. Brush head motor or jam causing overload

The powered head adds load. If it cuts out mainly when using the floor head but not with a crevice tool, suspect:

- Hair/debris round brush bar.
- Stiff brush bearings.
- Damaged head wiring/contact pins.
- Motorised head drawing too much current.

Test it with the wand and head removed. Then add parts back one at a time.

## 6. Dirty battery/body contacts or loose battery

Less common, but worth checking. If the battery connection is poor, high suction causes higher current and any contact resistance produces a voltage drop. Clean contacts gently, check the battery clicks/locks firmly, and look for heat marks or looseness.

## Best diagnostic order

Do this in sequence:

1. **Fully charge it.**
2. Remove wand/head/tools and run the bare body on Eco, Auto, then Boost.
3. If it still cuts out on Boost with the bare body, suspect **battery, filter, motor, or PCB**.
4. Refit the wand only. Test again.
5. Refit the head. Test again.
6. Wash/dry or replace the filter.
7. Empty and inspect the bin/cyclone path.
8. If Eco works but Boost cuts out even after all cleaning, the **battery is probably tired**.

My bet, based on your wording, would be either **restricted airflow/filter blockage** or **battery voltage sag under Boost**. If it pulses, think blockage/filter first. If it simply dies hard on Boost but runs on Eco, think battery.

That’s plausible, but I wouldn’t jump straight to **main motor** until you isolate whether the noise came from the **motor body** or the **floor head brush motor**.

On a Dyson V11 there are basically three “motor/noise” suspects:

## 1. Main digital motor in the body

More likely if the noise happens with **no wand or head fitted**.

Bad signs:

- High-pitched screeching/whining that has got worse.
- Grinding, rattling, scraping, or metallic noise.
- Burning smell or hot plastic smell.
- Cuts out even with a clean filter and no attachments.
- Boost mode makes it cut out almost immediately.
- It only restarts after cooling down.

If that’s the case, yes, the motor assembly or control electronics could be failing.

## 2. Brush-bar motor / floor head issue

Very common, and often mistaken for “the motor.”

More likely if it only makes noise when using the floor head. Causes include:

- Hair wrapped round the brush bar.
- Jammed roller.
- Worn brush bearings.
- Debris in the head.
- Head motor drawing too much current and causing shutdown.

Test: run the V11 handheld body only. Then add wand. Then add the powered head. If the noise/cut-out appears only with the head fitted, the main motor may be fine.

## 3. Blockage/filter making the motor sound strained

A blocked filter or airway can make the motor sound louder, strained, pulsing, or “surging.” Boost then pushes it over the edge and it cuts out.

This can sound like a failing motor, but it’s often airflow restriction.

## Best quick test

Do this:

1. Fully charge it.
2. Remove the floor head and wand.
3. Remove the bin and check the inlet path.
4. Refit a fully dry clean filter.
5. Run the bare handheld unit on Eco, Auto, then Boost.

Interpretation:

- **No noise/cut-out bare body:** problem is likely wand/head/blockage.
- **Noise/cut-out bare body:** likely filter, battery sag, or main motor/control PCB.
- **Grinding/screeching bare body:** main motor is a strong suspect.
- **Cuts out only on Boost but sounds normal:** battery still very possible.
- **Cuts out after warming up:** motor/control board overheating becomes more likely.

I’d tell him: **motor is possible, especially if the noise is from the handheld body itself, but prove it by running it with all attachments removed first.** If it screeches/grinds with the bare body, stop using it because a failing motor can shed debris or overheat.
